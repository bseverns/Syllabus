# Light, Color and Shadow Lab

> **PILOT syllabus.** Start with the shared [K–2 operating model](../K2-PILOT-OPERATING-MODEL.md). [Digital Imaging Lab](../../SECONDARY/digital-imaging-lab/README.md) contributes observation, framing, and consent habits; this does not import its camera or editing requirements.

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners test how light, distance, transparent color, and objects change a wall or table, then make a simple shadow scene.
- **Group / staffing:** 8–12 learners; one lead, with an assistant recommended for a darkened room or high support needs.
- **Final share:** a one-minute shadow story or tabletop gallery with an explanation of one tested change.
- **Community agreement:** *We point lights at the wall or table, never at eyes.*

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core tote:** 6–12 low-heat LED flashlights with checked batteries; 12 white paper screens or large sheets; 24 cardboard silhouette shapes; black paper; 12 small clear/translucent sheets; red, yellow, and blue transparent color film; masking tape; painter's tape; child-safe scissors; markers; cardboard bases; clothespins or binder clips used by adults/with supervision; and 12 table trays.

**Helpful extras:** a pop-up white board/sheet, a dimmable corner, translucent cups, a facilitator camera, and printed image-consent reminder cards for adults.

**Prep:** test room light before the session; tape down a light-test zone; check every flashlight for heat and battery condition; pre-cut silhouette starters; make one near/far shadow demo and one color-film demo.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Find a shadow

**Target:** notice that distance changes shadow size and sharpness.
**Prompt:** *What changes when an object moves closer to the light or wall?*
**Demo:** show one object near/far from the flashlight and wall; learners predict before testing.
**Build/play:** small groups test silhouettes at the screen, then trace or point to one shadow they notice.
**Evidence / parking:** capture `near / far` with a photo, arrows on a sketch, or spoken note.

### Session 2 — Mix a color window

**Target:** layer transparent colors and describe what changes.
**Prompt:** *What do you notice when two color windows overlap?*
**Demo:** place one film, then a second, in front of the same light; avoid presenting a single color-name answer as the goal.
**Build/play:** learners make a small color window or test strips over white paper.
**Evidence / parking:** learners choose a favorite change and identify the two layers or show them.

### Session 3 — Build a shadow scene

**Target:** make a character, place, or object readable in shadow.
**Prompt:** *What could happen in this tiny light world?*
**Demo:** create a foreground/background with two silhouettes and one clear action.
**Build/play:** learners make a tabletop screen or shared scene using silhouettes, tape, and color windows.
**Evidence / parking:** a partner identifies one character/place; builder changes one unclear shape if needed.

### Session 4 — Show the light

**Target:** share a scene and explain one tested effect.
**Prompt:** *What should visitors notice first?*
**Demo:** model `I changed ___, and the shadow/color ___.`
**Build/play:** reinforce the scene, rehearse a 20-second story or gallery explanation, and choose one audience-viewing spot.
**Evidence / share:** host a short gallery walk; capture one learner quote and one whole-scene image only with local consent.

## Safety, access, and failure backup

Use only low-heat LED lights; adults manage battery replacement and clips. No one points lights toward faces or uses a darkened room without a visible path and an adult at the edge. Learners may work in normal room light with a tabletop screen; darkness is not required.

If light conditions fail, make a paper color collage or silhouette scene and use one facilitator flashlight for the share. Observation and tested change still count.

## Assessment and public description

Use the shared four look-fors, plus: **the learner tests a light/color change and names, points to, or shows its effect.**

**Partner description:** A screen-light art and science workshop where young learners explore color, shadows, and storytelling through safe, tactile optical experiments.
