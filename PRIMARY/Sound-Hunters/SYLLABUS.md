# Sound Hunters

> **PILOT syllabus.** Start with the shared [K–2 operating model](../K2-PILOT-OPERATING-MODEL.md). [Exploration Sound Design](../../SECONDARY/ExplorationSoundDesign/README.md) contributes listening, Foley, cue, and reflection moves; this is not a DAW or account-based adaptation.

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners notice sounds, make safe object/body sounds, arrange them into a short sound story, and listen for what changes the meaning.
- **Group / staffing:** 8–12 learners; one lead, with an assistant recommended for large groups or sensory support.
- **Final share:** a 30–60 second live sound story with a simple cue order.
- **Community agreement:** *We listen for the sound that is already here before we make a new one.*

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core tote:** 12 soft rhythm cards; 12 stop/listen cards; 24 paper cups; 12 paper bags with a small handful of beans or rice sealed inside; paper, cardboard, fabric, wooden blocks, plastic containers, spoons used as taps, rubber bands around boxes, painter's tape, markers, a visible volume meter card (`quiet / medium / stop`), and a shared whiteboard or chart paper.

**Helpful extras:** one facilitator recorder and speaker, one tablecloth or rug for quieter testing, and printed picture cards for weather, animals, street, kitchen, park, and journey.

**Prep:** test every object for safe volume and edges; make three sound stations; choose a quiet corner; post the hand signal for stop/listen; make no headphones a requirement.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Sound walk and sound words

**Target:** notice and describe sound without needing to name it perfectly.
**Prompt:** *What sounds are near, far, soft, loud, fast, or slow?*
**Demo:** silent listening for ten seconds, then one body sound and one object sound.
**Build/play:** take a short room sound walk or listen from the circle; learners point, imitate, and sort sounds with picture cards.
**Evidence / parking:** facilitator records three group sound words or gestures on the chart.

### Session 2 — Foley table

**Target:** test an object sound and control its volume.
**Prompt:** *What could this sound become in a story?*
**Demo:** make the same object quiet, medium, then stop; model asking before playing a sound near someone.
**Build/play:** rotate through two or three safe Foley trays; learners choose one sound and one story image.
**Evidence / parking:** each learner/pair places a picture or drawing beside its chosen sound.

### Session 3 — Make a sound story

**Target:** arrange three to five sounds in an order another group can follow.
**Prompt:** *What happens first, next, and last?*
**Demo:** point to three picture cues and perform a short sequence with a clear beginning/end.
**Build/play:** small groups pick a setting card and build a cue strip; facilitator keeps the sequence short and repeats it once.
**Evidence / parking:** group rehearses the cue strip; leader records one place where the order got unclear.

### Session 4 — Listening performance

**Target:** perform and revise a sound story for listeners.
**Prompt:** *What should listeners imagine or feel?*
**Demo:** model audience listening posture and one kind feedback sentence: `I heard ___.`
**Build/play:** rehearse once, perform for another group, then change one cue, volume, or pause.
**Evidence / share:** capture the cue strip, one group quote, and an optional facilitator recording.

## Safety, access, and failure backup

No learner is required to make loud sounds or tolerate a loud performance. Keep volume at conversation level unless the group explicitly agrees to a short performance moment. Do not use balloons, sudden/noisy objects, fragile glass, or objects that cannot be cleaned. Learners may contribute through cue pointing, drawing, conducting, listening, or sound-making.

If the room becomes loud, remove all but one sound tray, reset to body percussion, and rehearse a three-cue sequence only.

## Assessment and public description

Use the shared four look-fors, plus: **the learner listens for timing or volume and changes a sound after feedback.**

**Partner description:** A screen-light sound workshop where young learners turn careful listening and everyday objects into a short group performance.
