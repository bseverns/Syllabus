# Week 9: Prototype 2

## Big idea
Failure is information, and revision makes the idea more real.

## Weekly learning goals
- test, debug, and revise a first prototype
- improve usability and technical reliability
- clarify team roles and short-term build priorities

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus repair supplies, stronger tape and glue options, extra connectors, and test logs.

## Must-have materials this week
- projects, planners, and last week's notes
- repair materials and replacement parts
- test logs or status cards
- a visible triage board showing fix now, fix later, and watch closely

## Nice-to-have materials
- stronger cardboard, velcro, zip ties, or cleaner connectors
- phone or tablet for before/after photos
- color-coded sticky notes for bugs and revisions
- extra containers for removed or broken parts

## Fastest successful build
One major failure point repaired and retested with evidence that the revision helped.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Keep the focus on finding and fixing one important problem. Use adult-led triage and simple test routines so revision feels doable instead of like starting over.

## Grades 5–7 extension
Invite students to address both reliability and user experience, or to compare two revision paths before choosing one. Older students can also separate cosmetic wants from must-fix technical issues.

## If tech fails, do this instead
Run revision on structure, signage, and interaction using cardboard mockups and user testing. Students can still improve clarity, sturdiness, and audience flow even without powered systems.

## Documentation target for the week
Capture one revision story: first version, problem found, change made, and result after retesting.

## This week, success looks like...
- teams work from evidence instead of guessing randomly
- revisions improve reliability or clarity in a visible way
- students can describe what changed between versions

## This week, success sounds like...
- "This was the first problem we had to solve."
- "We tested it again after the change."
- "The second version is clearer because..."

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What broke first, what felt confusing, and what needs reinforcement?

### Mini-demo focus
Examples of stronger wiring, sturdier structure, and clearer interfaces.

### Build activity
Students revisit prototype notes, identify their top two problems, and make a repair plan.

### Exit question
Which problem matters most to solve first?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What counts as a successful test this week?

### Mini-demo focus
How to run a repeatable test for function, durability, or audience understanding.

### Build activity
Students rebuild or reinforce the most fragile part of the project and test it multiple times.

### Exit question
What changed after testing?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
Now that the project works better, what could make it feel more complete?

### Mini-demo focus
Ways to improve audience experience through layout, controls, or more intentional visual choices.

### Build activity
Students add a second round of features only after core repairs are stable.

### Exit question
Which revision made your idea easier to understand or use?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How do you talk about a mistake without apologizing for it?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a revision story: first version, problem found, change made, result.

### Build activity
Students present a revision snapshot with photos or sketches and update their project planner for week 10.

### Exit question
What did revision teach you that the first build could not?

## Differentiation
- **Younger-leaning path:** fix one major issue, run one repeatable test, and explain the improvement in plain language.
- **Older/faster path:** address both function and audience experience, backed by clearer evidence.
- **Dignified low-floor move:** careful repair and retesting is core engineering work, not a fallback.

## Facilitator notes
- do not let students skip testing in order to chase polish
- keep revision visible by saving scraps, sketches, or photos from earlier versions
- use brief daily check-ins so each team names one must-fix problem and one realistic next step
- celebrate precise changes, not just dramatic ones
