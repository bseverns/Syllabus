# Week 7: Neighborhood Problem-Finding

## Big idea
Invention begins by noticing what a place needs, celebrates, remembers, or imagines.

## Weekly learning goals
- observe neighborhood life with curiosity and care
- generate project ideas rooted in local stories, needs, and joys
- shift from short builds into capstone planning

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus clipboards, sticky notes, large paper, markers, map printouts, and project planning sheets.

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What does your block need, celebrate, remember, protect, or imagine?

### Mini-demo focus
Examples of neighborhood-rooted projects connected to safety, joy, gathering, memory, or future dreams.

### Build activity
Students create a noticing map of places, sounds, routines, and problems they care about.

### Exit question
What neighborhood detail feels worth building about?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What makes a project idea specific instead of vague?

### Mini-demo focus
How to turn a broad idea into a simple "for people here, I want to make..." statement.

### Build activity
Students sort observations into themes, then draft one or two project directions with audience and purpose.

### Exit question
Who is your project for?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
Which tool or material from earlier weeks could help this idea come alive?

### Mini-demo focus
Quick examples of matching project ideas to mechanisms such as light, motion, sound, sensing, or code.

### Build activity
Students sketch, storyboard, or build rough cardboard mockups of their favorite idea.

### Exit question
Why does your idea matter?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How do you give feedback that helps an idea get stronger?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a short project pitch and warm/cool feedback routine.

### Build activity
Students present proposal posters, collect peer feedback, and choose a project to carry into prototype week.

### Exit question
What feedback changed your plan?

## Differentiation
- **Low-floor option:** offer project starters tied to common neighborhood themes such as parks, transit, safety, play, or memory.
- **Stretch challenge:** compare two project concepts and justify the stronger choice.
- **Team option:** let small groups form around a shared idea with clearly named roles and audience.

## Facilitator notes
- help students move from complaint to possibility
- protect the idea stage from becoming a race to the "best" concept
- make sure every student leaves the week with a chosen direction and a next build step
