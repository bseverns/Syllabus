# Week 16: Showcase and Reflection

## Big idea
Making becomes community when work is shared, celebrated, and reflected on.

## Weekly learning goals
- present projects to peers, families, staff, or community visitors
- reflect on growth across the semester
- celebrate invention, persistence, teamwork, and revision

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus showcase signage, reflection sheets, certificates if desired, and documentation devices.

## Must-have materials this week
- finished project stations and labels
- reflection sheets or reflection prompts
- cameras, phones, or tablets for documentation
- thank-you notes or appreciation plan for visitors and helpers

## Nice-to-have materials
- certificates, printed photo sheets, or celebration board
- extra batteries and emergency repair kit
- audio recording tools for student reflections
- folders or envelopes for take-home artifacts

## Fastest successful build
A project presented to an audience with a clear explanation and one meaningful reflection about what changed across the semester.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Use short speaking turns, visual reflection prompts, and options to point, record audio, or draw instead of writing at length. Keep hosting jobs concrete and rotate them.

## Grades 5–7 extension
Invite students to serve as docents, interviewers, or documentation leads while still presenting their own work. Older students can also help synthesize lessons from testing, revision, and showcase responses.

## If tech fails, do this instead
Present the project with labels, diagrams, and a spoken walkthrough. If something breaks during the showcase, model repair thinking and let the reflection include what the team learned from that moment.

## Documentation target for the week
Capture one final project photo, one student quote or audio reflection, and one record of visitor response for each project or team.

## This week, success looks like...
- students can talk about both product and process
- visitors understand that the work is neighborhood-rooted and student-made
- reflection happens with real specificity, not only celebration language

## This week, success sounds like...
- "Here is who this project is for."
- "This changed a lot from our first version."
- "Next time, I would..."

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What are you proud of, and what do you still want to tighten before guests arrive?

### Mini-demo focus
Review final setup, speaking roles, visitor flow, and calm troubleshooting moves.

### Build activity
Students complete last fixes, organize their display, and rehearse opening lines once more.

### Exit question
What do you want visitors to remember about your project?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
How do you stay present when you are also busy hosting?

### Mini-demo focus
No new technical demo; staff supports final setup and confidence-building.

### Build activity
Students run a final internal walkthrough and prepare documentation materials for the event.

### Exit question
What role will you take during the showcase?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
What does celebration look like in a makerspace?

### Mini-demo focus
Model quick visitor conversations, thank-you language, and ways to invite interaction.

### Build activity
Public showcase or family/community event.

### Exit question
What response from a visitor stayed with you?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
What did you learn about making, your neighborhood, and yourself?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a final reflection that names one skill learned, one challenge faced, and one future idea.

### Build activity
Students deinstall responsibly, create a final reflection artifact, and share what they would build next.

### Exit question
What do you want to carry into your next project?

## Differentiation
- **Younger-leaning path:** short presentation, visual reflection, and concrete hosting roles.
- **Older/faster path:** docent, interviewer, or documentation lead roles paired with a fuller reflection.
- **Dignified low-floor move:** a brief but clear explanation and honest reflection are meaningful final evidence.

## Facilitator notes
- protect time after the event for real reflection, not just cleanup
- capture quotes, photos, and short videos while the room is active
- assign one adult or one student role to battery checks and reset support during the event
- end by naming specific growth in confidence, skill, collaboration, and revision
