# Week 2: Light

## Big idea
Power moves through a path, and light can communicate safety, celebration, and story.

## Weekly learning goals
- understand simple closed circuits and switches
- use light as a design choice, not just a technical effect
- explain where power travels in a working build

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus LEDs, coin cells, copper tape, binder clips, foil tape, vellum, and map or sign templates.

## Must-have materials this week
- pre-tested LEDs, coin cells, and copper tape
- paper or cardstock for signs, symbols, or map pieces
- tape, scissors, markers, and backing cardboard
- a few working switch examples for demo

## Nice-to-have materials
- vellum or tissue for light diffusion
- foil tape, binder clips, or conductive fabric for alternate switch designs
- neighborhood map printouts or photo references
- small storage envelopes for half-finished circuit pieces

## Fastest successful build
A light-up sign, symbol, or map detail with one working LED and one on/off action.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Keep the goal to one LED and one obvious switch. Offer a short menu of shapes such as house, bus stop, tree, or street sign so younger students can focus on the power path without losing personal choice.

## Grades 5–7 extension
Invite students to design for purpose: a safety light, memory marker, or celebration sign with two light behaviors or a cleaner custom switch. They can also compare how different materials change brightness and readability.

## If tech fails, do this instead
Use laminated paper paths, arrow cards, and clothespin "switches" to model open and closed circuits. Students can still design where light belongs and mark where a switch would help a visitor use the piece.

## Documentation target for the week
Capture one image with the circuit visible and one simple diagram labeling battery, path, light, and switch.

## This week, success looks like...
- students can point to where power starts, travels, and stops
- most projects have one reliable light behavior instead of several unfinished ideas
- light choices connect to meaning, not only decoration

## This week, success sounds like...
- "The switch goes here because people need to control it."
- "The path breaks here when it is off."
- "I used light to show welcome, warning, or memory."

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
Where do we notice light in daily life: safety, celebration, art, navigation, or memory?

### Mini-demo focus
Review closed vs. open circuits and show several examples of switches.

### Build activity
Students test quick circuit stations, then choose a neighborhood icon, sign, or map fragment to light up.

### Exit question
Where does light help people on a block?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What makes a switch useful?

### Mini-demo focus
How to make a flap switch or pressure switch with paper and tape.

### Build activity
Students build a light-up sign or map element with at least one intentional on/off action.

### Exit question
How does your switch change the way someone uses the project?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
What should glow first, and why?

### Mini-demo focus
Layering paper, tracing wires cleanly, and using translucent materials to diffuse light.

### Build activity
Students add a second light, use color intentionally, or design a sign system that tells a small story.

### Exit question
What does your lighting choice communicate?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How can you show the hidden parts of a circuit to someone else?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a labeled diagram with battery, path, LED, and switch clearly marked.

### Build activity
Students create a simple how-it-works card and do a gallery walk focused on function and clarity.

### Exit question
Where did the power travel in your project?

## Differentiation
- **Younger-leaning path:** choose from a few sign or map templates and finish one light with one switch that works every time.
- **Older/faster path:** build two purposeful lights or compare two switch designs and justify the stronger choice.
- **Dignified low-floor move:** a small, readable light-up sign is a complete design task, not a lesser one.

## Facilitator notes
- keep spare batteries and pre-tested LEDs ready so troubleshooting stays productive
- ask students to predict before testing whenever possible
- use tray-based materials so the room can reset quickly between tables
- capture one image with the top paper layer lifted so the circuit path remains visible

## Bridge to capstone
How might a future project about your block use light to help people notice, remember, or feel welcome?
