# Week 4: Sound

## Big idea
Technology can be expressive, noisy, rhythmic, playful, and musical.

## Weekly learning goals
- understand sound as vibration, trigger, and response
- build a simple sound-making or sound-reactive object
- design an inviting interface for another person to use

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus littleBits, Makey Makey kits, conductive materials, speakers or buzzers, recycled sound-makers, and craft supplies for interfaces.

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What sounds belong to your neighborhood: buses, birds, music, games, construction, voices, or something else?

### Mini-demo focus
Show a few sound paths: touch to sound, switch to sound, and vibration from found materials.

### Build activity
Students try quick sound stations and list sounds that feel welcoming, surprising, annoying, or useful.

### Exit question
What kind of sound do you want your project to make or respond to?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What makes someone want to touch or play an instrument?

### Mini-demo focus
How to make a simple touch pad or conductive trigger with Makey Makey or another interface.

### Build activity
Students build a playable sound object, simple instrument, or sound machine with one clear input.

### Exit question
How does someone activate your project?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
What mood should your sound project create?

### Mini-demo focus
How to add a second sound option, change materials, or shape the body for a more intentional interaction.

### Build activity
Students add a second trigger, change the sound palette, or turn the project into a neighborhood sound sculpture.

### Exit question
How did your project invite someone to interact with it?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How can you describe a sound to someone who has not heard it yet?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a simple interaction card: touch here, listen for this, try this next.

### Build activity
Students host a small sound playground, observe how classmates use the project, and note one revision for clarity or durability.

### Exit question
What did another person do that taught you something about your design?

## Differentiation
- **Low-floor option:** one trigger and one sound outcome.
- **Stretch challenge:** build an instrument or interface with multiple touch zones or performance modes.
- **Team option:** pair interface designers with sound testers so both the object and the interaction get attention.

## Facilitator notes
- manage volume intentionally so exploration does not become chaos
- encourage students to name sounds with descriptive language, not just "good" or "bad"
- capture short audio or video clips when possible for later documentation
