# Week 13: Neighborhood Testing

## Big idea
Projects get better when real people try them and tell us what they experience.

## Weekly learning goals
- test projects with peers or invited visitors as real users
- gather useful feedback about clarity, function, and interest
- revise the project based on what users actually do, not only what builders expect

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus feedback forms, clipboards, sticky notes, timers, and cameras or tablets for documentation.

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What do you most want to find out from a tester?

### Mini-demo focus
How to run a friendly user test by watching, listening, and asking short follow-up questions.

### Build activity
Students prepare their project for testing and write two or three questions they want testers to answer.

### Exit question
What do you need feedback on most?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What should a tester try without your help?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a user test where the builder waits, observes, and takes notes before stepping in.

### Build activity
Students run first-round peer testing and track what visitors understand, miss, or misuse.

### Exit question
What surprised you about how someone used your project?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
Which feedback tells you to revise the project, and which feedback is just a preference?

### Mini-demo focus
Sorting feedback into categories: fix now, think about later, and keep as is.

### Build activity
Students make targeted revisions to improve function, clarity, or audience engagement.

### Exit question
Which revision came directly from a tester?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How can feedback make a project stronger without changing its core idea?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a short revision report with test result, change made, and new outcome.

### Build activity
Students document their testing cycle and prepare one concise story about how user feedback shaped the build.

### Exit question
What did testing teach you about your audience?

## Differentiation
- **Low-floor option:** conduct one structured test with one revision afterward.
- **Stretch challenge:** compare multiple testers' responses and identify patterns instead of reacting to one opinion.
- **Team option:** divide responsibilities into host, observer, and recorder during testing rounds.

## Facilitator notes
- coach students to observe before explaining
- keep feedback specific and actionable rather than vague praise
- invite another class, staff members, or family volunteers if possible so testing feels real
