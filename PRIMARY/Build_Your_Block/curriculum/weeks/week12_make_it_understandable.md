# Week 12: Make It Understandable

## Big idea
Communication is part of the build, not something added at the very end.

## Weekly learning goals
- add titles, labels, and explanations in plain language
- help audiences understand what the project is for and how it works
- document the project's process, systems, and changes over time

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus label cards, markers, clipboards, printed photo sheets, and project diagram templates.

## Must-have materials this week
- label cards, markers, and display paper
- project photos or sketches from earlier weeks
- diagram templates or blank paper with arrows
- examples of clear audience-facing instructions

## Nice-to-have materials
- printed photos from prototype weeks
- multilingual keywords or translation support
- laminating sleeves or tape for sturdier labels
- clipboards for peer readability tests

## Fastest successful build
A project with a clear title, one-sentence purpose, and one readable how-it-works explanation.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Use sentence frames, icons, arrows, and oral rehearsal before writing. Younger students can record a spoken explanation or make a drawn sequence if sustained writing is hard.

## Grades 5–7 extension
Invite students to create fuller diagrams, stronger story cards, or multiple layers of explanation for different audiences. Older students can also revise for tone so the project language matches its purpose.

## If tech fails, do this instead
This week works fully offline. Lean into posters, labels, maps, diagrams, audio recording on a shared phone, or live explanation practice.

## Documentation target for the week
Capture the current public-facing explanation: title, audience, what it does, and how a visitor should use it.

## This week, success looks like...
- visitors can understand the project faster
- labels support the project instead of repeating vague words
- teams connect explanation to both purpose and mechanics

## This week, success sounds like...
- "This project is for..."
- "First, the visitor does this."
- "We changed the wording because people were confused."

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
If someone saw your project for ten seconds, what should they understand?

### Mini-demo focus
Strong examples of titles, labels, and "how it works" cards written for young or non-technical audiences.

### Build activity
Students draft project titles, one-sentence descriptions, and audience instructions.

### Exit question
What do you want people to notice first?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What is the hardest part of your project to explain?

### Mini-demo focus
How to turn a complex system into a simple diagram with arrows and labeled parts.

### Build activity
Students create a how-it-works card or diagram showing inputs, outputs, and audience interaction.

### Exit question
What explanation got clearer today?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
What words fit your project's tone: playful, serious, helpful, dreamy, loud, calm?

### Mini-demo focus
Ways to make signage match the look and purpose of the project.

### Build activity
Students refine labels, add a project story, and connect visuals or colors to the build's theme.

### Exit question
How did your explanation become more inviting?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How do you know whether your explanation works?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a quick audience test using only the label card and project title.

### Build activity
Students test their signage with peers, revise confusing language, and capture photos for showcase materials.

### Exit question
What did another person misunderstand, and how did you fix it?

## Differentiation
- **Younger-leaning path:** title, one-sentence purpose, arrows, and a spoken or drawn explanation.
- **Older/faster path:** fuller systems diagram, richer process story, or multi-layer signage for different audiences.
- **Dignified low-floor move:** plain, readable explanation is strong communication, not a simplified version of it.

## Facilitator notes
- insist on plain language over jargon
- support multilingual explanation when possible with visuals, keywords, or translation help
- pull prototype photos into the room so students can show change over time
- remind students that a strong project explanation can make a simple build feel much more powerful
