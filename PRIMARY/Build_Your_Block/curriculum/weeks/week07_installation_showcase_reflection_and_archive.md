# Week 7: Installation, Showcase, Reflection, and Archive

## Teacher at a glance
- **This week is for:** installing work, hosting visitors, reflecting, and preserving the record of the intensive.
- **By the end of the week:** every team should have installed, presented, documented, and reflected on its project.
- **Prep first:** showcase map, batteries, labels, emergency fix kit, reflection prompts, and archive capture plan.
- **Watch for:** showcase energy crowding out reflection and archive work.

## Big idea
Making becomes public when the work is installed, shared, understood, and remembered.

## Weekly goals
- complete final fixes without losing calm
- install projects for visitors
- host a public showcase
- reflect on growth and archive the process

## Long-session rhythm
Each 1.75-hour session should include:
- 10 min launch / prompt
- 10 min demo or hosting check
- 30 min build, install, or rehearsal sprint 1
- 10 min break / movement / reset
- 30 min build, install, or rehearsal sprint 2
- 15 min share / document / park / cleanup

Use the reset block to lower adrenaline, reset stations, and keep the room usable for the second half of class or event prep.

## Must-have materials this week
- final projects, labels, and station pieces
- batteries, extension plan, and emergency fix kit
- reflection prompts or sheets
- photo or audio device
- take-down and storage materials

## Nice-to-have materials
- certificates
- printed photo sheets
- tablecloths or display risers
- thank-you board or guest comments

## Fastest successful build or minimum viable output
A clearly installed project station, a public presentation, and one documented reflection about how the work changed.

## Session 1
### Objective
Install the station and complete final practical fixes.

### Launch / prompt
What still has to happen before a visitor can really use this project?

### Demo focus
Model calm final-week work: install first, decorate second, fix only what matters most.

### Build sprint 1
Teams install projects, labels, and station elements.

### Break / reset
Walk the room as a visitor and note friction points.

### Build sprint 2
Teams make final practical changes to setup, reset, or readability.

### Share / document / park
Capture one full-station photo and a final risk note.

## Session 2
### Objective
Rehearse hosting, backup plans, and final speaking roles.

### Launch / prompt
What will you say if the project works immediately? What will you say if it does not?

### Demo focus
Model welcome language, handoffs, and calm troubleshooting in front of visitors.

### Build sprint 1
Teams rehearse title, purpose, how-it-works, and revision story.

### Break / reset
Quick voice rest, water, and station reset.

### Build sprint 2
Teams run one more internal walkthrough and finalize host roles.

### Share / document / park
Save one short script outline, audio rehearsal, or cue-card photo.

## Session 3
### Objective
Host the public showcase.

### Launch / prompt
What do you want visitors to remember about this project?

### Demo focus
No new technical demo. Review hosting, thanking, reset, and help signals.

### Build sprint 1
Final room check, calm warm-up, and station prep.

### Break / reset
Short team pause before guests arrive.

### Build sprint 2
Public showcase or community event.

### Share / document / park
Capture quotes, photos, visitor responses, and key project moments.

## Session 4
### Objective
Reflect, archive, deinstall responsibly, and close the studio cycle.

### Launch / prompt
What changed most about your project, your thinking, or your confidence?

### Demo focus
Model a reflection that names one skill, one challenge, one revision, and one future idea.

### Build sprint 1
Students create final reflections in visual, verbal, audio, or written form.

### Break / reset
Quiet transition into deinstallation and archive work.

### Build sprint 2
Teams deinstall responsibly, sort what should be saved, and complete final archive capture.

### Share / document / park
Close with a final circle, one quote, and one take-forward idea from each team.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Use a simpler hosting-and-reflection path:

- keep speaking turns short
- use visual or spoken reflection options
- give concrete jobs such as greet, point, reset, or thank visitors

## Grades 5–7 extension
Use a more public-leadership extension path:

- act as docents, interviewers, or archive leads while still presenting
- tighten station flow or hosting handoffs
- capture clearer archive notes about what changed across versions

## If tech fails, do this instead
Present with labels, diagrams, and a spoken walkthrough. If something fails during showcase, treat that moment as part of the reflection and archive.

## Documentation target for the week
One station photo, one visitor response, one student reflection, and one archived record of how the project changed.

## What success looks like
- visitors can understand and use the work
- students can explain both what the project does and why it matters
- the class ends with documented evidence, not only memories

## What success sounds like
- “This is for…”
- “It changed a lot from our first version.”
- “Next time I would…”

## Facilitator notes
- protect time after the showcase for reflection and archive
- keep the emergency fix kit close but the adult presence calm
- end by naming specific growth in making, revision, collaboration, and public communication
- archive should include more than photos: keep labels, diagrams, status notes, or short videos when possible

## Capstone continuity notes
Week 7 is not only a celebration. It is the final proof that the course has been building toward public work all along.
