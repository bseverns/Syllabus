# Week 5: Prototype Sprint II, Durability, and Clarity

## Teacher at a glance
- **This week is for:** strengthening prototypes so they survive contact with real users.
- **By the end of the week:** each team should have a sturdier, clearer project with an early reset routine.
- **Prep first:** repair supplies, reinforcement materials, labels, and repeat-use test routines.
- **Watch for:** teams adding extra features instead of fixing fragility or confusion.

## Big idea
If a project is for real people, it has to make sense and hold together.

## Weekly goals
- improve reliability and structure
- begin readability and public-use thinking before the final weeks
- add reset and repeated-use habits early
- keep revision evidence visible

## Long-session rhythm
Each 1.75-hour session should include:
- 10 min launch / prompt
- 10 min demo
- 30 min build sprint 1
- 10 min break / movement / reset
- 30 min build sprint 2
- 15 min share / document / park / cleanup

Use the reset block to move teams from “more features” talk back to “what would still fail for a visitor?”

## Must-have materials this week
- repair supplies and reinforcements
- labels, markers, and tape
- sturdier cardboard or supports
- durability checklist
- current prototype plus planner and status card

## Nice-to-have materials
- velcro dots, zip ties, cable ties
- display cards and arrows
- guards, handles, stands
- photo device for before/after comparisons

## Fastest successful build or minimum viable output
A prototype that works more reliably, is easier to understand, and includes a basic reset routine.

## Session 1
### Objective
Find weak points and repair the most important one first.

### Launch / prompt
What part of your project would fail first if five visitors used it?

### Demo focus
Show strong vs. weak joins, wiring paths, and reinforcement choices.

### Build sprint 1
Teams inspect their project and mark weak points.

### Break / reset
Quick triage board: fix now, later, or watch closely.

### Build sprint 2
Teams repair or reinforce the biggest risk.

### Share / document / park
Record one weak point and one repair move.

## Session 2
### Objective
Improve public readability and interaction clarity.

### Launch / prompt
Where would a visitor get confused first?

### Demo focus
Show touch points, titles, arrows, labels, and simple audience-facing instructions.

### Build sprint 1
Teams revise the interface, touch points, or layout.

### Break / reset
Peer walkthrough with no explanation at first.

### Build sprint 2
Teams revise based on confusion points and add a basic reset note.

### Share / document / park
Save one photo or note showing what got clearer.

## Session 3
### Objective
Practice repeated use and early internal testing.

### Launch / prompt
What should happen after the third user, not just the first?

### Demo focus
Model a short repeat-use test and a calm reset routine.

### Build sprint 1
Teams run repeated internal tests and track failures.

### Break / reset
Room reset and fast-fix round.

### Build sprint 2
Teams repair based on repeated use and add a stronger reset move.

### Share / document / park
Update the status card with durability and clarity notes.

## Session 4
### Objective
Close the week with a stronger prototype and a clear testing plan for Week 6.

### Launch / prompt
What does your project still need before an outside user tries it?

### Demo focus
Model a simple test-prep checklist: works, sturdy enough, readable enough, resettable enough.

### Build sprint 1
Teams make one last round of durability or readability revisions.

### Break / reset
Mini-conference on whether the project is ready for outside testing.

### Build sprint 2
Teams prepare testing questions and polish only what supports use.

### Share / document / park
Every team ends the week with a testing goal, not just a prettier object.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Use a simpler public-use path:

- fix one fragile area
- fix one confusing area
- practice one reset routine with visual support

## Grades 5–7 extension
Use a more public-ready extension path:

- compare multiple failure points and choose which to solve first
- improve station flow or touch-point clarity
- write or refine stronger user instructions after testing

## If tech fails, do this instead
Strengthen structure, labels, touch points, and user flow with cardboard and paper. A clearer physical station is still real progress.

## Documentation target for the week
One repair or clarity note per session, plus a current durability or reset check by the end of the week.

## What success looks like
- projects are sturdier than they were last week
- students are already thinking about visitors
- teams know how to reset and explain the project more clearly

## What success sounds like
- “That part was too fragile.”
- “Now people know where to press.”
- “Here is how we reset it.”

## Facilitator notes
- do not let polish replace repair
- frame durability and clarity as design work, not cleanup work
- keep visitors and younger users in the conversation
- if a team keeps adding features, redirect them to repeat-use testing immediately

## Capstone continuity notes
Week 5 should make it obvious that the project is headed toward public use, not just a working table prototype.
