# Week 1: Studio Entry, Light, Motion, and Neighborhood Sparks

## Teacher at a glance
- **This week is for:** room systems, first-build success, and early neighborhood project imagination.
- **By the end of the week:** students should have one light build, one motion build, and 2–3 possible neighborhood project directions.
- **Prep first:** tested LEDs, motors, batteries, parking bins, and a wall for neighborhood sparks.
- **Watch for:** tool exploration drifting into disconnected novelty. Keep asking who a future project could be for.

## Big idea
We are entering a neighborhood invention studio, not waiting to do the real work later.

## Weekly goals
- learn room routines, project parking, and cleanup jobs
- build visible first successes with light and motion
- connect those building blocks to neighborhood use, memory, joy, and public experience
- begin imagining what kind of project might belong on or about a block

## Long-session rhythm
Each 2.5-hour session should include:
- 10–15 min launch / prompt
- 10–15 min demo
- 45–55 min build sprint 1
- 10 min break / movement / reset
- 45–55 min build sprint 2
- 20–25 min share / document / park / cleanup

## Must-have materials this week
- LEDs, coin cells, copper tape
- motors and simple battery packs
- cardstock, cardboard scraps, tape, scissors, markers
- labeled project bins or folders
- neighborhood image prompts, maps, or memory words

## Nice-to-have materials
- vellum, foil tape, binder clips
- wheels, skewers, offset weights
- photo device for documentation
- pre-cut templates for younger students

## Fastest successful build or minimum viable output
A working light build, a working motion build, and one saved note or sketch about how one of those systems might belong in a future neighborhood project.

## Session 1
### Objective
Launch the room, teach parking and cleanup, and get every student to a first working light build.

### Launch / prompt
What is something on your block that deserves to be noticed?

### Demo focus
Room tour, tool expectations, battery direction, LED direction, and a one-light sign that works on day one.

### Build sprint 1
Students build a light-up name tag, symbol, or sign tied to self or place.

### Break / reset
Quick gallery glance, water break, and model how projects get parked safely.

### Build sprint 2
Students troubleshoot, personalize, and add one simple explanation label.

### Share / document / park
Take one photo, do one short explanation, and park the project with a next-step note.

## Session 2
### Objective
Introduce motion as behavior and build one simple moving object.

### Launch / prompt
What kinds of things move through your neighborhood every day?

### Demo focus
Show wobble, spin, and roll. Explain strong vs. weak motor attachment.

### Build sprint 1
Students test motion stations, then choose one movement type to build.

### Break / reset
Clear test lanes, reset sound level, and check loose parts.

### Build sprint 2
Students build a simple moving object and give it a job, mood, or role on the block.

### Share / document / park
Record one change that improved motion and park projects by team or table.

## Session 3
### Objective
Help students see light and motion as possible pieces of public-facing projects.

### Launch / prompt
How could light or motion help people notice, understand, or enjoy something on a block?

### Demo focus
Show a few very simple examples of signs, moving markers, or welcoming objects that use one system clearly.

### Build sprint 1
Students choose either their light build or motion build and revise it toward a neighborhood use.

### Break / reset
Short movement break and two-minute table reset.

### Build sprint 2
Students add one more purposeful design move: a clearer sign, a handle, a title, or a neighborhood reference.

### Share / document / park
Students post one possible project spark on the wall: “A future project could use light/motion to…”

## Session 4
### Objective
Close the week with a mini invention mashup and carry several possible project directions into Week 2.

### Launch / prompt
Who could use a project on your block: kids, families, neighbors, visitors, workers, or someone else?

### Demo focus
Model a short idea conference: who it is for, what it does, and what tool might help.

### Build sprint 1
Students sketch or mock up 2–3 possible neighborhood project directions using light or motion.

### Break / reset
Do a quick peer swap and warm feedback round.

### Build sprint 2
Students choose the strongest 2–3 ideas and label each with audience and possible tools.

### Share / document / park
Each student or team leaves one project-spark card in their bin and one on the wall.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Use pre-cut templates, one clear technical target at a time, and spoken explanations supported by pointing or picture cards.

## Grades 5–7 extension
Invite students to compare multiple project sparks and justify which one has the clearest audience or use.

## If tech fails, do this instead
Use paper circuits without live power, hand-powered motion, labels, and role-played neighborhood uses. Students can still seed real project ideas.

## Documentation target for the week
One photo of each working build, one motion or light test note, and 2–3 saved neighborhood project sparks per student or team.

## What success looks like
- students know how the room works
- students experience visible technical success early
- project ideas already sound public and neighborhood-rooted, not random

## What success sounds like
- “This could help people…”
- “I want a visitor to notice…”
- “We know what to try next session.”

## Facilitator notes
- teach parking and cleanup as seriously as building
- do not let Week 1 turn into isolated toy making
- keep asking audience and place questions without forcing final project decisions too early

## Capstone continuity notes
Week 1 should end with possibility, not commitment. Students should leave with multiple project sparks and a sense that the capstone has already begun.
