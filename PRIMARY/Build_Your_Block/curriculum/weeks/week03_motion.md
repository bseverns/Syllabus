# Week 3: Motion

## Big idea
Movement changes how a project feels, behaves, and tells a story.

## Weekly learning goals
- explore motors and simple mechanical movement
- connect energy, attachment choices, and resulting motion
- use movement to show mood, function, or character

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus hobby motors, battery packs, tape, skewers, wheels, offset weights, cardboard, and recycled materials.

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What kinds of things move through your neighborhood every day?

### Mini-demo focus
Show several kinds of motion: wobble, spin, roll, slide, and vibration.

### Build activity
Students rotate through short motion stations and sketch which motion feels funny, useful, fast, or calm.

### Exit question
Which kind of motion do you want to try building first?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What happens when a motor is attached off-center or with extra weight?

### Mini-demo focus
Basic motor wiring and secure attachment methods for cardboard builds.

### Build activity
Students build a vibrobot, spinner, or moving vehicle frame with one clearly visible motion behavior.

### Exit question
What made your project move the way it did?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
If your moving object had a job on the block, what would it do?

### Mini-demo focus
How shape, balance, and surface friction affect performance.

### Build activity
Students turn the mechanism into a character, creature, or neighborhood vehicle and revise the form to improve motion.

### Exit question
How did the movement change your idea?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How do you explain motion without using the word "it just works"?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a short test log: change made, result, next step.

### Build activity
Students run a motion test course, record what improved, and present one before/after change.

### Exit question
What small change had the biggest effect this week?

## Differentiation
- **Low-floor option:** focus on one motor and one simple movement outcome.
- **Stretch challenge:** tune for a specific direction, speed, or character behavior.
- **Team option:** split tasks into mechanism builder, body designer, and tester, then rotate roles.

## Facilitator notes
- make space for repeated testing on the floor or a long table
- normalize iteration by asking students to compare versions, not just finished products
- keep wiring support close so frustration does not overshadow the mechanics learning
