# Week 3: Motion

## Big idea
Movement changes how a project feels, behaves, and tells a story.

## Weekly learning goals
- explore motors and simple mechanical movement
- connect energy, attachment choices, and resulting motion
- use movement to show mood, function, or character

## Weekly rhythm
- Day 1 — launch and explore
- Day 2 — build the core skill
- Day 3 — extend and personalize
- Day 4 — share, document, and connect

## Materials
Use core materials from `docs/MATERIALS_AND_EQUIPMENT.md` plus hobby motors, battery packs, tape, skewers, wheels, offset weights, cardboard, and recycled materials.

## Must-have materials this week
- motors and battery packs tested before class
- cardboard, tape, scissors, and recycled build parts
- a safe test lane on the floor or long table
- a few attachment examples that show strong vs. weak motor mounting

## Nice-to-have materials
- wheels, bottle caps, straws, skewers, and rubber bands
- extra weights for wobble or vibration experiments
- masking tape course lines for testing
- simple trays or bins to keep small parts contained

## Fastest successful build
A moving object with one motor and one visible motion behavior such as wobble, spin, or roll.

## Grades 3–4 adaptation
Offer a narrow choice between two simple motion types, such as wobble bot or rolling frame. Keep the challenge on getting motion to happen reliably, then invite decoration after the mechanism works.

## Grades 5–7 extension
Invite students to tune for a purpose: move straighter, wobble more dramatically, carry a message, or behave like a neighborhood helper, creature, or vehicle. Older students can test and compare multiple body shapes.

## If tech fails, do this instead
Shift to hand-powered motion with pull tabs, wheels, cams, sliders, and marble runs made from cardboard. Students can still study how balance, friction, and shape create movement.

## Documentation target for the week
Capture one short before/after test note showing a change made to improve motion, plus one photo of the mechanism exposed.

## This week, success looks like...
- students repeat test-adjust-test cycles instead of treating the first try as final
- projects show one intentional movement clearly
- motor attachment and wire handling stay safe and manageable

## This week, success sounds like...
- "It moves differently when I change the balance."
- "We tested it three times."
- "The body changed because the motion needed space."

## Day 1 — Launch and explore
### Warm-up prompt
What kinds of things move through your neighborhood every day?

### Mini-demo focus
Show several kinds of motion: wobble, spin, roll, slide, and vibration.

### Build activity
Students rotate through short motion stations and sketch which motion feels funny, useful, fast, or calm.

### Exit question
Which kind of motion do you want to try building first?

## Day 2 — Build the core skill
### Warm-up prompt
What happens when a motor is attached off-center or with extra weight?

### Mini-demo focus
Basic motor wiring and secure attachment methods for cardboard builds.

### Build activity
Students build a vibrobot, spinner, or moving vehicle frame with one clearly visible motion behavior.

### Exit question
What made your project move the way it did?

## Day 3 — Extend and personalize
### Warm-up prompt
If your moving object had a job on the block, what would it do?

### Mini-demo focus
How shape, balance, and surface friction affect performance.

### Build activity
Students turn the mechanism into a character, creature, or neighborhood vehicle and revise the form to improve motion.

### Exit question
How did the movement change your idea?

## Day 4 — Share, document, and connect
### Warm-up prompt
How do you explain motion without using the word "it just works"?

### Mini-demo focus
Model a short test log: change made, result, next step.

### Build activity
Students run a motion test course, record what improved, and present one before/after change.

### Exit question
What small change had the biggest effect this week?

## Differentiation
- **Younger-leaning path:** build one simple moving object with a stable motor mount and enough time for repeated testing.
- **Older/faster path:** tune movement for direction, speed, or character and compare multiple versions.
- **Dignified low-floor move:** reliable movement and clear explanation matter more than a complex body or extra parts.

## Facilitator notes
- make space for repeated testing on the floor or a long table
- set a sound and movement boundary before build time so testing does not take over the room
- keep wiring support close so frustration does not overshadow the mechanics learning
- label parking spots for in-progress motion builds so loose parts do not disappear between days

## Bridge to capstone
What kind of neighborhood project could use motion to guide, greet, warn, or delight people?
