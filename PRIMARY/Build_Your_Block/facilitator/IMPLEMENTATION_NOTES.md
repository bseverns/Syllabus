# Facilitator Implementation Notes

## Recommended staffing
- 1 lead facilitator
- 1 assistant or floating support adult for groups above 12 students

## Room setup
Create distinct zones:
- demo / circle area
- tool and materials table
- electronics station
- build tables
- finished-project shelf
- documentation wall

## Session pacing
Young makers benefit from rhythm and repetition. Keep the top of class consistent and the transition into build time crisp.

## Classroom culture
This course works best when:
- student work is visible
- incomplete ideas are welcomed
- adults model troubleshooting
- cleanup is taught as part of the work
- public sharing happens every week, even briefly

## Differentiation
For each session, prepare:
- a basic version
- a stretch version
- a team version
- a low-language visual example

## Mixed-age strategy
- pair younger students with supportive peers when appropriate
- keep demonstrations short and concrete
- prepare optional extension cards for older or faster students
- avoid single-track instruction that leaves half the room waiting

## Behavior supports
- assign meaningful roles: materials runner, tester, photographer, presenter
- keep hands busy quickly
- use short demonstration cycles rather than long lectures
- treat repair as honorable work, not punishment

## Safety reminders
- explicit hot glue protocol
- tool carry and pass rules
- electronics handled with dry hands and calm pacing
- clear cleanup paths
- do not overload tables with materials

## Final showcase
Prepare early. Do not wait until the last week to think about display surfaces, labels, power, traffic flow, or reset procedures.
