# Week 1 Field Guide

## Focus
Launch the studio, get early wins with light and motion, and seed neighborhood sparks without letting the room overheat.

## What absolutely must happen this week
- Every student gets a real first-build success.
- The room learns parking, cleanup, and test-lane routines.
- Students leave with 1–2 usable neighborhood project sparks.

## What can be cut if time slips
- extra decoration
- too much spark talk
- multiple motion variants in one session

## Top 3 materials to prep first
- tested LEDs, coin cells, copper tape
- tested motors and battery packs
- project bins and parking labels

## Top 3 failure points to watch for
- motion testing spilling beyond the lane
- spark conversation replacing build time
- students not yet understanding how to stop, park, and restart

## Room-shape advice
- mark one motion test lane clearly
- keep light builds at tables, motion tests in one zone
- keep the spark wall visible but away from the busiest tool area

## Mixed-age caution
Younger students need one clear build target at a time. Older/faster students should compare choices or name audiences, not become roaming helpers.

## Fatigue / reset note
If noise rises and task clarity drops, stop early. Reset tools, water, tables, and the lane before sprint 2. Two strong project sparks are enough by the end of the week.

## What to photograph or capture
- one working light build
- one motion test moment
- one project-spark card or quote per student or team

## What students should have by the end of the week
- one light build
- one motion build
- a project bin or parking routine they understand
- 1–2 neighborhood project sparks worth carrying forward
