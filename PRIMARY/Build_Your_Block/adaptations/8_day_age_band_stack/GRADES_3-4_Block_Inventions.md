# Grades 3–4: Block Inventions

## Age-band frame

For grades 3–4, Build Your Block becomes a short invention lab. Students can work in pairs or small teams to build a project with one clear interaction, one audience, one revision, and one public explanation.

## Essential question

How can we make something that works for someone?

## Final outcome

A **Block Invention**: a small interactive object, model, sign, creature, helper, or station connected to neighborhood life.

## Technical expectation

By Day 8, each team should have:

- one visible working system or clear planned system
- one audience-facing cue
- one documented test or revision
- one short explanation of what it does and why it matters

## Day 1 — Maker Studio Welcome and Block Sparks

### Goal
Launch routines, make a first successful build, and collect project sparks.

### Make
A light-up maker sign, symbol, or block marker.

### Prompt
What is something on your block that people notice, need, celebrate, or remember?

### End-of-day outcome
One first build plus 2–3 project sparks.

## Day 2 — Light-Up Signs and Signals

### Goal
Use light as a signal, welcome, memory, warning, or celebration.

### Make
Paper circuit sign, map piece, welcome marker, safety signal, or memory light.

### Required idea
Light should have a job, not only decoration.

### End-of-day outcome
One light object with a battery/path/light/switch explanation.

## Day 3 — Motion With a Job

### Goal
Use motion to show behavior or purpose.

### Make
Moving helper, vehicle, creature, gate, flag, model, or reminder object.

### Required idea
Students test one change: weight, balance, attachment, wheel, surface, or shape.

### End-of-day outcome
One motion build and one test note.

## Day 4 — Interaction: Input and Output

### Goal
Build one cause/effect interaction.

### Make
Button/light, touch/sound, Makey Makey trigger, littleBits response, Scratch/SPIKE action, or simple sensor model.

### Prompt
What should a visitor do first?

### End-of-day outcome
One interaction sample and one likely project direction.

## Day 5 — Choose and Mock Up the Invention

### Goal
Move from many ideas to one buildable project.

### Make
Team planner, cardboard mockup, and first station sketch.

### Required planning questions
- Who is it for?
- What does it do?
- What does the visitor do?
- What is the smallest version worth testing?

### End-of-day outcome
Chosen project + rough mockup.

## Day 6 — Prototype Sprint

### Goal
Build the smallest working version.

### Make
Prototype with one core action or response.

### Teacher move
Hold students to one interaction first. Decoration waits until the core is visible.

### End-of-day outcome
Prototype parked with next-step card.

## Day 7 — Test, Repair, and Make It Clear

### Goal
Test with peers and revise for clarity or durability.

### Make
Labels, arrows, stronger structure, reset routine, improved touch point, or simplified interaction.

### User test question
What did the visitor understand right away? What stayed confusing?

### End-of-day outcome
One documented revision and one showcase label draft.

## Day 8 — Block Inventions Showcase

### Goal
Present, reset, explain, and reflect.

### Student explanation frame
- Our project is called __.
- It is for __.
- It does __.
- A visitor should __.
- We changed __ after testing.

### Archive
Photo, project label, and one revision story.

## Notes for grades 3–4 facilitators

- Teams of 2–3 work well.
- Keep the tool menu narrow.
- Use the main course templates selectively; do not over-paper the room.
- Push for clarity before complexity.
- Peer testing is enough if outside visitors are not available.
- A small working invention with a clear explanation is a complete success.
