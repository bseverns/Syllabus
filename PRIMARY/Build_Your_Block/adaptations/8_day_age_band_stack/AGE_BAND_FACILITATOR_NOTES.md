# Age-Band Facilitator Notes

## One course, three depths

The adaptation works best when the facilitator keeps the shared spine stable while changing the level of abstraction.

| Age band | Main verb | Main risk | Best anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| K–2 | make / notice / tell | too much abstraction | shared installation |
| Grades 3–4 | build / test / explain | too many tools | one interaction |
| Grades 5–7 | design / test / revise | feature creep | public prototype |

## Language shifts

### K–2
Say:
- “What belongs on our block?”
- “What should glow?”
- “What moves?”
- “What should someone touch or hear?”

Use less:
- capstone
- prototype
- stakeholder
- system map

### Grades 3–4
Say:
- “What does it do?”
- “Who is it for?”
- “What happens when someone uses it?”
- “What did you change?”

### Grades 5–7
Say:
- “What is the core interaction?”
- “What evidence did the user test give you?”
- “What should be removed to make it clearer?”
- “Can this survive repeated use?”

## Grouping

- K–2 can work individually inside a shared installation.
- Grades 3–4 works well in pairs or teams of 3.
- Grades 5–7 can work in teams of 2–4 with rotating roles.

## Role suggestions

### K–2
- builder
- color/detail chooser
- tester
- storyteller

### Grades 3–4
- builder
- tester
- label maker
- materials runner
- explainer

### Grades 5–7
- system lead
- structure lead
- user-test lead
- documentation lead
- station/reset lead

Rotate roles. Do not let one student own all technical work.

## Daily non-negotiables

Every day should include:

- one visible build target
- one moment of testing or trying
- one small trace of learning
- one protected cleanup/parking routine

## When the room starts to drift

Stop earlier than feels natural.

Use a short reset line:

- “Hands off builds. Eyes up. We are saving the second half.”
- “Everything parks. One sentence: what are you trying next?”
- “The room is too wide. We are narrowing to one goal.”

Then relaunch with a smaller target.
