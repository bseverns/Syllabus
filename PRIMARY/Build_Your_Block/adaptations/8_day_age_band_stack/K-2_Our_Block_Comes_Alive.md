# K–2: Our Block Comes Alive

## Age-band frame

For K–2, Build Your Block becomes a sensory neighborhood world-building course.

Students are not expected to carry full independent capstones. They contribute meaningful pieces to a shared block installation: places, people, helpers, lights, sounds, moving parts, dream spaces, and memory objects.

## Essential question

What can we make that belongs here?

## Final outcome

A shared installation called **Our Block Comes Alive**.

Each student contributes one or more pieces that may:

- glow
- move
- make sound
- invite touch
- represent a place
- show a helper, memory, dream, or favorite part of the block

## Teaching stance

Use words like:

- glow
- move
- listen
- touch
- welcome
- helper
- favorite place
- dream place
- safe place
- tiny story

Avoid overusing abstract language like prototype, audience, system, or problem-finding. Those ideas can still exist underneath the activity, but the spoken frame should stay concrete.

## Day 1 — Welcome to Our Block

### Goal
Students learn room routines, make a first visible object, and name something they notice or care about.

### Make
A maker name tag, favorite-place card, or simple light-up symbol.

### Prompt
What is a place, person, sound, animal, building, or feeling that belongs on our block?

### Teacher move
Model one complete build slowly, then give students a reachable path. Day 1 success matters more than complexity.

### End-of-day trace
Photo + dictated sentence: “I made __. It belongs on the block because __.”

## Day 2 — Light for Welcome and Memory

### Goal
Students use light to show something important.

### Make
A glowing window, star, sign, house, garden, bus stop, tree, or welcome marker.

### Prompt
What should glow on our block?

### Low-floor path
One LED or paper “planned light” if circuitry is too much.

### Stretch path
Add a switch flap, second glow point, or hidden/revealed message.

### End-of-day trace
Pointing label: “Light goes here.”

## Day 3 — Things That Move

### Goal
Students explore motion as behavior.

### Make
A moving creature, vehicle, helper, flag, weather object, or parade piece.

### Prompt
What moves through our block?

### Room control
Use short test turns. Only a few moving projects at once. Everything parks between tests.

### End-of-day trace
Student says or records: “It moves like __.”

## Day 4 — Touch, Sound, and Response

### Goal
Students experience cause and effect.

### Make
A press/listen object, touch game, soundscape card, or call-and-response station.

### Prompt
What should someone touch, hear, or try?

### Minimum viable output
One object with a clear invitation: touch here, listen here, look here, move this.

### End-of-day trace
Photo or video of the action.

## Day 5 — Choose a Piece for the Shared Block

### Goal
Students choose what they want to contribute to the final installation.

### Make
A rough cardboard version of their block piece.

### Prompt
What does our block still need?

### Teacher move
Offer project starter choices:
- a place
- a helper
- a sign
- a creature
- a light
- a sound
- a moving thing
- a dream place

### End-of-day trace
“I am making __ for our block.”

## Day 6 — Build the Final Piece

### Goal
Students build a final contribution with one clear action, image, or story.

### Make
Final block piece.

### Success looks like
The piece can be placed in the shared neighborhood and the student can say what it is.

### End-of-day trace
Photo before it enters the shared installation.

## Day 7 — Try It With a Friend

### Goal
Students test whether someone else understands or enjoys the piece.

### Make
Add an arrow, label, picture cue, stronger tape, or clearer touch point.

### Prompt
What did your friend notice first?

### End-of-day trace
One change: “I changed __.”

## Day 8 — Our Block Comes Alive Showcase

### Goal
Students share the block, speak about their piece, and reflect.

### Make
Install, host, and archive.

### Student sentence frame
“I made __. It does __. It belongs on our block because __.”

### Archive
Photo of the student with the shared block piece plus dictated sentence.

## Notes for K–2 facilitators

- Use the shared installation as the capstone structure.
- Keep builds short and material choices limited.
- Use picture labels more than writing.
- Allow spoken, pointed, or gestured explanations.
- Keep reset routines physical and visible.
- Publicness can be small: another class, staff walk-through, family photo wall, or table gallery.
