# Materials by Age Band

This guide stages the same general material world at three levels of complexity.

## Shared core materials

For all groups:

- cardboard and recycled clean packaging
- cardstock / construction paper
- masking tape / painter's tape
- markers, pencils, crayons
- scissors
- rulers
- glue sticks
- low-temp hot glue with adult supervision
- labels, index cards, sticky notes
- LEDs, coin cells, copper tape when appropriate
- simple motors and battery packs
- project bins, trays, or folders
- photo device for documentation

## K–2 materials

Prioritize sensory and structural success:

- pre-cut shapes
- larger cardboard pieces
- stickers, color tape, picture labels
- safe loose parts for collage
- LEDs and coin cells in heavily scaffolded builds
- a few prebuilt light/motion examples
- simple motors only in controlled table demos or guided builds
- sound makers, shakers, bells, paper tubes, found acoustic materials

Avoid too many tiny electronic parts in free circulation.

## Grades 3–4 materials

Add simple working systems:

- paper circuit supplies
- simple switches
- DC motors, battery packs, wheels, offset weights
- littleBits or Makey Makey if available
- alligator clips
- buttons or touch points
- Scratch/SPIKE only if devices are stable
- project planning sheets
- simple user-test cards

Keep the menu narrow: one or two active technologies per day.

## Grades 5–7 materials

Support prototype and station design:

- all grades 3–4 materials
- more structural reinforcement: zip ties, velcro, brads, stronger cardboard
- interface materials: labels, arrows, handles, guards, signage
- chosen coding platform
- Makey Makey / SPIKE / littleBits if available
- testing checklists
- station layout cards
- reset routine cards
- documentation and archive templates

## Prep priority

If time is short, prep in this order:

1. project storage
2. demo examples
3. tested batteries / LEDs / motors
4. table trays
5. templates / reflection cards
6. optional extension materials

## What not to do

- Do not put all materials on one open table at once.
- Do not offer every technology to every age band every day.
- Do not introduce coding, sound, and sensing simultaneously unless the pathway is tightly staged.
- Do not let decorative materials appear before the core build goal is visible.
