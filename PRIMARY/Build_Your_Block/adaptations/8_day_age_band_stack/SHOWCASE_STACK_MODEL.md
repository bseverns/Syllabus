# Showcase Stack Model

The three age bands can contribute to one layered neighborhood exhibition.

## Exhibition idea

**Build Your Block: A Neighborhood Comes Alive**

The final display can show how different age groups contributed different layers:

1. **K–2:** places, feelings, characters, lights, sounds, and symbols
2. **Grades 3–4:** working block inventions and interactive objects
3. **Grades 5–7:** public prototype stations, user-tested systems, and visitor flow

## If groups happen sequentially

### K–2 first
They create the shared block world: places, dream spaces, helpers, signs, textures, and story pieces.

### Grades 3–4 second
They add working inventions inspired by the shared block world: lights, moving objects, sound boxes, interaction points, and simple visitor actions.

### Grades 5–7 third
They design public stations, clearer signage, reset routines, interaction flow, and more advanced prototypes that respond to or extend the younger students' work.

## If groups happen separately

Each group can still hold a mini-showcase with its own age-appropriate format:

- K–2: walk-through gallery / table block / photo wall
- Grades 3–4: invention fair
- Grades 5–7: prototype station showcase

## Minimum publicness

Public sharing does not have to be a large event.

Acceptable formats:

- another group visits for 15 minutes
- staff gallery walk
- family pickup table
- photo wall with student quotes
- hallway installation
- ClassHub gallery
- short video walk-through

## Shared visitor prompts

Use these across the whole stack:

- What do you notice first?
- What do you think this is for?
- What should a visitor do?
- What changed after testing?
- What part of the block does this help, celebrate, remember, or imagine?

## Archive packet

For each project or contribution, try to capture:

- one photo
- student name/team
- title
- what it does
- who it is for or where it belongs
- one thing changed, tested, or learned

For K–2, this may be dictated or recorded verbally.
For grades 3–4, this can be a short label and revision note.
For grades 5–7, this should include user test evidence and a reset/station note.
