# Session 01: Welcome to the Lab

Mission: Establish room routines, first-build success, and the idea that students can make something real on day one.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s01-welcome-to-the-lab
Slug rule: start with s01- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Stage the room for a clear materials tour, cleanup workflow, and project parking system.
- Prepare battery, LED, and paper-circuit demo examples.
- Pre-test LEDs and coin cells.
- Post one simple documentation routine for the week.

Materials
- Must-have: paper circuit supplies, cardstock, cardboard scraps, markers, tape, labels
- Nice-to-have: pre-cut templates, photo device, printed routine cards

Fastest successful build
- One folded name tag or sign with a single working LED and a simple explanation of how it turns on.

Agenda
- Day 1: launch the room, routines, and first LED build
- Day 2: rebuild and troubleshoot with clearer circuit logic
- Day 3: personalize the sign with identity or neighborhood references
- Day 4: document, label, and share the build

Checkpoints
- Students complete a first working light-up build.
- Students can name at least one room routine and one cleanup job.
- Students explain in simple language what made the light turn on.

Documentation target
- One photo of each student with a first build and one labeled or spoken explanation.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Students think day-one success has to look polished -> reinforce that visible function matters more than neatness.
- Troubleshooting feels like failure -> model calm battery and LED checks out loud.
- Electronics do not cooperate -> switch to paper mock circuits and keep the routine lesson intact.

Grades 3-4 path
- Use pre-cut templates and a one-LED target.

Extensions
- Add a handmade switch.
- Create a sign or symbol tied to a neighborhood landmark.

# Session 02: Light

Mission: Help students understand closed circuits and use light as both a technical and expressive choice.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s02-light
Slug rule: start with s02- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Pre-test batteries and LEDs.
- Prepare simple switch examples and one labeled circuit diagram.
- Set out translucent materials or map/sign templates.

Materials
- Must-have: LEDs, coin cells, copper tape, paper templates, backing cardboard
- Nice-to-have: vellum, binder clips, foil tape, neighborhood map printouts

Fastest successful build
- One light-up sign, symbol, or map detail with one intentional on/off action.

Agenda
- Day 1: explore where light matters in everyday neighborhood life
- Day 2: build a light-up sign or map feature with a switch
- Day 3: extend with color, multiple lights, or diffusion
- Day 4: create a simple how-it-works card and gallery walk

Checkpoints
- Students can identify battery, path, light, and switch.
- Students build one light-based project with intentional on/off behavior.
- Students explain what the lighting choice communicates.

Documentation target
- One image with the circuit visible and one diagram labeling battery, path, light, and switch.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Students hide the circuit too early -> keep the system visible until function is dependable.
- Light is treated as decoration only -> ask what message or function the light adds.
- Circuit supplies fail -> model open and closed paths with paper strips and clothespin switches.

Grades 3-4 path
- One LED and one obvious switch with a small menu of sign shapes.

Extensions
- Add a second light with a different role.
- Build a neighborhood map fragment with labeled paths.

# Session 03: Motion

Mission: Introduce motors and simple mechanics as a way to build personality, function, and story into a project.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s03-motion
Slug rule: start with s03- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Assemble a few motion stations showing wobble, spin, and roll.
- Prepare safe motor wiring examples and sturdy attachment samples.
- Clear and mark test space on the floor or tables.

Materials
- Must-have: motors, battery packs, cardboard, tape, test-course space
- Nice-to-have: wheels, skewers, weights, recycled materials

Fastest successful build
- One moving object with one visible motion behavior.

Agenda
- Day 1: compare kinds of motion and what they communicate
- Day 2: build a simple moving object
- Day 3: personalize the moving form into a vehicle, creature, or machine
- Day 4: run tests and log before/after changes

Checkpoints
- Students create one clearly moving build.
- Students connect structure and attachment choices to movement outcomes.
- Students document one change that improved motion.

Documentation target
- One exposed-mechanism photo and one before/after test note.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Students expect motors alone to solve movement -> slow down and focus on balance and attachment.
- Testing gets rushed -> require one repeatable test path or condition.
- Motion week gets chaotic -> use test lanes and short turns.

Grades 3-4 path
- Choose between two simpler motion targets such as wobble or roll.

Extensions
- Tune for a specific speed or motion behavior.
- Add a neighborhood job or story role to the moving object.

# Session 04: Sound

Mission: Use sound as both a technical response and an expressive design material.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s04-sound
Slug rule: start with s04- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Set volume expectations and station flow before class.
- Prepare Makey Makey, littleBits, or other sound-trigger examples.
- Collect conductive and found materials for interface experiments.

Materials
- Must-have: one dependable sound path, conductive materials or simple triggers, craft supplies
- Nice-to-have: headphones, found sound-makers, extra buzzers or speakers

Fastest successful build
- One playable or reactive sound object with one clear trigger.

Agenda
- Day 1: explore neighborhood sounds and sample sound stations
- Day 2: build a simple sound-making or sound-triggered object
- Day 3: extend the interface or mood of the sound experience

Checkpoints

- Students can explain how someone activates the project.

Documentation target

- Electronic sound tools are limited -> switch to acoustic sound builds with found materials.

Grades 3-4 path
- One trigger and one sound with a simple invitation card.

Extensions
- Add multiple triggers or sound zones.
- Turn the build into a neighborhood sound sculpture.

# Session 05: Sensing and Reacting

Mission: Make inputs and outputs visible so students can design clear cause-and-effect systems.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s05-sensing-and-reacting
Slug rule: start with s05- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Prepare clear examples of input/output chains.
- Set out buttons, touch points, or sensors with matching outputs.
- Print simple arrow charts or cause/effect templates.

Materials
- Must-have: one clear input method, one clear output method, cardboard, labels
- Nice-to-have: extra sensors, arrow cards, clipboards

Fastest successful build
- One project with a clear input and a clear output.

Agenda
- Day 1: test reactive stations and sort inputs from outputs
- Day 2: build a clear reactive object
- Day 3: add a second response or improve the interface
- Day 4: diagram the cause-and-effect chain and partner test it

Checkpoints
- Students can name the input and the output in their own build.
- Students build one dependable trigger/response interaction.
- Students explain the chain in plain language.

Documentation target
- One input/output diagram and one quick test note.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Students pile on effects too early -> keep one readable chain before adding complexity.
- Trigger areas are unclear -> label touch, press, or cover points explicitly.
- Sensors are unreliable -> use buttons, touch points, paper flaps, or switches instead.

Grades 3-4 path
- One obvious trigger and one visible response with arrows and spoken explanation.

Extensions
- Add layered outputs.
- Build a simple game or alert with clear feedback.

# Session 07: Neighborhood Problem-Finding

Mission: Shift from short skill-builds into capstone planning rooted in neighborhood life.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s07-neighborhood-problem-finding
Slug rule: start with s07- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Prepare sample project themes tied to safety, joy, memory, or gathering.
- Print maps, planning sheets, and pitch templates.
- Set up feedback routines for project proposals.

Materials
- Must-have: clipboards or large paper, markers, sticky notes, maps, planning sheets
- Nice-to-have: observation frames, voice recorder, example mockups

Fastest successful build
- One chosen project direction with audience, purpose, and a smallest testable version.

Agenda
- Day 1: notice neighborhood needs, joys, and stories
- Day 2: turn observations into specific project statements
- Day 3: sketch or mock up a first idea
- Day 4: pitch ideas and collect feedback

Checkpoints
- Students identify an audience and purpose for a project.
- Students leave with one chosen capstone direction.
- Students can explain why the idea matters to place or people.

Documentation target
- One saved project idea with audience, purpose, and first testable version.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Students stay vague -> require “for people here, I want to make...” language.
- Idea choice becomes competitive -> protect multiple valid directions.
- Writing slows students down -> allow drawing, collage, or verbal planning.

Grades 3-4 path
- Use sentence stems, drawing, and project starter cards.

Extensions
- Compare two ideas and justify the stronger choice.
- Build a faster cardboard mockup before the pitch.

# Session 08: Prototype 1

# Session 09: Prototype 2

Mission: Use revision and testing to improve reliability, clarity, and usability.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s09-prototype-2
Slug rule: start with s09- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Lay out repair materials and test logs.
- Prepare examples of stronger connections and clearer interfaces.
- Model how to name a problem without apologizing for it.

Materials
- Must-have: repair supplies, test logs, replacement parts, documentation tools
- Nice-to-have: stronger cardboard, color-coded bug notes, before/after photo device

Fastest successful build
- One major problem repaired and retested with evidence that the change helped.

Agenda
- Day 1: identify the biggest failure points
- Day 2: repair and retest the most fragile area
- Day 3: improve usability after function stabilizes

- Teams guess instead of testing -> ask what evidence will show the repair worked.

Grades 3-4 path
- Fix one big issue and test it clearly.

Extensions

# Session 10: Combine Systems

Mission: Help students integrate multiple technical building blocks without losing clarity.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s10-combine-systems
Slug rule: start with s10- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Prepare examples of projects that combine two systems clearly.
- Print or model a simple systems map.
- Gather each team's chosen materials in advance.

Materials
- Must-have: current projects, systems-map sheets, labels, shared maker tools
- Nice-to-have: color-coded tape, extra adapters, a staging table

Fastest successful build
- Two systems working together in one understandable interaction.

Agenda
- Day 1: choose which systems belong in the build
- Day 2: connect a second system without breaking the first
- Day 3: simplify timing, layout, or interactions
- Day 4: create a system diagram and gather partner feedback

Checkpoints
- Students combine at least two systems intentionally.
- Students can name which feature is central and which supports it.
- Students identify one remaining disconnect in the build.

Documentation target
- One systems map naming the main interaction and the supporting system.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Students add features randomly -> ask what each new feature is for.
- Combined builds become hard to explain -> require a one-page systems map.
- Tech is limited -> combine lower-tech systems such as light + sign or motion + structure.

Grades 3-4 path
- Keep the challenge to one main system plus one support system.

Extensions
- Add branching behavior or sequencing.
- Compare the integrated build to an earlier simpler version.

# Session 11: Make It Durable

Mission: Prepare projects for repeated use by strengthening fragile structures, connections, and interfaces.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s11-make-it-durable
Slug rule: start with s11- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Collect reinforcement materials and example durable joins.
- Prepare a quick durability checklist.
- Set expectations for repeat-use testing.

Materials
- Must-have: stronger cardboard, repair supplies, labels, stress-test routine
- Nice-to-have: velcro dots, cable ties, guards, stands

Fastest successful build
- One fragile area reinforced and one reset routine tested.

Agenda
- Day 1: inspect the project for weak points
- Day 2: reinforce the most fragile areas
- Day 3: improve interface safety and readability
- Day 4: run repeat-use tests and create a reset or repair guide

Checkpoints
- Students identify and reinforce a fragile area.
- Students run a repeat-use or stress test.
- Students create a basic reset or repair instruction.

Documentation target
- One durability record showing what failed or held and what changed.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Reinforcement feels boring -> frame it as designing for real users.
- Students only test once -> require repeated use or multiple users.
- Electronics are failing unpredictably -> swap tested batteries and check the simplest point first.

Grades 3-4 path
- Focus on the single weakest point and one reset routine.

Extensions
- Build for younger visitors or family use.
- Improve both durability and interface clarity in one pass.

# Session 12: Make It Understandable

Mission: Turn explanation into part of the project by adding titles, labels, diagrams, and plain-language instructions.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s12-make-it-understandable
Slug rule: start with s12- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Bring strong examples of title cards and how-it-works diagrams.
- Print signage templates or plain label cards.
- Prepare a peer-test routine for explanations.

Materials
- Must-have: label cards, markers, diagram templates, draft signage materials
- Nice-to-have: photo sheets, multilingual keywords, lamination or tape

Fastest successful build
- A clear title, one-sentence purpose, and one readable explanation card.

Agenda
- Day 1: draft project title and one-sentence purpose
- Day 2: diagram how the build works in plain language
- Day 3: match signage to the tone and look of the project

Checkpoints
- Students create a title and audience-facing purpose statement.
- Students produce at least one diagram or how-it-works card.

Documentation target

- Writing stamina is low -> use arrows, icons, audio, or a spoken explanation.

Grades 3-4 path
- Use sentence frames, pointing, and drawn sequences.

Extensions
- Add a richer story card or prototype timeline.

# Session 13: Neighborhood Testing

Mission: Improve projects by observing real users and revising from evidence instead of assumption.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s13-neighborhood-testing
Slug rule: start with s13- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Prepare quick tester-feedback forms and observation routines.
- Invite another class, staff members, or volunteers if possible.
- Model how to watch before stepping in to explain.

Materials
- Must-have: feedback forms, clipboards, sticky notes, timers
- Nice-to-have: cameras, tablets, invited testers

Fastest successful build
- One structured user test and one revision made directly from that test.

Agenda
- Day 1: write testing questions and prepare projects
- Day 2: run first-round user tests
- Day 3: sort feedback and revise accordingly
- Day 4: document the test-change-result cycle

Checkpoints
- Students gather feedback from real testers.
- Students make at least one revision directly tied to observed use.
- Students can tell the story of how testing changed the project.

Documentation target
- One test record with question, observation, revision, and result.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Builders over-explain during testing -> assign observer roles and delay help.
- Feedback becomes vague praise -> require one specific behavior or confusion point.
- Powered features are unreliable -> still test signage, interaction flow, and clarity.

Grades 3-4 path
- One shorter test round, one supported observation, one clear change.

Extensions
- Compare patterns across multiple testers.
- Test with different age groups if available.

# Session 15: Showcase Setup

Mission: Turn finished projects into usable public displays by solving traffic, power, signage, reset, and hosting logistics.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s15-showcase-setup
Slug rule: start with s15- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Walk the room as a visitor and pre-plan display zones.
- Gather table setup, signage, and emergency repair materials.
- Prepare a day-of job chart for teams.

Materials
- Must-have: table setup materials, power plan or batteries, labels, emergency fix kit
- Nice-to-have: stands, floor arrows, waiting spots, layered display pieces

Fastest successful build

Agenda

- Power is inconsistent -> prepare a battery or low-tech fallback demonstration.

Grades 3-4 path
- One clear station, one welcome line, and one reset routine.

Extensions
- Add docent roles or layered displays.
- Improve the station mood with color or audience prompts.

# Session 16: Showcase and Reflection

Mission: Share finished neighborhood-rooted work with an audience and reflect on growth across the full course.
Lesson slug (for course.yaml): s16-showcase-and-reflection
Slug rule: start with s16- and use lowercase letters, numbers, and dashes only.

Teacher prep
- Confirm final event timing and hosting flow.
- Prepare reflection sheets or visual reflection options.
- Set aside time after the event for deinstallation and documentation.

Materials
- Must-have: showcase signage, reflection prompts, documentation device
- Nice-to-have: certificates, extra batteries, audio recorder, take-home folders

Fastest successful build
- One public presentation and one meaningful reflection about what changed across the semester.

Agenda
- Day 1: finish final fixes and rehearse opening lines
- Day 2: run the last internal walkthrough and assign roles
- Day 3: host the showcase or public event
- Day 4: reflect, deinstall responsibly, and document growth

Checkpoints
- Students present work to peers, families, staff, or community visitors.
- Students reflect on one skill learned, one challenge faced, and one future idea.

Documentation target
- One final project photo, one student quote or reflection, and one visitor response.

Common stuck points + fixes
- Showcase energy crowds out reflection -> protect debrief time after the event.
- Students understate their growth -> prompt for concrete examples of confidence, teamwork, and revision.
- A project fails during the event -> model calm reset language and include that moment in reflection.

Grades 3-4 path
- Use short speaking turns and visual or spoken reflection options.

Extensions
- Use student docents or interviewers during the event.
- Capture quotes or short videos for future program documentation.
