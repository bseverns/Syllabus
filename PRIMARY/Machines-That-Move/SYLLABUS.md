# Syllabus — Machines That Move

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners build and test pivots, sliders, wheels, and cams, then use one mechanism to make a sculpture, toy, or story object move on purpose.
- **Group / staffing:** 10–16 learners; one lead with an assistant recommended for cutting, hole-punching, and shared tool flow.
- **Final share:** a working movement object and an explanation of where motion begins, travels, and appears.
- **Agreement:** *We change one thing, then test again.*

## 75-minute session rhythm

- 10 min launch and mechanism observation
- 10 min demo with working and half-working versions
- 25 min build sprint 1
- 5 min materials reset
- 15 min build sprint 2
- 10 min test, documentation, cleanup, and parking

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core:** cardboard panels and scraps; 36 brads/paper fasteners; 24 straws; 24 short capped dowels/skewers; 24 bottle caps or cardboard wheels; tape; glue; rubber bands; string; markers; scissors; hole punches; 12 build trays; and a pre-cut circle/cam set.

**Optional:** LEGO, small hand cranks, low-speed motors, and 3D-printed cams/gears. None are needed for the pilot.

**Preflight:** adults pre-punch thick card and prepare/cap dowels. Mark a single motion test lane. Keep tape-hinge alternatives ready for learners who should not use brads.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Pivots and sliders

**Target:** compare a hinge/pivot with a slider.

**Prompt:** *What stays still, and what gets to move?*

**Build:** learners make a flap, lever, or slider creature. They test one motion before decorating.

**Evidence:** labeled sketch or spoken explanation: `I push here; this moves here.`

### Session 2 — Wheels and axles

**Target:** make an object roll, spin, or carry movement from one part to another.

**Prompt:** *What changes when the axle is loose, tight, centered, or off-center?*

**Build:** learners make a rolling cart, spinning sign, or wheel-driven character; change one variable after the first test.

**Evidence:** record a before/after test result using arrows, photo, or spoken note.

### Session 3 — Cams and repeated action

**Target:** use a hand crank or wheel to repeat a lift, drop, flap, or bounce.

**Prompt:** *What action should happen again and again?*

**Build:** teams attach a cam to a known-good base and tune the follower/character position.

**Evidence:** `turns / moves / returns` check plus one explanation of the repeated action.

### Session 4 — Motion with purpose

**Target:** make a playable object move for a story, game, or helpful purpose.

**Prompt:** *Who uses this, and what should the movement tell them?*

**Build:** reinforce one reliable mechanism, add a clear audience/story detail, and run peer tests.

**Evidence:** final demo and one revision note based on what a peer understood or could not use.

## Safety, access, and recovery

Adults manage sharp cutting, thick-card punching, dowel preparation, and optional motors. Learners may design, build, test, narrate, or document. If a cam mechanism stalls, return to a reliable pivot, slider, or wheel; a clear single movement is a complete success.

## Assessment and partner description

Observe: identifies fixed/moving parts; tests a mechanism; changes one variable; explains motion flow; cares for shared materials and test lanes.

**Partner description:** A hands-on mechanisms studio where learners turn cardboard, wheels, cams, and found materials into moving objects with clear stories and testable behavior.
