# Build a Neighborhood

> **PILOT syllabus.** Start with the shared [K–2 operating model](../K2-PILOT-OPERATING-MODEL.md). This keeps the audience-and-place ethic of [Build Your Block](../../PRIMARY/Build_Your_Block/README.md) while removing its electronics, capstone, and seven-week load.

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners build places people use, connect them into a shared block, and give visitors a tour.
- **Group / staffing:** 8–12 learners; one lead, with an assistant recommended for a shared-floor build or high support needs.
- **Final share:** a group neighborhood map/model with each learner or pair able to identify one place and one user.
- **Community agreement:** *We build for people, not only for ourselves.*

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core tote:** 12 low cardboard bases or tray lids; 36 small cardboard panels; blocks or DUPLO/LEGO; 24 paper tubes; 24 clean recycled containers; masking tape; painter's tape; glue sticks; markers; construction paper; fabric scraps; 12–24 large toy figures; 12 clipboards or firm drawing boards; and printed picture cards for home, park, library, bus stop, garden, store, clinic, and playground.

**Helpful extras:** simple road/river strips, local map or neighborhood photos, name labels, and one facilitator camera for documentation.

**Prep:** choose a floor/table footprint; pre-sort materials into building, connecting, and decorating trays; make one simple place model; post the question `Who uses this place?`.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Notice places and people

**Target:** name a place and someone who might use it.
**Prompt:** *Where do people go to play, rest, learn, get help, or meet?*
**Demo:** choose one picture card, add one block, and say who it helps.
**Build:** learners draw, point to, or begin a small model of one place.
**Evidence / parking:** facilitator captures `place + user` in a photo, sketch, or spoken note.

### Session 2 — Build one place

**Target:** make a place recognizable enough for another learner to use in play.
**Prompt:** *What does someone need to see or do here?*
**Demo:** add an entrance, path, sign, or seat to make a model readable.
**Build:** learners complete one place with one access feature and one detail.
**Evidence / parking:** partner points to where they would start; builder changes one confusing feature if needed.

### Session 3 — Connect the block

**Target:** connect individual places with paths, roads, gardens, or shared spaces.
**Prompt:** *How does a person get from one place to another?*
**Demo:** connect two models with a tape road and test it with a toy figure.
**Build:** teams negotiate one connection; the lead limits each team to one new shared-material pull at a time.
**Evidence / parking:** learners trace or walk a route and name one place they can reach.

### Session 4 — Welcome visitors

**Target:** explain the neighborhood as a place for people.
**Prompt:** *Who should come visit your block, and what can they do here?*
**Demo:** model a 20-second tour: `This is ___. It is for ___. You can ___.`
**Build:** add signs, people, or one welcome feature; practice tours in pairs.
**Evidence / share:** visitors/tables tour the block; capture one learner quote and one full-map photo before reset.

## Safety, access, and failure backup

Use large parts appropriate to the learners present; adults manage cutting and do not require writing. Keep the shared base low and reachable. A learner may contribute by choosing a place card, making a sign, arranging people, building, or narrating the tour.

If the group model becomes territorial or crowded, split it into two blocks connected by a paper road during Session 3. Connection matters more than one giant model.

## Assessment and public description

Use the shared four look-fors, plus: **the learner connects a built place to a person or purpose.**

**Partner description:** A hands-on civic-imagination workshop where young learners build a shared neighborhood and practice noticing who public places are for.

## Pilot review after delivery

Record: whether the shared footprint was large enough, which picture cards prompted the richest talk, whether 12 learners could connect models without bottlenecking, and what storage worked between sessions.
