# Syllabus — Friendly City Lab

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners notice where public messages help or confuse people, then create and test a clearer sign, map, label, or guide for a real place.
- **Group / staffing:** 10–16 learners; one lead, with an assistant recommended for an observation walk or off-room site work.
- **Final share:** a helpful-media prototype and a user test explaining what became clearer.
- **Agreement:** *We design for people who do not already know what we know.*

## 75-minute session rhythm

- 10 min launch and observation question
- 10 min example/mini-demo
- 25 min observation or build sprint 1
- 5 min reset and evidence sort
- 15 min build/test sprint 2
- 10 min share, documentation, cleanup, and parking

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core:** clipboards; pencils; markers; colored paper; index cards; cardboard; tape; rulers; 12 observation sheets; 24 arrow/icon cards; 12 simple base maps or room outlines; sticky notes; and a `confusing / helpful` evidence board.

**Optional:** printer, camera/tablet for facilitator documentation, local map, laminator, and approved school signage examples.

**Preflight:** choose a single observation site; confirm adult supervision and host permission; do not photograph people or replace actual signs without approval. Prepare an in-room fallback with photos/printed signs.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Notice public messages

**Target:** identify a message that helps, confuses, welcomes, or leaves out a user.

**Prompt:** *What does this sign expect you to already know?*

**Build:** learners take a short observation walk or inspect provided images, then mark one helpful and one confusing message.

**Evidence:** `I noticed ___; it helps/confuses ___ because ___.`

### Session 2 — Make clarity visible

**Target:** use words, icons, arrows, color, and layout for a specific user.

**Prompt:** *How can someone understand this quickly?*

**Build:** learners redesign one sign or create a simple map key; peers test without the designer explaining first.

**Evidence:** tester points to what they understood; designer changes one unclear part.

### Session 3 — Prototype a helpful guide

**Target:** build a clear sign, map, label system, or short guide for a chosen place/routine.

**Prompt:** *Who needs this, and what should they do next?*

**Build:** teams make a paper/cardboard prototype with one entry point, one clear action, and one supporting visual.

**Evidence:** user test record: `start / understood / got stuck / changed`.

### Session 4 — Walk through and revise

**Target:** show how the prototype makes a real experience more legible or kind.

**Prompt:** *What became easier after our design changed?*

**Build:** revise from user evidence, then host a short walk-through or tabletop demo.

**Evidence:** final prototype, one tester quote, and a learner explanation of a revision.

## Safety, access, and recovery

Keep observation routes short, accessible, and optional; learners can work from printed examples instead. Provide icon cards, oral description, arrows, partner sketching, and large-print materials. If host permission or weather blocks the observation, use the prepared in-room site examples without changing the project goal.

## Assessment and partner description

Observe: notices a user need; identifies clarity/confusion; makes a testable public message; changes a design after user evidence; explains who the artifact helps.

**Partner description:** A hands-on civic-design studio where learners turn signs, maps, labels, and guides into clearer, kinder tools for real public spaces.

## Pilot review after delivery

Record site-permission friction, observation timing, which icon cards were useful, whether user testing created useful revisions, and whether printing/lamination mattered to learning or only presentation.
