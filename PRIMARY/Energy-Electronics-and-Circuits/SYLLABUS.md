# Syllabus — Energy, Electronics & Circuits

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners route low-voltage energy through a light, sound, or motion system, then diagnose and improve one small invention.
- **Group / staffing:** 10–16 learners; one lead with an assistant recommended whenever motors or multiple powered stations are active.
- **Final share:** a working input/output invention plus a simple explanation of where energy starts, what controls it, and what it does.
- **Agreement:** *We test power before we add features.*

## 75-minute session rhythm

- 10 min launch and visible question
- 10 min low-voltage demo and safety check
- 25 min build sprint 1
- 5 min reset, test-bin check, and water
- 15 min build sprint 2
- 10 min demo, documentation, cleanup, and parking

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core:** 24 coin cells or 12 AA packs with holders; 36 LEDs; copper tape; cardstock; tape; markers; 12 small cardboard bases; 12 binder clips; 12 pre-built paper-switch strips; 12 trays; scissors; glue sticks; and a `tested / needs checking` bin.

**Session 3 stations:** 6 low-voltage buzzers, 6 small DC motors with protected leads, 6 switches/buttons, 6 alligator-clip sets, and paper/cardboard housings. Rotate teams; do not give every learner every powered part.

**Preflight:** no mains electricity, soldering, loose lithium cells, or unprotected wires. Test polarity, battery condition, and all moving parts before class. Plan a paper-circuit-only fallback.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Make energy visible

**Target:** make and explain one closed paper circuit.

**Prompt:** *What has to connect for the light to work?*

**Build:** learners make a light-up sign or symbol with LED, coin cell, and copper tape. Facilitator models a deliberate open path and a polarity reversal.

**Evidence:** learner marks `power / path / light` on a sketch or points while explaining.

### Session 2 — Add control

**Target:** use a paper switch to change an output.

**Prompt:** *How can we choose when something turns on?*

**Build:** learners add a flap, pressure, or contact switch to yesterday's circuit, then compare always-on and controlled-on behavior.

**Evidence:** test twice, record one failure, and name the change that fixed it.

### Session 3 — Sound or motion station

**Target:** make a safe low-voltage output do one clear thing.

**Prompt:** *Should this invention light, buzz, spin, or vibrate?*

**Build:** teams rotate through a buzzer or motor station, attaching the output to a cardboard body. The adult checks every power connection before a motor/buzzer runs.

**Evidence:** facilitator checks `power / control / output`; team documents one attachment improvement.

### Session 4 — Tiny invention share

**Target:** combine a clear control with one output for a user or story.

**Prompt:** *Who uses this invention, and what happens when they touch or switch it?*

**Build:** teams choose a light, sound, or motion output; reinforce connections, name the user, and rehearse a 30-second demo.

**Evidence:** final demo plus one spoken or drawn `what changed after testing` note.

## Safety, access, and recovery

Adults manage batteries, motors, and any repair beyond a simple taped connection. Learners may be builder, tester, explainer, or documenter. If powered components stall, return to Session 2's working paper-switch circuit; control logic remains the learning target.

## Assessment and partner description

Observe: completes a safe connection; tests a path; changes one variable after a failure; explains input, energy path, and output; returns components to the correct bin.

**Partner description:** A hands-on, low-voltage circuitry studio where learners make lights, switches, sound, and motion understandable through small working inventions.
