# Scratch Arcade Adventure Studio (Grades 3–5) — School-Facing Syllabus

**Grade level:** 3–5  
**Meeting time:** 1 hour/week for 12 weeks  
**Class size:** about 18 students  
**Platform:** Scratch (MIT) — scratch.mit.edu  
**Core promise:** Students design and publish a more advanced arcade-style game in Scratch.

---

## Course overview

Scratch Arcade Adventure Studio is for students who already know the basics of Scratch and are ready to build games with stronger systems and polish.

Students will:

- design a game loop with clear rules
- build a HUD using variables such as score, health, timer, or energy
- create enemies, hazards, and power-ups
- organize a game into stages, rooms, or waves
- playtest, debug, revise, and publish

This course emphasizes design thinking as much as coding. Students learn that a game should communicate clearly with the player: what the goal is, what is dangerous, what is helpful, and how to recover after a mistake.

---

## Who this course is for

This course is recommended for students who can already:

- open and save Scratch projects
- move sprites with code
- use loops and simple conditionals
- change a variable during play

No typing-heavy programming experience is required.

---

## Learning goals

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- build a Scratch game with a stable title screen, instructions, and ending
- use multiple variables to control a game system
- create at least one enemy pattern and one power-up system
- use broadcasts and scene changes to manage game flow
- run playtests, identify bugs, and revise based on feedback

---

## Weekly arc

**Weeks 1–3:** choose a game idea, build the player loop, and create a working HUD  
**Weeks 4–6:** add enemies, hazards, checkpoints, and stage flow  
**Weeks 7–8:** build a final challenge and stronger feedback  
**Weeks 9–10:** playtest, debug, and improve clarity and fairness  
**Weeks 11–12:** finish, publish, and showcase

---

## Class format

Each class follows a predictable routine:

1. short demo
2. build sprint
3. mini playtest or teacher checkpoint
4. share + save

This keeps the room active while giving students enough time to make real progress.

---

## Materials and technology

- device with internet access
- mouse recommended
- Scratch account or class studio
- headphones optional

---

## Studio culture and content boundaries

We encourage games that are exciting, silly, surprising, and imaginative. We also teach students to think about the player experience.

Students practice:

- giving kind, useful feedback
- making rules clear to a first-time player
- revising unfair or confusing mechanics

Projects may not include hate symbols, targeted bullying, or graphic content. We steer away from realistic weapons and encourage students to redesign intense ideas into safer, more creative mechanics.

---

## Assessment

Assessment is growth-focused and based on:

- weekly milestones
- participation in playtesting and revision
- completion of the final game requirements

Creativity, persistence, and clarity matter more than perfection.

---

## Final showcase

During the final session, students present their published games in a mini arcade showcase and give a short creator talk about one mechanic they are proud of and one challenge they solved.
