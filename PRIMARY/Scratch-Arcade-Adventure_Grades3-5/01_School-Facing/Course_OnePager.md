# Scratch Arcade Adventure Studio (Grades 3–5) — One-Page Course Summary

**Audience:** Grades 3–5  
**Duration:** 12 weeks  
**Cadence:** 1 hour/week  
**Platform:** Scratch (MIT)

## What students make

A polished arcade-style game with:

- a title screen
- instructions players can understand
- score, health, timer, or another HUD system
- enemies, hazards, or chase patterns
- power-ups or special abilities
- a final challenge and ending

## Best fit

This class works best for students who have already used Scratch before and are ready for a bigger design challenge.

Students should already know how to:

- move a sprite
- use `when green flag clicked`
- repeat actions with loops
- make and change a variable

## Skills students learn

- stronger game logic with variables, broadcasts, and state changes
- player feedback: sound, animation, screen text, and visible goals
- fair challenge design: checkpoints, readable hazards, and difficulty ramp
- debugging and playtesting using clear bug reports
- presentation and reflection through an arcade-style showcase

## Class flow

- quick demo
- build sprint
- mini test round
- share, save, and next-step note

## Studio culture

Students learn that good games are not just hard or flashy. Good games are:

- readable
- fair
- funny or surprising
- fixable
- welcoming to a player who has never seen them before

## Final showcase

In the last session, students host a mini arcade festival and explain:

- how their game works
- their favorite mechanic
- one bug or challenge they solved
