# Supplemental Resources – DIY Instrument Lab (College)

This file lists tools, libraries, and references that tend to be useful when running the course.

Adapt and localize as needed.

---

## 1. Tools & Workspace

- Basic electronics lab:
  - Breadboards, jumper wires, crocodile clips  
  - Soldering irons, solder, helping hands, fume extraction  
  - Wire strippers, cutters, small pliers, screwdrivers  
  - Multimeters for continuity and voltage checks
- Basic fabrication:
  - Hand saws, drills, clamps  
  - Access to a laser cutter or 3D printer (optional but nice for enclosures)  
  - Cardboard and foam-core for quick mockups

---

## 2. Audio & Monitoring

- Small mixers, headphone amps, and/or powered speakers  
- A few decent dynamic microphones  
- A couple of DI boxes for safely plugging into PA systems  
- At least one computer running a DAW or free software (Reaper, Audacity, etc.)

---

## 3. Parts & Components

Commonly-used components:

- Contact mic elements (piezo discs)  
- 1/4" and 1/8" jacks, RCA jacks, terminal blocks  
- Resistors (a basic E12 or E24 kit)  
- Capacitors (ceramic and electrolytic, audio-friendly ranges)  
- Potentiometers, switches, momentary buttons  
- A few op-amps, NE555 timers, simple transistors  
- Microcontrollers (Arduino-class boards, etc.) for higher-tier builds

You can maintain a simple `BOM_TEMPLATE.md` elsewhere in your Syllabus repo if you want a consistent format across courses.

---

## 4. Software & Libraries

- DAW for recording and routing (Reaper, Audacity, etc.)  
- Visual patching tools (Pure Data, Max, etc.) if you want to offload some complexity to software  
- Arduino IDE or PlatformIO for microcontroller programming  
- Optional: visual coding environments (like p5.js) for visual feedback paired with instruments

---

## 5. Documentation Practices

Encourage students to:

- Keep build logs with dates, photos, and “what broke” notes  
- Archive circuit diagrams (even messy ones) and update them as the build changes  
- Make short demo recordings periodically, not only at the end

You can model this by keeping a small course “lab notebook” or wiki with each cohort’s instruments.

---

## 6. Local Suppliers & Repair Ecosystem

Consider maintaining a local list in `LOCAL_RESOURCES.md` (or elsewhere) that includes:

- Electronics stores or surplus shops  
- Makerspaces and community labs  
- Local luthiers, repair techs, and DIY music scenes  
- E-waste or thrift shops suitable for harvesting enclosures and parts
