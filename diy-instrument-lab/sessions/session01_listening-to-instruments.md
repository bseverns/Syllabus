# Week 1 – Listening to Instruments

## At a glance

- Length: 2–3 hours  
- Mode: listening + discussion + tool/safety overview + first sketches  
- Big idea: Start by expanding what “instrument” can mean, and set the lab culture for the term.

## Goals

By the end of Week 1, students should:

- Have a shared sense of what the course covers and what it does *not* try to do.  
- Be familiar with lab expectations and basic safety (tools, sound levels, power).  
- Have listened to several examples of self-built or unusual instruments.  
- Have started an **Instrument & Rig Journal** with first entries.  

This week is mostly about framing and expectation-setting rather than technical mastery.

## Suggested listening / media

Bring a short, varied listening/viewing set, such as:

- 1–2 excerpts of self-built electronic instruments or noise boxes  
- 1 example of a highly controlled, commercial instrument demo (e.g., keyboard, controller)  
- 1–2 clips of sound art / installation where the “instrument” is ambiguous (room, object, environment)

Use very short clips; the point is diversity, not completeness.

---

## Before class (instructor prep)

- **Space:**  
  - Arrange the room so students can both listen comfortably and later gather around a table or bench.  
  - Bring any instruments or DIY devices you have access to for show-and-tell.

- **Safety & tools:**  
  - Prepare a clear, *concise* safety overview: electrical, hearing, physical tools.  
  - Decide whether you’ll do hands-on tool handling this week or in Week 2.

- **Handouts or slides:**  
  - A one-page outline of the course structure (weeks + big checkpoints).  
  - A short list of what students need (e.g., notebook, storage box, etc.).

---

## In-class plan

### 0–20 min – Welcome, intros, and expectations

- Introduce yourself and the course in 2–3 sentences:  
  - Emphasize **experimentation**, **failure as learning**, and **no prior electronics virtuosity required**.  
- Do a quick round:
  - Name, background (major or practice), and one instrument or tool they feel comfortable with (can be voice, DAW, phone, etc.).

Outline:

- Major checkpoints (Prototype 0, Final Instrument, performance/installation, documentation).  
- How grading will roughly work (participation, studies, builds, docs).  
- How you expect critique to function (supportive, specific, not about virtuosity).

---

### 20–45 min – Listening: What is an instrument?

Play short excerpts (30–60 seconds each) of 4–6 contrasting examples.

After each example, ask a few quick questions:

- “Where is the instrument here?”  
- “How many parts of this system feel instrument-like?”  
- “What gestures do you imagine the performer is using?”  
- “Does this feel DIY or commercial? What makes you say that?”

Write down key words from student responses (e.g., **fragile**, **industrial**, **toy-like**, **improvised**, **controlled**).

---

### 45–65 min – Instrument definitions (small group and whole class)

Hand out index cards or sticky notes.

1. **Individual (5 min):**  
   - Write: *“For me, an instrument is…”* and finish the sentence.  
   - Then: *“One thing this course could change about that definition is…”*

2. **Small groups (10–15 min):**  
   - In groups of 3–4, students share definitions.  
   - Each group writes 3 key words on a big sheet that they want to keep accessible all term (e.g., **body**, **risk**, **play**, **care**, **feedback**).

3. **Whole-class synthesis (5 min):**  
   - Groups tape their 3-word sheets to the wall.  
   - You briefly note overlaps, contradictions, and oddities.

Tell them: these words are not a rubric but a **compass** for the rest of the course.

---

### 65–90 min – Safety, tools, and lab culture

Do a calm, clear walkthrough:

- **Electrical safety:** low-voltage focus, no mains hacking, checking polarities.  
- **Hearing safety:** max levels, breaks, earplugs are welcome.  
- **Tool safety:** what tools exist, what training is needed, where to return things.  
- **Space norms:** cleaning up, labeling works-in-progress, how to store projects.

Invite questions. Encourage students to name fears, not just excitement (e.g., “I’m nervous around soldering irons”) so you can plan scaffolding.

If time and context allow, hand each student a **non-powered** tool (wire stripper, small screwdriver) and walk through basic use.

---

### 90–110 min – Instrument & Rig Journal

Introduce an ongoing **Instrument & Rig Journal** (can be a physical notebook or digital doc).

Prompt:

> Over the semester, you will document:
> - Instruments you encounter (in class and outside)
> - Rigs and setups you use or see others use
> - Your own builds as they evolve
>
> Think of this as a field notebook, not a polished archive.

In class, ask students to start with at least **two entries**:

1. An instrument from today’s listening or discussion.  
2. A familiar instrument or tool from their own life (phone + earbuds counts).

For each entry, have them jot:

- What are the main components?  
- What is the main gesture vocabulary?  
- What is one thing that feels accessible about this instrument?  
- What is one thing that feels exclusionary?

Have them keep these journals handy for later weeks (you’ll refer back to them).

---

### 110–120 min – Closing and homework brief

Closing round (very short):

- Ask each student to name one **instrument** they’d like to investigate or “take apart” conceptually this term (can be anything).

Then outline homework.

---

## After class / Homework

### 1. Instrument & Rig Journal (Week 1 entries)

- Add at least **3–5** new entries during the week.  
- Include at least:
  - One instrument you see in person (busker, friend’s rig, school gear).  
  - One purely digital or software-based instrument.  
  - One object that is not typically considered an instrument but could be.

For each: describe, sketch, or diagram what’s happening.

### 2. Short reading / listening

Assign 1–2 short texts (or a text + interview) from the reading list that introduce DIY instruments or noise practices. Ask for:

- 3 bullet-point reactions, and  
- One question they’d like to discuss in Week 2.
