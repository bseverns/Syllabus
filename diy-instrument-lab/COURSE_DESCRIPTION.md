# DIY Instrument Lab (College)

## Catalog description

In **DIY Instrument Lab**, students design and build their own musical instruments and sound-making systems. We treat instruments as **interfaces**, **sculptures**, and **political objects**: tools that shape who gets to make sound, how, and with what bodies.

We begin with listening and found-object sound, build contact microphones, and move through simple analog circuits (oscillators, mixers, crude filters) toward sensor- and microcontroller-based controllers that speak audio or MIDI. Along the way, we read and discuss how histories of noise, lutherie, access, and labor haunt contemporary instrument design.

The semester culminates in a playable instrument or instrument system presented in performance, installation, or participatory format, accompanied by documentation and a short critical text.

## Audience & level

- Advanced undergraduates and graduate students  
- Students from art, music, design, media, performance, engineering, etc.  
- Prior experience with *either* sound/music practice **or** basic electronics/coding is helpful but not required

The course is designed so students can enter from different strengths and learn from each other.

## Duration & format

- 14 weeks, 1 × 2–3 hour meeting per week  
- Studio + seminar hybrid:
  - Listening sessions and short talks  
  - Bench work (breadboards, soldering, enclosures)  
  - Sketch crits and in-progress playtesting  
  - Public or internal showing

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. **Analyze instruments as systems**: physical form, signal path, interface, and context.  
2. **Build and debug simple audio circuits** using breadboards and basic components.  
3. **Implement gestural control** with sensors and/or microcontrollers (e.g., Arduino, Teensy, micro:bit).  
4. **Design playable interfaces** that consider ergonomics, access, and embodied difference.  
5. **Situate their instruments critically**, relating them to histories of sound art, noise, and music technology.  
6. **Document and share** their work with diagrams, audio/video, and concise writing.
