# Choir Divider — Student Start Here

You do not need electronics or synthesizer experience. You will build with power off, ask for an inspection before power, and prove the circuit with a light before patching sound.

## What you are making

One repeating clock pulse advances a counter through outputs Q0, Q1, Q2, and onward. Choosing outputs and deciding when to reset turns counting into a rhythm.

## The safe build loop

1. Power off.
2. Name the node or pin you are adding.
3. Make one connection.
4. Ask your partner to check the pin number and orientation.
5. Ask for the required facilitator inspection.
6. Power and observe without moving parts.
7. Power off before changing anything.
8. Record what worked.

Call the facilitator and power down for heat, odor, unexpected voltage, a backward IC, uncertain rails, or behavior you cannot explain.

## Reset length reminder

- Reset from Q3: Q0–Q1–Q2 repeat = 3 visible steps.
- Reset from Q4: Q0–Q1–Q2–Q3 repeat = 4 visible steps.
- Reset from Q5: Q0–Q1–Q2–Q3–Q4 repeat = 5 visible steps.

## Evidence to keep

- completed worksheet and measured rail voltage;
- photograph or diagram of the working build;
- three named rhythmic outputs;
- one proven reset length;
- a 30–60 second recording or facilitator-observed patch;
- three to five sentences, a spoken note, or an annotated diagram explaining how counting became groove.
