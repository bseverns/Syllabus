# Debugging Studio — One-Page Overview

**Audience:** Secondary students  
**Length:** 8 sessions  
**Format:** 60 to 90 minutes per session  
**Platforms:** flexible across coding, robotics, media, or maker projects

## What students learn

Students learn that debugging is not just about fixing errors quickly. It is also about:

- describing a problem clearly
- testing ideas carefully
- learning from confusion
- working with peers productively
- noticing how emotion affects problem-solving

## What students make

By the end of the course, students build a small debugging portfolio that may include:

- a debugging log
- a bug report
- a revision comparison
- a peer coaching protocol
- a final debugging case study

## Why this course matters

Many students think debugging means either:

- "I am bad at this"
- "Someone smarter will fix it"
- "I should try random things until it works"

This course replaces those habits with a visible process.

## Core questions

- What counts as a bug?
- What do we do when a problem is open-ended?
- Are we trying to solve this now, prevent it later, or build future skill?
- How do frustration and confidence affect our next move?
- What does good debugging support sound like?

## Flexible by design

The course is written for secondary students, but the scaffolds are intentionally portable. Teachers can scale the same structure down for younger learners or up for older learners by adjusting:

- system complexity
- amount of abstraction
- length of writing
- independence expectations
