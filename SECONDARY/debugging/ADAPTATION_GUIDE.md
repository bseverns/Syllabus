# Adaptation Guide

This course is written for secondary students, but it is designed to travel.

## One level lower

Use this version for upper elementary or middle school by making these shifts:

- shorten sessions
- reduce the amount of abstract reflection
- use concrete, visible bugs in Scratch, robotics, or unplugged systems
- replace long written responses with sentence frames or drawing prompts
- keep emotional reflection short and specific

What to keep:

- debugging log
- bug report
- one-change-at-a-time testing
- peer help norms

What to simplify:

- refactoring debates
- academic vocabulary like "tradeoff" or "norms"
- long case-study analysis

## Secondary level

This current version assumes students can:

- describe a system in basic terms
- follow a multi-step test
- compare two possible strategies
- reflect in writing for a short paragraph

## One level higher

Translate this course upward for upper secondary, post-secondary, or teacher PD by making these shifts:

- use more complex systems and longer debugging cases
- assign readings on debugging, agency, efficiency, and classroom norms
- ask students to analyze transcripts or peer interaction
- deepen refactoring and design-tradeoff discussions
- require stronger written case studies with evidence

What to keep:

- debugging as technical plus human practice
- multiple valid goals
- emotion and support as part of the work

What to deepen:

- sociotechnical analysis
- equity and participation questions
- discipline-specific debugging methods

## Translation principle

Do not change the core idea when moving levels. Change:

- the complexity of the system
- the amount of abstraction
- the length of reflection
- the independence expected from students
