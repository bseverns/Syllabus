# Sensing & Gesture — Student Start Here

You do not need to be a dancer, performer, programmer, or electronics expert for this course.

A gesture can be:

- large or tiny;
- seated, standing, imagined, or performed by an object;
- drawn, described, sounded, or represented with recorded data;
- easy for one system to recognize and difficult for another.

You choose how your body participates. You will always have an alternative to touch, standing, camera recording, or public performance.

## The course's basic pipeline

1. **Gesture:** something changes or carries meaning.
2. **Sensing:** a person or device notices part of that change.
3. **Interpretation:** the system decides what the signal means.
4. **Response:** sound, image, light, text, movement, or another event occurs.
5. **Reflection:** we ask what was missed, assumed, or made possible.

## When the technology is unfamiliar

Start with four questions:

- What is the input?
- What can the sensor actually measure?
- What rule connects that measurement to the output?
- What should happen when the system is uncertain or wrong?

You may show learning through a diagram, mapping table, spoken walkthrough, paper prototype, recorded-data test, working sensor, performance score, or documented failure.

## Consent and privacy

- Ask before touching, recording, tracking, or sharing another person's movement.
- A person may change their mind after agreeing.
- Do not upload identifiable recordings to an unapproved service.
- Use sample or fabricated data when collecting embodied data is unnecessary.
- Tell the instructor if the available participation choices do not feel real or accessible.

## What to keep

Maintain one course folder with your gesture journal, scores, mapping tables, diagrams, tests, access/ethics notes, Prototype 0 evidence, final documentation, and individual reflection. A system that fails clearly can teach more than one that works without explanation.
