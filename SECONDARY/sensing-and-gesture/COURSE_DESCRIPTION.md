# Sensing & Gesture (College)

## Catalog description

This critical studio focuses on **gesture** and **sensing** as two sides of the same system. Students examine how bodies are tracked, interpreted, and disciplined through technology—from motion capture rigs and VR controllers to everyday phone sensors and CCTV. In weekly labs, we prototype gesture-driven interactions using cameras, microcontrollers, and simple wearables. In seminar, we read across media theory, disability studies, and HCI to question who gets sensed, how, and to what end.

The semester culminates in a gesture-driven installation, performance, or speculative prototype accompanied by robust documentation (diagrams, mapping tables, logs, and critical writing).

## Audience & level

- Advanced undergraduates and graduate students  
- Students from art, media, design, performance, HCI, architecture, etc.  
- Prior experience with *either* performance/movement *or* basic coding/electronics is helpful but not required  

## Duration & format

- 14 weeks, 1 × 2–3 hour meeting per week  
- Studio + seminar format:
  - Short lectures and reading discussions  
  - Embodied warm-ups and movement scores  
  - Hands-on prototyping with sensors and code  
  - Critiques and public or internal showings

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

1. **Analyze gesture as both embodied action and technical signal.**  
2. **Design and prototype gesture-based interactions** using at least one sensing modality (e.g., IMU, camera, capacitive touch).  
3. **Map gesture data to visual, sonic, or spatial responses** in clear, legible ways.  
4. **Articulate ethical, political, and accessibility stakes** of motion tracking and body-based sensing.  
5. **Plan, stage, and document** a gesture-driven work (installation, performance, or interactive system).
