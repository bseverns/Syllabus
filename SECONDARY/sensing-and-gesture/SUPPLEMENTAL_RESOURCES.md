# Supplemental Resources – Sensing & Gesture (College)

Pointers to tools, libraries, and examples you can plug into assignments.

---

## 1. Creative Coding & Prototyping Tools

### p5.js

Use p5.js for:

- Quick browser-based sketches of gesture mappings  
- Experiments with mouse/touch input as stand-ins for more complex sensing  
- Simple live visuals or sound triggers

### ml5.js (PoseNet or similar)

Use ml5.js (or another high-level JS library) for:

- Webcam-based pose estimation  
- Mapping skeleton keypoints (e.g., wrists, ankles, head) to audiovisual responses  
- Simple gesture classification experiments

---

## 2. Labanotation & Gesture Libraries

Use Labanotation and related resources to:

- Show how movement can be symbolically described and read back  
- Bridge between dance notation and robot/gesture libraries  
- Inspire students’ own “informal” notations for their sensing systems

Look for:

- Introductory Labanotation texts or online modules (e.g., LabanLab)  
- Examples of Labanotation used in robotics or gesture libraries

---

## 3. Case Studies in Gesture Interfaces

Examples to discuss and critique in class:

- User-defined gesture sets for touch or surface computing  
- Depth-camera systems that track bodies and gestures in space (Kinect, OmniTouch, etc.)  
- Everyday consumer tech: phone gestures, VR hand tracking, game controllers

Key questions:

- Who decides which gestures “count”?  
- What gestures are hard or impossible for some bodies?  
- What happens when the system is wrong or fails to see someone?

---

## 4. Community Performance and Disability Culture

Look to disability arts and community performance work (e.g., Petra Kuppers, disability culture collectives) to:

- Model alternative movement vocabularies and practices  
- Explore how performance can center care, access, and mutual aid  
- Ground final projects in real-world community practices rather than abstract UX concerns
