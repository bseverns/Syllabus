# Week 1 – What Is Gesture? What Is Sensing?

## At a glance

- Length: 2–3 hours  
- Mode: embodied warm-up + discussion + light making/writing  
- Big idea: Gesture is both embodied action *and* something systems try to measure. We’ll start by noticing that tension.

## Goals

By the end of Week 1, students should:

- Have a working, shared set of **definitions** for “gesture” and “sensing” (open to revision).  
- Experience at least one **embodied warm-up** that surfaces how they already “compute” movement.  
- Begin a **Gesture Journal** documenting everyday gestures.  
- See a few **technical and artistic precedents** where gesture is interpreted by systems.

This week is intentionally low-tech: it builds trust with movement and language before you add hardware.

## Suggested readings / media

- Short essay or article on everyday touch/gesture interfaces (tap, swipe, pinch, zoom).  
- A selection of short Fluxus event scores from the *Fluxus Performance Workbook*.

---

## Before class (instructor prep)

- **Space:**  
  - Reserve a room with enough open floor area that students can comfortably stand and move without bumping into each other.  
  - If possible, avoid fixed desks; or plan a quick desk rearrange at the start.

- **Prompts/materials:**  
  - Sticky notes or index cards + pens for quick definitions and lists.  
  - Large paper or whiteboard for shared definition mapping.  
  - Optional: music playback for warm-up (something with a clear beat, but not too directive).

- **Media (optional short slides):**  
  - 2–4 short clips/images of:
    - A gesture-driven interface (e.g., game controller, touchless UI, AR/VR demo).  
    - A performance or artwork where gesture is central (dance, sign language, experimental performance, etc.).  

- **Optional pre-class prompt for students (if you email ahead):**  
  > Between now and our first meeting, notice three everyday gestures you see in public (scrolling, waving, swiping a card, etc.). Jot down what you saw and where.

---

## In-class plan

### 0–15 min – Arrival & space reset

- Quickly rearrange the room (if needed) to make:
  - A circle or semi-circle of chairs with open space in the middle.  
- Ask students to:
  - Drop bags to the side.
  - Be ready for some light physical warm-up (no extreme athleticism, with alternatives offered).

You can frame this as: “We’ll be working with bodies in space; let’s set the room up to respect that.”

---

### 15–35 min – Round of introductions + first gestures

**Activity: Name + micro-gesture**

- Go around the circle. Each person offers:
  - Name, discipline/background, what brought them here.  
  - A very small gesture that “fits” how they feel today (e.g., a tiny shrug, a hand flick, a stretch).
- After each person’s gesture, the group loosely mirrors it.

**Instructor prompts:**

- “What changed when we copied someone else’s gesture?”  
- “Which gestures felt ‘natural’ vs. ‘performed’?”

Set tone: no one is being graded on grace or athleticism; we care about noticing, not virtuosity.

---

### 35–55 min – Quick-write: what is gesture? what is sensing?

Hand out sticky notes / index cards.

1. **Individually (4–5 min):**  
   - Card A: *“Gesture is…”* (write a sentence or cluster of words).  
   - Card B: *“Sensing is…”* (same deal).  

2. **Pair share (5–10 min):**  
   - Students compare definitions with a partner.  
   - Each pair chooses one word/phrase from each card they want to bring back.

3. **Whole group synthesis (10 min):**  
   - On the board, create two columns: “gesture” and “sensing”.  
   - Ask pairs to place or read out their chosen words.  
   - Notice overlaps and contradictions.

You don’t need to force a single definition; emphasize that these will evolve and that ambiguity is a resource.

---

### 55–80 min – Embodied warm-up: everyday gesture lab

**Framing:**  
“We’re surrounded by designed gestures: tap, scroll, pinch, swipe, wave for the camera. Let’s make some of that visible.”

1. **Silent observation (5 min):**  
   - Ask students to walk around the room slowly and:
     - Notice how they hold a phone, adjust glasses, shift weight, fidget.
     - Notice others’ micro-movements (without staring anyone down).
   - Call them back into a circle.

2. **Group listing (5–10 min):**  
   - On the board, list “everyday tech gestures” they’ve observed (scrolling, typing, pinching to zoom, swiping, double-tap, etc.).  
   - Also list **non-tech gestures** (shrugs, nods, waves, fidgets).

3. **Mini-score (10–15 min):**  
   - In pairs or trios, students choose 3–5 gestures from the lists.  
   - They compose a tiny **gesture score**: a 10–20 second sequence using only those gestures, repeated 2–3 times.  
   - Have 3–4 groups volunteer to perform their score for the class.

4. **Quick discussion (5–10 min):**  
   - What changed when those gestures were taken out of context and put on display?  
   - Did anything start to look “machine-readable” (binary, repetitive, patterned)?

You can name-check the idea that many current sensing systems *want* clean, repeatable gestures.

---

### 80–100 min – Mini lecture: sensing & gesture in systems

Short, informal talk with a few visuals (10–15 min) + some Q&A.

- Show 2–4 examples:
  - A phone or game controller gesture (swipe, wave, tilt).  
  - A motion-capture rig or VR tracking system.  
  - An artwork or performance using gestural data (even just stills / short clips).
- For each example, ask:
  - “What counts as a gesture in this system?”  
  - “What is ignored?”  
  - “What happens when the system is wrong or doesn’t see someone?”

Introduce very light vocabulary:

- **Input / sensor / signal** – what the system captures.  
- **Interpretation** – how the system decides “you just did X.”  
- **Output / response** – what changes as a result.

Make clear: *we will build small things that do this too*—and we’ll also critique them.

---

### 100–115 min – Start of the Gesture Journal

This becomes a recurring artifact.

**Prompt:**

> Start a **Gesture Journal**: a notebook or digital doc where you log gestures over the term.  
> For now, capture:
> - 2–3 gestures you’ve used today that feel “designed” (e.g., interface gestures, classroom rituals).  
> - 2–3 gestures that feel like they belong more to you than to a system (personal tics, rituals, ways you greet someone).

In class (10–15 min):

- Ask them to sketch / describe at least **four** gestures:
  - Name it (your own label).  
  - Describe when it occurs.  
  - Note: does any device or system “care” about this gesture right now?

Let them know they’ll be returning to this journal in later weeks (for mapping and for proposals).

---

### 115–120 min – Closing round & homework briefing

Quick closing circle; each person answers one of:

- “Name one gesture you’re now more aware of.”  
- “Name one system that watches your gestures.”

Then outline homework clearly.

---

## After class / Homework

### 1. Gesture Journal (Week 1 entry)

- Add at least **5–10 new entries** over the week.  
- For each entry:
  - Short description + rough sketch.  
  - Where/when it happens (context).  
  - Whether any system is *already* sensing it (phone, camera, door sensor, etc.).  
  - How you’d feel if it *were* sensed and logged all the time.

These can be messy; they’re working notes, not polished drawings.

### 2. Short reading

Assign the short reading on everyday interfaces plus the Fluxus scores.  
Ask for:

- 3–5 margin notes or bullet-point reactions to bring to Week 2.

Optional if your schedule is tight: they can instead bring one example (video/link) of a gesture-based interface or performance that they find interesting or troubling.

---

## Local notes (for you)

Use this space when you run the class:

- How comfortable was this cohort with movement?  
  - Did you need to offer more seated / micro-gesture alternatives?  
- Did anyone express discomfort with being observed / mirrored? How did you respond?  
- Did the room you used work for both discussion and movement?  
  - If not, note what you’d adjust next time (different space, more time for rearranging furniture, etc.).  
- Any student comments you want to remember as seeds for later weeks?
