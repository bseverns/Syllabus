# Sensing & Gesture — Facilitator Launch Guide

Use this guide with `SCHEDULE.md`, `INSTRUCTOR_NOTES.md`, and the 14 session files. It turns the course's flexible technology tiers into a concrete launch decision and makes a low-tech path visible from the first day.

## Choose the course tier before enrollment

| Tier | What every student can use | Local preflight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 0 — embodied/analog | Paper scores, drawing, objects, audio description, performance or observation | Accessible room, consent routines, documentation/storage plan |
| Tier 1 — lightweight sensing | Tier 0 plus shared IMU, capacitive-touch, flex, or webcam stations | Devices, libraries, sensor kits, cables, sample files, privacy settings |
| Tier 2 — camera/pose pipeline | Tier 0–1 plus pose estimation or depth camera work | Compatible hardware, current software environment, local processing/privacy plan, technical support |

The full 14-week learning arc can run at Tier 0–1. Do not advertise Tier 2 unless the exact lab image and camera pipeline work on student hardware. Students may complete a critical or speculative final project without producing a reliable machine-learning classifier.

## Minimum materials for 16 students

Every tier:

- 16 sketchbooks or folders, pencils, markers, index cards, tape, and large paper;
- string, fabric, simple props, and objects with varied weight/texture;
- printed gesture-score, mapping-table, system-diagram, consent, critique, and reflection sheets;
- projector or large display; speaker if sound response is used;
- private course storage for diagrams, logs, and consent-sensitive documentation;
- floor tape and movable seating for accessible work zones.

Tier 1 adds, per team of 3–4:

- one tested computer;
- one microcontroller/sensor kit or one locally processed webcam station;
- known-good cables, power, spare sensors, and a paper/data replay fallback;
- approved creative-response software for sound, image, text, or light.

Tier 2 adds only the hardware and libraries already verified in the local lab. Keep one frozen known-good demo and a recorded dataset for every live sensing station.

## Before Week 1

- Publish the chosen tier, software, account requirements, and alternatives.
- Test open, save, reopen, export, camera permission, and sensor connection on a student machine.
- Decide where recordings and embodied data live, who can view them, and when they are deleted.
- Prepare consent choices: participate without being recorded, observe, describe, manipulate an object, draw a score, or work from recorded/sample data.
- Mark a quiet zone and clear paths; check seating, reach, lighting, and sound needs.
- Prepare one successful demo, one intentionally ambiguous demo, and one no-device version.
- Decide whether the final showing is public, internal, or documentation-only.

## Default 150-minute session rhythm

| Time | Move |
| --- | --- |
| 0–15 min | Arrival, consent/access check, and low-intensity warm-up or observation |
| 15–35 min | Framing, reading discussion, or demonstration |
| 35–65 min | Study 1: movement, drawing, or sensing experiment |
| 65–75 min | Break and room reset |
| 75–115 min | Study 2: mapping, prototype, or critique work |
| 115–135 min | Test with an opt-in participant or recorded/sample input |
| 135–150 min | Save evidence, access/ethics note, cleanup, and next step |

For a two-hour section, shorten both studies. Preserve the break, documentation, and cleanup.

## Plain-language opening

Say this in Week 1:

> You do not need to be a dancer, performer, programmer, or electronics expert. A gesture can be large, tiny, imagined, drawn, spoken, performed by an object, or represented in recorded data. You choose how your body participates. Our job is to notice what a sensing system assumes, test how it responds, and make those choices visible.

Shared words:

- **gesture:** an action or change that carries meaning in a context;
- **sensor:** something that measures a change;
- **signal/data:** a representation of what was measured;
- **feature:** the part of a signal a system pays attention to;
- **threshold:** a value where the system changes its response;
- **mapping:** the rule connecting an input to an output;
- **misrecognition:** when a person/system interprets a gesture differently than intended;
- **opt out:** a real participation choice without academic penalty.

## Participation and access routes

- Never require touching, standing, eye contact, camera exposure, or high physical intensity.
- Treat describing, observing, drawing, operating, logging, composing, and facilitating consent as substantive roles.
- Let students use micro-gestures, seated gestures, assistive devices, objects, voice, or prerecorded/sample data.
- Describe visual demonstrations aloud; caption or transcribe recorded material; make diagrams readable without color alone.
- Grade the clarity of inquiry, mapping, evidence, and reflection—not bodily range, sensor accuracy, technical spectacle, or willingness to be recorded.

## Common-stuck and recovery guide

| What happens | Keep the inquiry moving |
| --- | --- |
| Camera permission or library fails | Use recorded/sample coordinates, hand-entered values, paper frames, or an observer-generated event stream |
| Sensor values jump or disappear | Log the valid range, add a clear status indicator, replay a short capture, and discuss dropout behavior |
| A gesture feels inaccessible or exposing | Change scale, body part, role, modality, or use an object/sample dataset without requiring an explanation |
| Team chases recognition accuracy | Return to the conceptual question: what counts, who is missed, and what should happen on uncertainty? |
| Mapping is arbitrary | Make a table: gesture feature, sensed value, response, intended meaning, possible misreading |
| One technical student takes over | Rotate `performer/input`, `operator`, `mapper`, and `documenter/access observer` roles |
| Public showing is inappropriate | Use a private studio crit, screen recording, diagram walk, or documentation-only final |

## Evidence and completion

Keep the gesture journal/score, a mapping table, Prototype 0 documentation, ethics/access note, system diagram, final project evidence, and individual reflection. A project may intentionally expose failure or misrecognition; completion depends on clear inquiry, evidence, responsible participation design, and reflection rather than seamless recognition.

After the course, record the actual tier used, kit failures, account/privacy friction, access routes selected, session timing, and what must change before the next delivery.
