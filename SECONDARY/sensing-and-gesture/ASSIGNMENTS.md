# Sensing & Gesture (College) – Assignments & Evaluation

Suggested weighting (tune to your program):

- Participation & seminar prep – ~20%  
- Short studies & gesture scores – ~20%  
- Prototype 0 + midterm documentation – ~20%  
- Final project (gesture-driven work) – ~25%  
- Final documentation bundle + individual reflection – ~15%  

---

## 1. Participation & Seminar Prep (~20%)

Ongoing across Weeks 1–14.

Includes:

- Embodied warm-ups and movement scores  
- Reading/viewing preparation and discussion  
- In-progress shares and crit participation  
- Peer testing and feedback in lab

You can define a simple rubric (e.g., “prepared, engaged, respectful, generous”) and share it in your institutional LMS or syllabus.

---

## 2. Short Studies & Gesture Scores (~20%)

These small, weekly-ish artifacts scaffold toward the final project.

**Examples (edit to match your run):**

- **Week 1–2 – Gesture Journal & Score v1**  
  - Short written + drawn documentation of 2–3 everyday gestures.  
  - One experimental score for a simple movement piece (can be text-only).

- **Week 3 – Body-as-Sensor Reflection**  
  - 1–2 page reflection on how they already “sense” balance, strain, and space.  
  - Prompt them to note where normative assumptions about bodies show up.

- **Week 4–5 – Sensor & Camera Study**  
  - Quick prototype that maps one simple gesture to a visible or audible response.  
  - Short write-up: what gesture did you choose, and why? What failed?

- **Week 6 – Mapping Table v1 (Gesture → System Response)**  
  - First stab at mapping specific gestures (or gesture features) to system behaviors.  
  - Can be symbolic (tap/hold/sweep) or numeric (ranges, thresholds).

- **Week 7 – Ethics & Access Note v1 (Gesture Edition)**  
  - Draft statement about whose bodies are invited, what is being sensed, and how consent/opt-out works in their system.

- **Week 9 – Systems Packet (Mini)**  
  - System diagram sketch, updated mapping table, and early assumption/risk list.

You can bump any of these up/down or combine them, depending on cohort and workload.

---

## 3. Prototype 0 + Midterm Documentation (~20%)

**Due Week 8 – Prototype 0 Crit**

A small gesture-driven system that:

- Recognizes at least one gesture reliably (or intentionally fails in an interesting way).  
- Produces a clear response (visual, sonic, spatial, textual, etc.).

Deliverables:

1. **Prototype 0 demo**  
   - Live in-class demo of the system with a short walkthrough of what you tried.

2. **Prototype 0 Brief (2–3 pages)**  
   - Concept and context: what are you exploring?  
   - Diagram(s): gesture → sensing → interpretation → response.  
   - Observations: what worked, what broke, who might be excluded?  
   - Next steps: what you want to keep, change, or throw away.

---

## 4. Final Project: Gesture-Driven Work (~25%)

**Spans Weeks 10–13**

Group or solo project that could be:

- A small installation that reacts to visitors’ gestures.  
- A performance piece where tech plays the role of observer/interpreter.  
- A speculative prototype that exposes or critiques existing gesture recognition systems.

Core expectations:

- A clear **gesture vocabulary** (what “counts” as input).  
- A coherent **mapping** from gesture to response.  
- Thoughtful attention to **ethics, access, and misrecognition**.  
- Some form of **choreography or interaction path** for visitors/performers.  
- A plan for **documentation** (photo, video, diagrams, text).

You can choose whether these are solo or collaborative, and set approximate size/complexity based on your institutional context.

---

## 5. Final Documentation Bundle + Individual Reflection (~15%)

### Group / Project bundle (~10%)

A shared submission that includes:

- Title & abstract  
- Concept narrative (what the work is doing and why)  
- Gesture vocabulary list and final mapping table  
- System diagram (final)  
- Ethics & Access note (final)  
- Event/performance log (what happened at the showing)  
- Documentation media (stills, short video, audio if relevant)

### Individual reflection (~5%)

Each student also submits a short reflection covering:

- Their role(s) in the project  
- What they learned about sensing and gesture (technically and conceptually)  
- How readings / crit shaped their decisions  
- What they would do differently in a v2 or in a different context

You can align these with your program’s expectations for “writing in the major” or capstone reflections.
