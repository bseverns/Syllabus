# Course Overview

## Program title
**AI in Your Feed: Create, Explore, Protect**

## Audience
High school students

## Format
Weekly 90-minute studio sessions

## Course goal
Empower students to use AI creatively and responsibly while understanding bias, privacy, safety, and practical uses in daily life.

## Why this course matters

AI is already shaping how young people encounter music, news, images, recommendations, advertising, search results, school tools, and public narratives. Many students are using AI before they have had a real chance to ask what it is doing, what it gets wrong, what it collects, or how to push back.

This course offers a different posture:
- creative use without blind trust
- experimentation without oversharing
- excitement without hype
- critique without fear
- practical literacy without gatekeeping

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students will be able to:
1. identify everyday places where AI influences attention and decision-making
2. use AI-supported tools to make a small creative work
3. revise prompts and outputs with intention
4. spot common forms of bias, hallucination, flattening, and misinformation
5. apply basic privacy and safety practices when using AI systems
6. explain, in their own words, how to use AI with more care and control

## Final project options

Students may create one of the following:
- a mini game or interactive scene
- a short visual series or poster set
- a beat, sound collage, or AI-assisted audio sketch
- a short story, zine spread, or spoken-word support piece
- a community-facing informational artifact such as a bias-check poster, privacy mini-zine, or “how to verify AI” guide

## Local framing for North Minneapolis

This course should honor local intelligence.

Useful prompts:
- How do recommendation systems shape taste, trends, and visibility?
- What stories about a neighborhood get amplified, and what gets erased?
- What does it mean when a tool makes people or communities look generic?
- What happens when AI is trained on the internet’s stereotypes?
- How can young people make with these tools without being flattened by them?

## What success looks like

Success is not “students become AI believers.”

Success is:
- they become sharper readers of systems
- they protect themselves better
- they make something that feels like theirs
- they can explain where a tool helped and where it failed
- they leave with more agency than they arrived with
