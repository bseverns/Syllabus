# AI in Your Feed: Create, Explore, Protect

A 4-session, high-school studio course built for weekly 90-minute meetings.

This course treats AI as something students already live with, not something arriving from the future. It meets young people where they are: in feeds, search, music discovery, images, games, school tools, and everyday decisions about what to trust, what to share, and what to make.

The course is designed for a **North Minneapolis context**:
- asset-based, not deficit-based
- grounded in local life, local culture, and local questions
- attentive to bias, surveillance, privacy, and misrepresentation
- oriented toward creation, critique, and self-protection
- flexible enough for mixed technical experience

## Core promise

Students leave with:
1. a clearer sense of where AI already shapes their lives
2. hands-on experience using AI creatively
3. strategies for checking bias, errors, and misinformation
4. stronger habits around privacy, safety, and consent
5. a small finished project and a student-friendly survival guide

## Course shape

- Audience: High school students
- Format: Weekly 90-minute studio sessions
- Length: 4 weeks
- Final output: a small AI-supported creative project
- Tone: studio, workshop, discussion, reflection

## North Minneapolis alignment

This course should feel relevant without asking students to perform their lives for adults.

That means:
- no one is required to “represent the neighborhood”
- local examples can include music scenes, fashion, entrepreneurship, sports, transit, mutual aid, school life, gaming, family businesses, and public storytelling
- students can work from personal interests, community questions, or pure imagination
- privacy and dignity matter more than novelty

The course is strongest when it helps students ask:
- Why am I seeing this?
- Who benefits from this system?
- What gets left out or distorted?
- What should I never upload?
- How can I use this tool without letting it use me?

- `COMMUNITY/` — family-facing description and implementation notes

## Recommended facilitation stance

Lead the course as a studio, not a lecture series.

Students should feel:
- trusted
- protected
- challenged
- capable of making strong work
- allowed to disagree with the tool
- allowed to refuse the frame a tool gives them

## Practical notes

This course can run:
- with web-based AI tools
- with school-approved accounts
- with a mixed stack that includes non-AI tools such as Scratch, p5.js, or audio editors
- with more discussion and less account creation if privacy constraints are tight

When in doubt, choose:
- fewer tools
- more reflection
- stronger consent
- clearer boundaries
- better documentation
