# Session 4 — Privacy, Safety, and Showoff

## Theme
Protect and present

## Session purpose
Students finish their projects, learn core privacy habits, and present what they made. The ending note should be confidence with caution: creative possibility tied to self-protection and better judgment.

## Learning objectives
Students will:
- understand key privacy and digital safety practices for AI tools
- finalize and present a small project
- reflect on what they learned about authorship, bias, and care
- leave with a practical guide for future use

## Essential question
How can I keep making with AI without giving away too much or trusting too much?

## Materials
- projector
- laptops or desktops
- printed `HANDOUTS/AI_Survival_and_Creation_Guide.md`
- final reflection prompts
- optional display space for posters or screenshots

## Prep
- decide on showcase order
- confirm export options from tools
- prepare 5–7 minute privacy mini-lesson
- open the privacy checklist in the [Session 1–4 visual pack](../assets/session01-04/README.md)
- print handouts for all students

## Agenda

### 1. Privacy mini-lesson — 20 min
Cover:
- what data tools may collect
- why logins matter
- safe sharing practices
- not uploading faces, personal info, school IDs, or private family details
- consent and other people’s images/voices
- the difference between public posting and classroom sharing

Useful board prompt:
**“Just because a tool can take your data does not mean it deserves it.”**

### 2. Final studio time — 50 min
Students finalize:
- mini games
- poster sets
- image series
- beats or sound sketches
- short story or dialogue scenes
- verification or survival guides

Facilitator support:
- help students trim scope
- help students export or capture final work
- help students prepare a 1-minute explanation

### 3. Show and tell — 15 min
Students present:
- what they made
- what AI helped with
- what they changed themselves
- one privacy or bias lesson they are taking with them

### 4. Reflection and next steps — 5 min
Closing prompt:
- What will you do differently the next time you use AI?

## Output
Students leave with:
- one finished or near-finished project
- one reflection
- one printed survival guide
- stronger language for protecting themselves online

## Optional celebration
End with a gallery walk, mini listening station, or rotating showcase.

## North Minneapolis note
Frame the showcase as a demonstration of authorship and discernment. Students are not being asked to prove that they love AI. They are showing that they can use it with vision, skepticism, and care.
