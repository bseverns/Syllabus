# Session 1 — Meet Your AI

## Theme
Introduction and exploration

## Session purpose
Students begin by naming where AI already lives in their world. The goal is not to impress them with technology, but to surface that the feed is already shaped, sorted, and nudged by systems they did not choose.

## Learning objectives
Students will:
- identify places where AI appears in everyday life
- begin experimenting with simple AI tools
- establish safety and community agreements
- reflect on first impressions, excitement, and concern

## Essential question
Where is AI already shaping what I see, hear, and do?

## Materials
- projector
- laptops or desktops
- handouts:
  - `HANDOUTS/Community_Agreements.md`
  - `HANDOUTS/AI_Survival_and_Creation_Guide.md`
- prompt log sheets or notebooks
- selected safe tools for text, image, and audio generation

## Prep
Before class:
- test all tools
- make sure no tool requires unnecessary personal data
- prepare 3 demo prompts: one image, one text, one sound/music
- print handouts
- have a low-tech backup in case accounts fail

## Agenda

### 1. Welcome and icebreaker — 15 min
Prompt:
**“Where have you seen AI today?”**

Possible examples:
- TikTok or Instagram recommendations
- YouTube autoplay
- Spotify playlists
- phone camera filters
- autocorrect
- search engines
- gaming suggestions
- school software

Facilitator move:
Write student examples on the board and cluster them into categories:
- entertainment
- school/work
- communication
- images/video
- shopping/ads

### 2. Community agreements and safety frame — 10 min
Review the agreements and privacy guide.

Say clearly:
- We are here to use tools without handing over our whole lives.
- We do not need to upload our faces, names, or private details to learn.
- We can enjoy a tool and still critique it.

### 3. Mini-demo — 20 min
Show:
- one AI image prompt
- one short text prompt
- one short sound/music example

Suggested discussion:
- What is impressive here?
- What feels off?
- What looks generic?
- What feels useful?
- What feels suspicious?

### 4. Hands-on exploration — 30 min
Students rotate through or choose one or two tools.

Suggested prompt directions:
- create an album cover
- generate a short scene or monologue
- make a soundtrack loop for a game or story
- create a visual for a fictional event or world

Ask students to log:
- the prompt
- what came back
- what they liked
- what they would change

### 5. Reflection and wrap-up — 15 min
Whole-group share:
- What surprised you?
- What annoyed you?
- What looked strong but felt empty?
- What would you be careful about next time?

## Output
Each student leaves with:
- at least one saved output or screenshot
- one prompt log entry
- one privacy or safety rule they can explain

## Homework / practice
Make one small AI-supported artifact:
- image
- short sound
- short text
- game concept

Bring it back next week with one sentence:
**What did the tool help with, and what did it mess up?**

## North Minneapolis note
Keep local references optional and open-ended. Invite students to work from neighborhood energy, style, rhythm, and public life without asking them to expose private stories.
