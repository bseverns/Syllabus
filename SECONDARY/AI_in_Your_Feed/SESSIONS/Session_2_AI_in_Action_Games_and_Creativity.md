# Session 2 — AI in Action: Games and Creativity

## Theme
Build and play

## Session purpose
Students move from dabbling to directing. They use AI as a creative partner while learning that good outputs usually come from revision, constraints, and stronger choices.

## Learning objectives
Students will:
- use AI tools to help build a simple interactive or creative artifact
- practice iterative prompt writing
- notice where AI helps and where it produces shallow results
- strengthen authorship over the process

## Essential question
How can I make something with AI without letting the tool make all the decisions?

## Materials
- laptops or desktops
- projector
- headphones if using sound tools
- `HANDOUTS/Prompt_Cards.md`
- optional platforms for assembly: Scratch, Twine, slides, p5.js, simple web tools

## Prep
Prepare one demo project that can be built quickly:
- branching story
- tiny text adventure
- visual scene with choices
- soundtrack plus title screen
- poster + short written lore

## Agenda

### 1. Check-in and share — 15 min
Students show the artifact they brought or describe what they tried.

Prompt:
- What part felt easy?
- What part took real decision-making?
- What part felt fake, flat, or over-polished?

### 2. Demo: building a small interactive piece — 20 min
Model how to move from vague prompt to usable output.

Example flow:
1. initial idea
2. first weak result
3. revision with more detail
4. reject cliché output
5. combine AI output with student edits
6. place it into a simple game/story structure

Name the principle:
**AI can generate material, but students still design the experience.**

### 3. Hands-on studio — 40 min
Students choose a project track:
- mini game
- interactive scene
- poster/story world
- beat plus cover art
- short narrative or dialogue sequence

Encourage students to:
- revise prompts at least 3 times
- change tone, setting, stakes, or audience
- save versions
- combine multiple tools if helpful

### 4. Reflection — 15 min
Prompt:
- What did you have to fix?
- What made your project feel more like yours?
- What did the AI keep trying to do that you did not want?

## Output
Students leave with a rough draft of a project they can expand.

## Homework / practice
Refine the project.
Be ready to explain:
- what AI generated
- what you changed
- what still needs work

## Extension idea
Have students make a “before revision / after revision” comparison to show how authorship emerged through editing.

## North Minneapolis note
Offer project prompts that connect to movement, music, style, public space, games, transit, sport, entrepreneurship, and neighborhood storytelling, but do not require any student to narrate their own life.
