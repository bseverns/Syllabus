# Facilitator Quickstart — Lo-Fi Beat Laboratory

Use this before Session 1. It fills the cataloged facilitation, assessment, and troubleshooting gap without replacing the eight session plans.

## 1. Choose one audio tool

Pick **one** tool for the whole course. Do not make students learn a new platform halfway through.

- [BandLab](https://www.bandlab.com/) — browser path.
- [Soundtrap](https://www.soundtrap.com/musicmakers) — browser path.
- [GarageBand](https://www.apple.com/mac/garageband/) — Apple-device path.
- [Audacity](https://www.audacityteam.org/download/) — local desktop path.

Before the course, test this exact sequence on a student device: record or import audio → trim → loop → duplicate → adjust volume → export or save the project.

## 2. Stage the course once

- Make one shared class folder or LMS turn-in location; use short, non-personal file names such as `teamname_loop_v01`.
- Create three facilitator-owned fallback clips: one short sound, one longer recording with clear events, and one loop. Do not depend on a copyrighted sample pack or a student recording for the demo.
- Test headphones, audio permissions, import, playback, and export on the actual devices.
- Put the [`STUDENT_PROJECT_CARD.md`](STUDENT_PROJECT_CARD.md) at every station before Session 1.

## 3. Keep recording safe

- Record sounds, not private conversations or identifiable people.
- Use only instructor-approved locations. Never record in restrooms, locker rooms, private offices, or spaces with people who have not agreed.
- Keep field-recording groups together and set a return time.
- Let students use the facilitator clips or make a tabletop sound when recording outside the room is not appropriate.

## 4. What to collect each session

| Session | Student finishes with | Facilitator check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3–5 short sounds or a sound-hunt plan | Files are named or the plan is written. |
| 2 | 4–8 bar loop | One saved project or audio export. |
| 3 | Chopped-loop version | At least four slices rearranged. |
| 4 | Noise beat | Low, mid, and high sound roles are audible. |
| 5 | Dry + roomed comparison | Approved location and a short note about the difference. |
| 6 | 30–60 second draft | Intro, change, and ending are present. |
| 7 | Final mix + cover | Key sounds are audible; title and cover are attached. |
| 8 | Share + reflection | Final file/project and three written reflection sentences. |

## 5. Recover without stopping the course

- **Student has no recordings:** use the three facilitator clips; the student still chops, loops, and arranges them.
- **Audio tool is down:** students make a paper timeline with sound names, time marks, and one planned change; demonstrate the idea with the facilitator project when it returns.
- **No safe room recording:** play a loop softly at the approved table, record it there, and compare it with the dry version.
- **Export fails:** save the project, take a screenshot of the timeline, and collect the title/cover/reflection as the evidence.

## 6. Final evidence

A complete student project has a 30–60 second piece, a title, simple cover art, and a reflection that names one found sound, one change after listening, and one thing the room did to the audio. Keep the course `PILOT` until a real run confirms timing, tool reliability, access needs, and the recovery paths above.
