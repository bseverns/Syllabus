# Course Overview

## Title
**AI at Work: Tools, Judgment, and Professional Practice**

## Format
- Audience: High school students preparing for professional settings
- Partner context: Genesys Works / GenSys Works-aligned workforce readiness
- Duration: 6 weeks
- Meetings: 4 days per week
- Contact hours: 48 total hours
- Session length: 2 hours

## Course description

This course helps students build a grounded, professional understanding of how AI tools work and how they can be used responsibly in real-world workplace settings. Students begin by learning the foundations: digital systems, files and folders, prompting, verification, privacy, bias, and professional judgment. They then move into workplace simulations and practicum tasks such as drafting emails, summarizing research, organizing notes, rewriting for different audiences, and building repeatable workflows. By the end of the course, each student leaves with a personal toolkit of prompts, templates, checklists, and a documented AI-supported workflow they can use in future classes, internships, and jobs.

## Big goals

By the end of the course, students will be able to:
1. Explain in plain language how AI tools differ from traditional software and search tools.
2. Use AI to support workplace-style tasks without treating outputs as automatically correct.
3. Verify, revise, and improve AI-generated drafts using source material and judgment.
4. Protect privacy and recognize professional, ethical, and legal boundaries in AI use.
5. Build and maintain a personal document system for future work and learning.
6. Demonstrate one complete, repeatable AI-supported professional workflow.

## Core course question

**How can I use AI as a real support tool at work without giving up my judgment, my voice, or my responsibility?**

## End-of-course student deliverables

Each student should leave with:
- a professional digital folder structure
- a resume or brag-sheet starter
- a prompt notebook
- a verification checklist
- an email and meeting-notes template set
- a research summary template
- a personal AI use policy
- a capstone workflow and presentation

## Pedagogical notes

This course is designed for students who may be entering workplaces where expectations are often hidden or assumed. It makes the invisible visible:
- how to ask for help professionally
- how to name files and save versions
- how to document your work
- how to check a draft before sending it
- how to shift tone for different audiences
- how to work across home language, school language, and workplace language with dignity

## Instructional stance

The course is:
- workforce-facing
- systems-based
- practical
- supportive
- explicit about hidden rules
- alert to privacy and bias
- oriented toward reuse, not one-off assignments
