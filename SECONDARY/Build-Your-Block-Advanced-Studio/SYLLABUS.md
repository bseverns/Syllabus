# Build Your Block: Advanced Studio — Pilot Syllabus

## Workshop contract

- **Audience:** Grades 6–8; 12–18 learners in teams of 3–4
- **Schedule:** 28 sessions × 105 minutes (49 contact hours)
- **Staffing:** one lead; add support for machine work or groups above 15
- **Screen/equipment:** `S2–S3`; `E1` minimum, `E3` with SPIKE or fabrication
- **Final share:** community research, design brief, digital model, physical interactive prototype, user-test record, public demonstration, and archive

## Before learners arrive

Choose one bounded site theme such as waiting, wayfinding, welcoming, sharing, or gathering. Confirm which people may provide feedback and obtain permission before interviews, photography, or recording. Select and test exactly one interaction platform and one fabrication pathway for the site. Inventory materials, stage known-good examples, and prepare labeled team project-parking space. Machines are facilitator-operated unless separately approved local procedures say otherwise.

## Materials

Cardboard, paper, tape, markers, scissors, reusable fasteners, found materials, rulers, sticky notes, one site-selected interaction kit per team, shared CAD devices, materials for one approved fabrication route, documentation paper, storage bins, and a camera only where consent permits.

## Repeatable rhythm

Each 105-minute session uses: 10 minutes encounter and prediction; 15 minutes demonstration/planning; 60 minutes make/test studio; 10 minutes evidence and revision; 10 minutes reset and project parking.

## 28-session sequence

### Week 1 — Community and audience research

**Session 1 — Encounter the system.** Tour or study a bounded public condition. Separate direct observation from assumptions and create a shared question wall.

**Session 2 — Map people and movement.** Build an anonymized journey map showing actions, pauses, barriers, and existing supports. Do not collect identifying data.

**Session 3 — Practice consent-based inquiry.** Rehearse neutral questions and refusal. Conduct only approved interviews or use facilitator-supplied scenarios.

**Session 4 — Frame an opportunity.** Each team chooses one audience and one small condition, cites its evidence, and archives rejected problem statements.

### Week 2 — Interaction systems and design specification

**Session 5 — Inputs, rules, outputs.** Analyze familiar systems and diagram how action becomes feedback. Build one unplugged input → rule → output model.

**Session 6 — Platform baseline.** Verify the site-selected interaction platform with a known-good example. Record ports, versions, wiring, and recovery steps.

**Session 7 — Accessible interaction studies.** Prototype two ways to sense or invite the same action, including a route that does not depend on sight, hearing, speech, or standing.

**Session 8 — Write the design brief.** Name audience, desired change, constraints, safety/access boundaries, success evidence, selected platform, and non-goals.

### Week 3 — Digital modeling and low-fidelity prototypes

**Session 9 — Scale and measurement.** Measure relevant bodies, objects, and site constraints without recording identities. Build a dimensioned paper layout.

**Session 10 — Digital model baseline.** Reproduce a known-good CAD example, export the required file type, and document units and version.

**Session 11 — Model the physical interface.** Create a simple digital model with readable dimensions and assembly intent; keep decoration out of scope.

**Session 12 — Paper interaction test.** Test a full-size cardboard or role-play interface before fabrication. Record where another team hesitates or misreads it.

### Week 4 — Functional prototypes

**Session 13 — Build one behavior.** Implement the minimum reliable input → rule → output behavior and preserve a known-good copy.

**Session 14 — Add physical feedback.** Integrate one light, sound, motion, or tactile response. Verify that feedback is perceivable without creating a new access barrier.

**Session 15 — Enclosure and mounting study.** Mock up the body, cable path, service access, reach, stability, and reset method with low-cost materials.

**Session 16 — Midpoint critique.** Demonstrate audience, evidence, design brief, digital model, and functional core. Choose one consequential revision and cut unsupported features.

### Week 5 — Fabrication and integration

**Session 17 — Fabrication preflight.** Review the approved machine workflow, material limits, queue, and instructor-only boundaries. Submit a small fit or process test.

**Session 18 — Produce bounded parts.** Facilitator runs approved jobs while teams prepare assemblies, labels, code, and manual fallback components.

**Session 19 — Assemble the prototype.** Integrate fabricated and low-tech parts. Inspect edges, fasteners, pinch points, strain relief, and access to power/reset controls.

**Session 20 — Integration recovery.** Reproduce failures one at a time, compare against the known-good core, and either repair or simplify to a stable prototype.

### Week 6 — Accessibility, reliability, and user testing

**Session 21 — Accessibility review.** Test reach, contrast, language, volume, force, timing, and alternate participation routes with an explicit checklist.

**Session 22 — Reliability protocol.** Run at least ten start/action/reset cycles, logging failures by condition rather than guessing causes.

**Session 23 — Consent-based user test.** Invite an approved participant to complete one short task without coaching. Collect only necessary, permitted evidence.

**Session 24 — Evidence-led revision.** Compare test evidence with the success condition, change one feature, and repeat the same task or reliability protocol.

### Week 7 — Public demonstration and archive

**Session 25 — Installation plan.** Draw placement, power, supervision, traffic flow, accessibility, reset, emergency stop, and removal. Site approval is required before any public setup.

**Session 26 — Rehearse and stress-test.** Run the full demonstration with peer visitors, a deliberate reset, and a low-tech fallback. Remove any unreliable feature.

**Session 27 — Public demonstration.** Share only in the approved site and format. Teams explain audience, evidence, interaction, revision, limit, and next test; participation and recording remain optional.

**Session 28 — Archive and handoff.** Restore equipment, save approved files, photograph only permitted artifacts, complete the maintenance/reset note, and record the post-delivery review.

## Safety and access

No learner conducts unsupervised fieldwork or records identifiable people without explicit permission. Provide seated and nonverbal roles. Keep walkways and exits clear; contain moving mechanisms; stop on heat, odor, damaged power parts, or uncertain machine state. Facilitators operate printers/CNC equipment. A cardboard role-play is a complete fallback when devices, kits, or fabrication are unavailable.

## Observable assessment

A team can: name the audience and evidence; explain input, rule, and output; show one controlled test; identify a change made from feedback; state a current limit; and leave a resettable, documented prototype.

## Post-delivery review

Record actual timing, kit/material quantities, access adaptations, technical failures, test-participant permissions, and the revision needed before another run. This evidence is required before readiness promotion.
