# 10th Grade – Intro to Physical Computing with micro:bit  
## Assignments & Grading Overview

### Course Summary

Students learn how a small computer (micro:bit) senses the world and responds. They build simple programs using buttons and motion, then create a cardboard “controller” shell around the micro:bit and debug both code and physical design.

### Total Points (Suggested)

- Weekly build/experiment assignments (Weeks 1–5): **5 × 10 pts = 50**
- Weekly short-answer questions / exit tickets (Weeks 1–5): **5 × 5 pts = 25**
- Final controller + program submission (Week 6): **20 pts**
- Final reflection (Week 6): **5 pts**

**Total:** 100 points for the unit

---

## Assignment List

### Week 1

**Mood Face – First micro:bit Program** – 10 pts  
Create a MakeCode program where the micro:bit shows a starting icon, a happy face on button A, and a different face on button B. Turn in a screenshot/photo of the code and a sentence describing button presses as inputs and LED faces as outputs.

**Vocab Check: Input & Output** – 5 pts  
Short answer: define input and output in your own words and give one example of each from your MoodFace program.

---

### Week 2

**Gesture Tester – Shake & Tilt** – 10 pts  
Write or modify a program that reacts differently when you shake the micro:bit or tilt it left/right. Turn in a screenshot/photo of your code and a short explanation of how the accelerometer (motion sensor) is used.

**Vocab Check: Sensor** – 5 pts  
Short answer: explain what a sensor is, using the micro:bit as an example.

---

### Week 3

**Reaction Timer – Build or Modify** – 10 pts  
Plan a simple reaction game as a step-by-step algorithm (on paper or in a doc), then implement or modify it in MakeCode. The game waits a random time, shows a signal, and measures how fast the player presses a button. Turn in a copy/photo of your algorithm plus a screenshot/photo of your code and a brief comparison between the two.

**Vocab Check: Algorithm** – 5 pts  
Short answer: define “algorithm” in your own words and explain how you used one in your reaction game.

---

### Week 4

**Controller Prototype – Cardboard Build** – 10 pts  
Sketch and build a first cardboard housing for your micro:bit that can be held like a small controller. The LEDs should be visible, and the buttons reachable. Turn in a photo of the prototype with the micro:bit in it, plus bullet points about what works and what feels awkward.

**Ergonomics Quick Check** – 5 pts  
Short answer: describe how it feels to hold your controller for a full minute and where (if anywhere) you feel strain or discomfort.

---

### Week 5

**Debug & Refine – Make It Reliable** – 10 pts  
Use a debugging checklist to test both your code and your physical design. Fix at least one bug in your program and/or one issue in your housing (labels, taped joints, button access). Turn in a short written description of what was wrong, what you changed, and whether it fixed the problem.

**Vocab Check: Bug & Debugging** – 5 pts  
Short answer: define “bug” and “debugging” in your own words, with one example from your project.

---

### Week 6

**Final Controller & Program Submission** – 20 pts  
Turn in your final micro:bit project and physical housing. It should:

- Respond to at least one **input** (button, shake, or tilt)
- Show at least one **output** (LED images, patterns, or numbers)
- Be mounted in some sort of **physical housing** that can be held and used

Students submit a clear photo of the controller in use, a screenshot/photo of their code, and a short “device card” (name, what the user does, what the micro:bit does).

**Course Reflection: Talking Like a Tech** – 5 pts  
4–6 sentence reflection using vocabulary from the course (input, output, sensor, algorithm, bug, debugging), what feels less mysterious now, and what they’d like to try if they had more time.
