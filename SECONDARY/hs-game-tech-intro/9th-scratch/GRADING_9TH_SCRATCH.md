# 9th Grade – Intro to Games & Code with Scratch  
## Assignments & Grading Overview

### Course Summary

Students use Scratch to peek inside simple games, change how they behave, and learn basic vocabulary for talking about code and game design. Emphasis is on **tinkering, understanding, and naming what’s happening**, not building large projects from scratch.

### Total Points (Suggested)

- Weekly build/change assignments (Weeks 1–5): **5 × 10 pts = 50**
- Weekly short-answer questions / exit tickets (Weeks 1–5): **5 × 5 pts = 25**
- Final game submission (Week 6): **20 pts**
- Final reflection (Week 6): **5 pts**

**Total:** 100 points for the unit

---

## Assignment List

### Week 1

**Scratch Starter Remix #1 – Change One Thing** – 10 pts  
Open a pre-built Scratch mini-game, make your own copy, and change at least one visible thing (character, background, or a speed/number). Turn in the project link and a brief explanation of what you changed and how it feels different.

**Tech/Experience Check & “What Is a Game?”** – 5 pts  
Short written answers about past tech/coding experience and what makes something a game (vs. just an animation or video).

---

### Week 2

**Control Remix – Change the Keys** – 10 pts  
Modify a starter project so that different keys control the character and movement speed changes. Turn in the project link plus a short explanation of which keys you used, what numbers you changed, and how it affects gameplay.

**Vocab Check: Event** – 5 pts  
Short answer: explain what an “event” is in Scratch and give one example from your project.

---

### Week 3

**Win/Lose Logic – Editing Conditions** – 10 pts  
Change at least two conditions that control win/lose logic (for example, what counts as a wall, what happens when you hit it, or how you win). Turn in the project link and a short reflection on how your changes affect fairness or difficulty.

**Vocab Check: Condition** – 5 pts  
Short answer: explain what a “condition” is in Scratch and give one example.

---

### Week 4

**Score or Timer – Add a Variable** – 10 pts  
Add a variable (score, lives, or time) to your project. Set it at the start, change it during gameplay, and show it on screen. Turn in the project link and a sentence about what your variable tracks and how players use it.

**Vocab Check: Variable** – 5 pts  
Short answer: define “variable” in your own words and describe the variable you used in your game.

---

### Week 5

**Add Feedback – Sound & Animation** – 10 pts  
Add at least one sound or visual effect for good events (like scoring) and one for bad events (like losing a life), plus a small animation. Turn in the project link and a short note on how your new feedback helps the player.

**Exit Ticket: Favorite Change So Far** – 5 pts  
Short written answer about your favorite code change so far and why you like it.

---

### Week 6

**Final Game Submission** – 20 pts  
Turn in your current version of your game. It should include:

- At least one **event** (key press or click)
- At least one **condition** (`if … then …`)
- At least one **variable** (score, lives, or timer)
- Some kind of **feedback** (sound, animation, or message)

Student submits the project link plus a brief “game card” (title, controls, goal, one change they made in code).

**Course Reflection: Talk Like a Game Designer** – 5 pts  
4–6 sentence reflection using vocabulary from the course (sprite, event, condition, variable, feedback), plus what they can do now that they couldn’t at the start and what they’d like to try next.
