## Goal

Create a self-portrait that is intentionally “digital,” using visible pixels or low resolution as part of the style.

## Steps (student-facing)

1. **Capture or choose a source image**
   - Take a selfie or a symbolic image that represents you.
   - Keep the framing simple (face, hands, a meaningful object, etc.).

2. **Make it small**
   - Resize the image down to a very small size (e.g., 32×32, 64×64, or similar).
   - Notice how details disappear and shapes simplify.

3. **Edit the pixels**
   - Use a zoomed-in view.
   - Adjust individual pixels or small clusters:
     - Simplify shapes.
     - Choose a limited color palette.
   - Keep it readable, but accept some abstraction.

4. **Scale it back up**
   - Resize the edited image larger again so the pixels are clearly visible.
   - Avoid smoothing if possible (“nearest neighbor” scaling).

5. **Optional variations**
   - Make two versions: one moody, one bright.
   - Try a “symbolic” portrait (no literal face, just objects/colors that feel like you).

6. **Reflect**
   - Write 3–5 sentences:
     - What did you simplify or change?
     - How does the pixelation affect how we see you?
     - What choices did you make about color?

## Technical notes (teacher)

- Demo resizing with **nearest neighbor** vs. smooth interpolation.
- Emphasize saving **working files** (e.g., `.xcf`, `.psd`) and final exports (`.png`).
- Connect to vocabulary:
  - Pixel, resolution, upscaling, compression, palette.
