Connect scavenger images to more intentional shooting and introduce Project 1.

## Goals

- Deepen understanding of composition and light.
- Teach basic camera settings (exposure triangle in kid-friendly terms).
- Launch Pixel Self-Portrait project.

## A-Block (≈45 min)

### 1. Warm-up: image pair critique (10 min)

- Show two student images (with permission) or sample photos.
- Ask:
  - What feels stronger and why?
  - Where does your eye go first?

### 2. Mini-lesson: light & exposure (15–20 min)

Keep it simple:

- Brightness vs. darkness.
- Direction of light (front, side, back).
- Shadows and contrast.

If cameras/phones allow:

- Demonstrate tap-to-focus and exposure slider.
- Show what happens when you shoot toward vs. away from light.

### 3. Introduce Project 1 (10–15 min)

- Show example pixel portraits (real or mocked).
- Explain:
  - We’ll make a self-portrait that embraces low resolution.
  - Today: capture source images.

## B-Block (≈45 min)

### 4. Guided self-portrait shoot (25–30 min)

- Students work in pairs:
  - Each student gets several photos taken.
  - Try different backgrounds, angles, and moods.
- Encourage:
  - One straightforward portrait.
  - One playful or unexpected portrait.

### 5. Import & selection (10–15 min)

- Students import photos and choose 1–2 favorites for Project 1.
- If time: create a “WORKING” folder on their account for this course.

### 6. Exit ticket (5 min)

Prompt:

> Which portrait did you choose and why? What mood do you want your pixel portrait to have?
