## Goals

- Introduce pixels, resolution, and file formats.
- Begin editing Pixel Self-Portrait at low resolution.

## A-Block (≈45 min)

### 1. Warm-up: zoom & guess (10 min)

- Display extreme close-ups of images (just pixels).
- Ask students to guess what the full image might be.
- Reveal and connect to idea of pixels as the building blocks.

### 2. Mini-lesson: pixels, resolution, formats (20–25 min)

Key points:

- **Pixel** = smallest unit of an image on screen.
- **Resolution** = how many pixels wide × tall.
- **Higher vs lower** resolution:
  - More detail vs. larger visible blocks.
- File formats:
  - JPG = small size, some quality loss.
  - PNG = better for sharp edges/transparency.

Demonstrate:

- Show same image at 4000×3000 vs 64×48.
- Show difference between save as JPG vs PNG (if possible).
- Project the [Session 3–10 visual pack](../assets/session03-10/README.md) before the live resizing demo.

### 3. Demo: resizing for pixel art (10–15 min)

- Open your own example image.
- Resize to something tiny (e.g., 32×32, 64×64).
- Zoom in to edit.
- Resize back up with “nearest neighbor” or equivalent.

## B-Block (≈45 min)

### 4. Student work time: shrink & edit (30–35 min)

Students:

- Open chosen self-portrait.
- Resize down to chosen small size.
- Edit basic shapes and colors at pixel level:
  - emphasize eyes, glasses, hair, or key symbol.
- Save both:
  - Working file (e.g., `.xcf`, `.psd`).
  - Exported PNG.

Teacher:

- Circulate to help with resolution settings and saving.

### 5. Optional share (5–10 min)

- Invite a few students to show “in-progress” pixel portraits.
- Celebrate weirdness and abstraction.

### 6. Exit ticket (5 min)

Prompt:

> What surprised you when you zoomed in on your own face/image? Name one technical word from today (pixel, resolution, JPEG, PNG) and how it mattered.
