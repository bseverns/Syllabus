## Goals

- Explore glitch and experimental techniques.
- Emphasize images as processes, not just representations.
- Generate source material for Experimental Series.

## A-Block (≈45 min)

### 1. Warm-up: what counts as a “good” image? (10–15 min)

- Show:
  - A technically perfect “stock” photo.
  - A messy, glitched image with strong feeling.
- Use the controlled-glitch sheet in the [Session 3–10 visual pack](../assets/session03-10/README.md).
- Ask:
  - Which is more interesting to you? Why?

### 2. Mini-lesson: glitch & experimentation (20–25 min)

Depending on your tools:

- Demonstrate at least **two** techniques, e.g.:
  - Duplicate and offset layers to create RGB splits.
  - Use a displacement filter or distortion effect.
  - Copy/paste rectangular chunks to scramble areas.
- Emphasize saving copies and enjoying “happy accidents.”

### 3. Connect to themes (5–10 min)

- Ask students to pick their Experimental Series theme if they haven’t already.

## B-Block (≈45 min)

### 4. Glitch lab (30–35 min)

Students:

- Take 1–2 images related to their theme and apply glitch/experimental techniques.
- Save multiple versions (v1, v2, etc.).
- Aim to create at least one “keeper” image today.

Teacher:

- Normalize that many experiments will fail; the point is to explore.

### 5. Gallery mini-walk (5–10 min)

- Pop some student screens up briefly.
- Ask: “What’s working here? What feels new or strange?”

### 6. Exit ticket (5 min)

Prompt:

> Did glitching your image help you say something you couldn’t with a normal photo? How?

## Prep

- Next session (10) will introduce simple generative code; pick a technique that fits your comfort level with p5.js.
