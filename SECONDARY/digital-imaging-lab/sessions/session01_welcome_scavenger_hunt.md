## Goals

- Build class community and norms.
- Introduce the idea that digital images are *made* and *organized*, not just “found.”
- Begin looking closely at composition.

## A-Block (≈45 min)

### 1. Welcome & framing (10 min)

- Quick intro: who you are, what the course is about.
- Show 3–4 contrasting images:
  - A low-res pixelated image.
  - A high-res photograph.
  - A poster/graphic.
  - A glitch/abstract image.
- Use the ready-to-project [Session 1–2 visual pack](../assets/session01-02/README.md).
- Ask: “What makes these feel different? What do you notice first?”

### 2. Norms & consent (10 min)

- Establish:
  - Asking before photographing people.
  - Respecting “no.”
  - Saving work in the right place.
- Briefly mention that we’ll talk more about ethics later.

### 3. Mini-lesson: basic composition (10–15 min)

- Concepts:
  - Rule of thirds.
  - Leading lines.
  - Foreground / background.
- Show examples with simple overlays (grid, arrows).

## B-Block (≈45 min)

### 4. Image scavenger hunt (25–30 min)

Students (in pairs or small groups) use cameras/phones to capture:

- A strong diagonal.
- A tiny object that feels big.
- A reflection.
- A shadow that looks like something else.
- A color that shows up in at least 3 different places.

Teacher:

- Set a clear boundary for where they can wander.
- Encourage quantity first, quality later.

### 5. Quick import & share (10–15 min)

- Demonstrate importing photos to computers or a shared drive.
- Have each group pick **one** image to share.
- Rapid projection: each group says one sentence:
  - “We liked this because…”

### 6. Exit reflection (5 min)

Prompt (on board):

> Name one composition trick you used or noticed today, and one question you have about how digital images work.

Students respond in a quick sticky note / shared doc.

## Prep for next time

- Collect a few student images as future examples.
- Check that saving/import process works smoothly; adjust instructions if needed.
