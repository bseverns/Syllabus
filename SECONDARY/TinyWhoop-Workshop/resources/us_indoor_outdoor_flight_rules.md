# U.S. Indoor vs Outdoor Flight Rules for Tiny Whoops

This guide is written for classroom planning in the United States. It is not legal advice. Always verify current FAA, FCC, school, and site rules before flying.

## The basic distinction

### Indoor flight

Indoor flight is primarily governed by:

- school or program rules
- property-owner permission
- your workshop safety plan
- common sense about people, property, batteries, and radios

For an indoor whoop workshop, this means your room rules matter more than airspace charts. But "indoor" does **not** mean "anything goes."

### Outdoor flight

Outdoor flight adds federal flight-rule concerns, including:

- FAA operating rules
- airspace restrictions
- weather and visibility
- nearby airports or heliports
- site permission
- radio / FPV equipment compliance

## Practical classroom rule

For beginners, treat **indoor first** as the default. Only add outdoor flights if the instructor has checked the site and current legal requirements ahead of time.

## FAA points instructors should know

At the time this document was updated:

- Outdoor recreational flyers need to pass **TRUST** before flying.
- Drones under **250 grams** flown recreationally generally do **not** need FAA registration.
- Drones that are required to be registered, or that are registered, must comply with **Remote ID**.
- Night operations add lighting requirements and should not be improvised casually.

## Important school-program nuance

Educational use is not automatically the same thing as recreational use.

If your school or organization is conducting structured outdoor operations, especially under instructor direction, verify whether the flights fit the recreational exception or should be run under **Part 107** or another approved operational framework.

Do not make students carry the legal uncertainty. The instructor or program should settle that before outdoor flights happen.

## Outdoor planning checklist for instructors

Before any outdoor session:

- Confirm the site is approved by your school or program.
- Check current airspace and location restrictions.
- Decide whether the operation is recreational, Part 107, or another approved category.
- Confirm whether registration or Remote ID applies to the aircraft you are using.
- Confirm whether students need TRUST completion evidence.
- Decide on observer roles, pilot line, spectator line, and no-fly directions.
- Check weather, wind, and daylight conditions.

## FCC / analog FPV note

FAA rules and FCC rules are different.

If you use analog FPV gear:

- the VTX must be appropriate for U.S. use
- the operating setup must comply with FCC rules
- amateur-only operation assumptions should be checked before class
- students should not be told to raise VTX power or change channels casually

For beginner indoor use, keep power conservative and your channel plan simple.

## Student-facing version

Students can remember this:

1. **Inside the room:** follow the workshop rules.
2. **Outside the building:** extra flight rules apply.
3. **If you are not sure:** do not launch until the instructor confirms.

## Good default policy

If you want the safest and simplest school version of this workshop:

- fly indoors only
- keep props-off bench work strict
- use low VTX power
- use one active pilot at a time in small rooms
- treat outdoor flying as a separate, pre-approved activity
