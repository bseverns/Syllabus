# Tiny Whoop Maintenance and Crash Recovery

Tiny whoops are durable because pilots inspect them constantly. This is especially true in a student workshop where crashes are part of learning.

## The rule

After any meaningful hit, tumble, or hard landing:

**Disarm first. Inspect before relaunch.**

## Fast crash-recovery sequence

### 1. Disarm

- Cut motors immediately.
- Do not walk toward a spinning quad.

### 2. Approach safely

- Make sure props stopped.
- If the quad landed awkwardly, unplug the battery before handling.

### 3. Check the obvious damage points

- props
- ducts
- frame
- canopy
- battery lead and connector
- camera angle
- antenna

### 4. Check the motors

- Look for hair, carpet fiber, dust, or tape wrapped on the shafts.
- Spin each motor gently by hand.
- Listen and feel for grinding, rubbing, or resistance.

### 5. Decide

**Safe to relaunch** only if:

- props are intact enough to fly
- no duct or frame crack is spreading
- battery lead is secure
- camera and antenna are still mounted correctly
- motors sound and feel normal

## Common tiny-whoop post-crash problems

### Bent or chipped props

Symptoms:

- worse lift
- wobble or rough sound
- sudden drift or weak climb

Action:

- replace the prop

### Hair in a motor

Symptoms:

- motor feels sticky
- rough sound on spin-up
- one corner runs hot

Action:

- remove props if needed
- use tweezers carefully
- spin motor by hand again before testing

### Shifted camera angle

Symptoms:

- view points too high, too low, or sideways
- quad feels "wrong" in FPV even if it still flies

Action:

- reset the canopy or camera angle before relaunch

### Split duct or cracked frame

Symptoms:

- quad drifts strangely
- repeated prop strikes
- visible structural damage

Action:

- do not relaunch until inspected by the instructor

### Loose battery lead

Symptoms:

- intermittent power
- plug wiggles too easily
- exposed conductor or torn insulation

Action:

- ground the quad until fixed

## Routine maintenance between sessions

- wipe dust and hair from motors
- inspect props and replace weak ones
- check screws and canopy fit
- inspect antenna routing
- check battery condition and storage habits
- confirm the saved tune / backup still matches the current quad

## Classroom norm

Teach students that maintenance is part of flying skill.

A pilot who stops, checks, and relaunches safely is doing better work than a pilot who keeps sending a damaged whoop.
