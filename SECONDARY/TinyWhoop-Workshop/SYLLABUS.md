# Tuning Tiny Whoops: A Betaflight Configurator Workshop

## Overview

**Length:** 10 sessions (recommended 90–120 minutes each)  
**Audience:** High school students with access to tiny whoop–class quadcopters (AIO FC/ESC)  
**Focus:** Using **Betaflight Configurator** as the primary tool for understanding and shaping how the quad flies, while also learning the safety, maintenance, simulator, and rule framework around tiny whoops.

The workshop walks students from “I can plug it in” to “I can prep, maintain, and race my whoop safely.”  
We move in this order:

1. Safety, hardware anatomy, rules, and first connection  
2. Radio mapping, ELRS link basics, arming, and flight modes  
3. VelociDrone fundamentals, analog FPV basics, OSD, and first controlled flights  
4. Rates, presets, and basic flight feel  
5. Profiles, backups, and tune documentation  
6. Crash recovery, batteries, and maintenance  
7. Track design, race line, and marshal roles  
8. Troubleshooting clinic and consistency flights  
9. Practice heats and final race prep  
10. Race day and reflection

## Assumptions & Prerequisites

Students:

- Can operate a laptop comfortably (installing software, basic USB troubleshooting)
- Have a tiny whoop–class quad that can run Betaflight firmware
- Have a bound (or bindable) radio transmitter
- Have at least a casual interest in flying FPV or line–of–sight

This workshop is especially well matched to a beginner-friendly brushless whoop such as the **BETAFPV Air65**. The materials now assume students may encounter:

- a 5-in-1 board combining FC, ESC, OSD, receiver link, and VTX functions
- an **ELRS 2.4 GHz** control link
- an **analog 5.8 GHz** FPV video link
- a 1S battery-powered indoor whoop frame with ducts

Each student should bring:

- Tiny whoop with AIO FC/ESC  
- Radio transmitter  
- 2–4 charged 1S LiPo batteries  
- A **data–capable** USB cable for their flight controller  

The classroom provides:

- Laptops or desktops with a Chromium–based browser
- Access to the Betaflight Configurator app or web version
- Access to **VelociDrone** on at least some machines, ideally with radios configured as USB controller inputs
- A safe indoor flying area (even a small gym or classroom with a simple track)
- Extra USB cables and a few spare whoops if possible

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the workshop, students will be able to:

1. **Name the Main Systems**
   - Explain, in plain language, what the frame, ducts, motors, props, FC, ESC, receiver, VTX, camera, antenna, and battery each do.

2. **Connect & Back Up**
   - Connect a tiny whoop to Betaflight and export a configuration backup.

3. **Map Controls Safely**
   - Verify radio channels, set up arming and flight modes, and test motors safely with props off.

4. **Shape Flight Behavior**
   - Adjust basic rate, expo, and mode settings and explain how they affect the “feel” of the quad.

5. **Read the Aircraft**
   - Use on–screen information and simple tab checks (Setup, Receiver, Modes, Motors, OSD) to diagnose basic issues.

6. **Operate with Intention**
   - Follow a written pre–flight checklist and articulate simple safety practices for small drones.

7. **Explain the Rule Difference Between Indoor and Outdoor Flying**
   - Describe which rules are school or site rules, which are FAA flight rules, and why outdoor flying requires extra planning.

8. **Recover from Small Crashes**
   - Perform a basic post-crash inspection and decide whether the whoop is safe to fly again.

9. **Race Safely and Fairly**
   - Follow track rules, marshal roles, start/stop commands, and safe race etiquette.

10. **Use Simulator Time Intentionally**
   - Practice launch, throttle control, turns, and track rhythm in VelociDrone before carrying the same habits into real flights.

## Session Sketch

### Session 1 — Ground School, Rules & First Connection

- Safety culture and expectations  
- Anatomy of a tiny whoop (frame, AIO FC/ESC, motors, receiver, VTX, battery)  
- Basic mechanics and signal flow using an Air65-style example  
- Indoor vs outdoor operating context  
- Installing / opening Betaflight Configurator  
- Connecting and exporting a first backup  
- Verifying FC orientation in the Setup tab

**Milestone:** Each student has connected their whoop and saved at least one config backup.

---

### Session 2 — Radio, Modes & Arming

- Binding / checking the radio link  
- ELRS basics: packet link, model match habits, and why the radio link is separate from the video link  
- Receiver tab: channel mapping, endpoints, AUX channels  
- Modes tab: arming switch, beginner–friendly flight modes  
- Safe motor testing in the Motors tab (props off)

**Milestone:** Each student can safely arm/disarm (props off) and show correct stick motion in Betaflight.

---

### Session 3 — VelociDrone, Analog FPV, OSD & First Flights

- VelociDrone setup, basic controls, and beginner drills
- OSD basics: voltage, timer, and basic warning elements  
- Analog FPV basics: camera, VTX, goggles, channel/power habits, and why low power matters indoors  
- Short line-of-sight and/or beginner FPV flights on a simple indoor track

**Milestone:** Each student can launch, fly a short controlled pattern, land safely, and identify key OSD information.

---

### Session 4 — Rates, Presets & Flight Feel

- Using a sensible tiny–whoop preset (if available for your target)  
- Rates and expo as “character controls”  
- Creating at least two rate profiles (mild vs spicy)  
- Short VelociDrone warm-up before real-flight comparison
- Controlled comparison flights

**Milestone:** Each student can feel the difference between at least two rate setups and explain which is more comfortable.

---

### Session 5 — Profiles, Backups & Tune Notes

- Save and label updated backups
- Record preferred profile choices
- Build a simple tune notebook or screenshot log
- Practice controlled laps with documented settings

**Milestone:** Each student has a labeled backup and a written record of their preferred profile.

---

### Session 6 — Crash Recovery, Batteries & Maintenance

- Crash inspection steps
- Tiny-whoop wear points: props, ducts, motors, canopy, battery lead
- Battery handling and cooldown habits
- Repair triage: relaunch, bench-check, or ground the quad

**Milestone:** Each student can complete a crash-recovery checklist and explain when not to relaunch.

---

### Session 7 — Track Design, Race Line & Marshal Roles

- Build or refine a classroom race course
- Learn gates, corners, lines, and safe passing expectations
- Assign pilot, spotter, and marshal roles
- Practice the same course ideas in VelociDrone if sim stations are available
- Practice smooth laps over fast laps

**Milestone:** Students can explain the course, marshal safely, and fly a predictable line.

---

### Session 8 — Troubleshooting Clinic & Consistency Flights

- Saving a “final” backup and recording key settings  
- Reading arming flags and basic error messages  
- Troubleshooting clinic with common whoop issues  
- Consistency flights with a focus on repeatability rather than speed
- Writing and practicing a personal pre–flight checklist

**Milestone:** Each student can work through a structured diagnosis and fly repeatable laps on a known setup.

---

### Session 9 — Practice Heats & Final Race Prep

- Heat structure and race commands
- Pre-race tech check
- VelociDrone warm-up laps or line rehearsal
- Practice heats or time trials
- Final tune lock and backup

**Milestone:** Each student is race-ready with a working quad, a known profile, and a clear understanding of race procedures.

---

### Session 10 — Race Day & Reflection

- Safety briefing and pilot order
- Practice launch window
- Heats, time trials, or bracket racing
- Reflection on setup, maintenance, flying, and sportsmanship

**Milestone:** Students complete the final race day using safe race procedures and reflect on what they learned.

## Assessment & Evidence of Learning

This workshop leans on **demonstration and reflection** more than grades.

Possible assessment tools:

- **Checklists** – students complete and sign off on their own pre–flight checklist.
- **Crash checks** – students show a safe post-crash inspection after a simulated incident.
- **Exit tickets** – short written reflections at the end of each session (e.g., “One setting I changed and what it did”).
- **Demonstrations** – each student shows a safe arm/disarm sequence and a simple flight.
- **Simulator drills** – students demonstrate controlled launch, turns, and landing in VelociDrone before higher-pressure real flights.
- **Race roles** – students are assessed on pilot discipline, marshal behavior, and sportsmanship, not only lap time.

## Adaptation Notes

- For shorter blocks (e.g., 60 minutes), split each session into A/B halves and run the workshop over 6–8 meetings.
- For more experienced pilots, compress Sessions 1–4 and spend more time on advanced tuning, track design, and race analysis.
- For beginners with no flight experience, add a VelociDrone warm-up before every real-flight block.
- If you expect outdoor flying, review `resources/us_indoor_outdoor_flight_rules.md` before the first battery is plugged in outside.
