# Tuning Tiny Whoops: A Betaflight Configurator Workshop

## Overview

**Length:** 10 weeks, meeting Tuesday and Thursday each week

**Class time:** 20 meetings, 90 minutes each

**Audience:** High school students with access to tiny whoop-class quadcopters

**Focus:** Students build flight control through early and repeated **VelociDrone** practice while using **Betaflight Configurator** to understand and shape how a tiny whoop flies. Safety, maintenance, configuration, and rules are taught as the necessary support system for getting more controlled, more reliable flight time.

The course starts with something immediately engaging: students fly in the simulator during Week 1. Technical and safety work then becomes the structure that lets them fly better, protect the equipment, and eventually race with confidence. Each week uses the same rhythm:

- **Tuesday:** hook the concept through control practice, then introduce the week's technical or safety layer.
- **Thursday:** practice, troubleshoot, document evidence, and apply the skill in simulator or real-flight work.

## Assumptions & Prerequisites

Students:

- Can operate a laptop comfortably.
- Have or share a tiny whoop-class quad that can run Betaflight firmware.
- Have access to a bound or bindable radio transmitter.
- Have at least a casual interest in FPV, robotics, racing, or hands-on flight.

This workshop is especially well matched to a beginner-friendly brushless whoop such as the **BETAFPV Air65**. The materials assume students may encounter:

- a 5-in-1 board combining FC, ESC, OSD, receiver link, and VTX functions
- an **ELRS 2.4 GHz** control link
- an **analog 5.8 GHz** FPV video link
- a 1S battery-powered indoor whoop frame with ducts

Each student or team should have:

- Tiny whoop with AIO FC/ESC
- Radio transmitter
- 2-4 charged 1S LiPo batteries
- A **data-capable** USB cable for the flight controller

The classroom should provide:

- Laptops or desktops with a Chromium-based browser
- Access to the Betaflight Configurator app or web version
- Access to **VelociDrone** on at least some machines, ideally with radios configured as USB controller inputs
- A safe indoor flying area
- Extra USB cables and spare whoops if possible

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the workshop, students will be able to:

1. **Name the main systems.** Explain, in plain language, what the frame, ducts, motors, props, FC, ESC, receiver, VTX, camera, antenna, and battery each do.
2. **Connect and back up.** Connect a tiny whoop to Betaflight and export a configuration backup.
3. **Map controls safely.** Verify radio channels, set up arming and flight modes, and test motors safely with props off.
4. **Shape flight behavior.** Adjust basic rate, expo, and mode settings and explain how they affect flight feel.
5. **Practice control deliberately.** Use VelociDrone to build launch, throttle, turn, landing, gate, and race-line habits from the first week.
6. **Read the aircraft.** Use on-screen information and basic Betaflight tabs to diagnose common setup issues.
7. **Operate with intention.** Follow written pre-flight and post-crash checklists.
8. **Explain indoor vs outdoor rule differences.** Describe which rules are school/site rules, which are FAA flight rules, and why outdoor flying requires extra planning.
9. **Recover from small crashes.** Perform a basic post-crash inspection and decide whether the whoop is safe to fly again.
10. **Race safely and fairly.** Follow simulator race norms, track rules, marshal roles, start/stop commands, and race etiquette.

## Weekly Schedule

### Week 1 - Ground School & First Connection

**Tuesday:** First VelociDrone flight-control hook, safety culture, tiny whoop anatomy, signal flow, and indoor/outdoor rule framing.

**Thursday:** Betaflight tour, USB connection, baseline backup, FC orientation check, and short simulator reps.

**Evidence:** Students complete a first simulator control attempt, save a baseline backup, and explain one difference between the control link, video link, and power system.

**Materials:** `Betaflight_Config_Worksheet_student.md`, `Safety_Agreement_student.md`

### Week 2 - Radio, Modes & Arming

**Tuesday:** Simulator control drills, radio link basics, ELRS vocabulary, Receiver tab, stick axes, channel map, and AUX switches.

**Thursday:** Modes tab, arming switch, Angle mode, props-off motor testing, and simulator gate practice.

**Evidence:** Students demonstrate correct stick response, a safe arm/disarm sequence with props off, and one repeatable simulator drill.

**Materials:** `Betaflight_Config_Worksheet_student.md`, `Flight_Readiness_Card.md`

### Week 3 - VelociDrone, Analog FPV, OSD & First Flights

**Tuesday:** Simulator race-line drills, analog FPV chain, VTX/channel norms, and OSD basics.

**Thursday:** Flight-area briefing, first line-of-sight or beginner FPV flights, clean landing, and post-flight reflection.

**Evidence:** Students complete a short controlled pattern and identify useful OSD information.

**Materials:** `Betaflight_Config_Worksheet_student.md`, `Flight_Readiness_Card.md`

### Week 4 - Rates, Presets & Flight Feel

**Tuesday:** Baseline presets or stock tune review, rates, expo, gentle vs responsive profiles, and simulator feel tests.

**Thursday:** Simulator time trials plus cautious real-space comparison, with reflection on which profile feels more controllable.

**Evidence:** Students compare two rate profiles, complete a simulator time trial or clean-lap challenge, and explain which profile they would trust for practice.

**Materials:** `Betaflight_Config_Worksheet_student.md`, `Student_Tune_Log_Template.md`

### Week 5 - Profiles, Backups & Tune Notes

**Tuesday:** Naming conventions, screenshots, tune logs, profile records, restore points, and race setup notes.

**Thursday:** Simulator race heats, documented real-lap practice if ready, and a short debrief on what changed or stayed stable.

**Evidence:** Students produce a labeled backup, a tune note that matches the profile they flew, and a simulator race reflection tied to control choices.

**Materials:** `Student_Tune_Log_Template.md`, `Competency_Grid.md`

### Week 6 - Crash Recovery, Batteries & Maintenance

**Tuesday:** Crash inspection sequence, common wear points, and relaunch/bench-check/ground decisions.

**Thursday:** Battery care, cooldown habits, maintenance stations, and checklist practice.

**Evidence:** Students complete a crash-recovery checklist and identify at least one condition that grounds a quad.

**Materials:** `Crash_Recovery_Checklist_student.md`, `Whoop_Down_Learning_Board.md`

### Week 7 - Track Design, Race Line & Marshal Roles

**Tuesday:** Course walk, safe zones, gates, turns, race line, and track vocabulary.

**Thursday:** Marshal responsibilities, structured track practice, and predictable lap routines.

**Evidence:** Students explain the course, serve in a track role, and fly or support a safe practice run.

**Materials:** `Track_Roles_and_Marshal_Guide.md`, `Student_Tune_Log_Template.md`

### Week 8 - Troubleshooting Clinic & Consistency Flights

**Tuesday:** Structured diagnosis using Setup, Receiver, Modes, Motors, OSD warnings, and arming flags.

**Thursday:** Pair diagnostics, pre-flight checklist independence, and consistency flights.

**Evidence:** Students narrate a diagnosis and complete repeatable laps or repeated controlled patterns.

**Materials:** `Troubleshooting_Decision_Tree.md`, `Whoop_Down_Learning_Board.md`

### Week 9 - Practice Heats & Race Prep

**Tuesday:** Race format, start commands, heat sheets, marshal assignments, final tech check, and channel plan.

**Thursday:** Practice heats or time trials, tune lock, final backup, and race-day plan.

**Evidence:** Students finish with a working race setup, a final backup, and a clear race-day routine.

**Materials:** `Flight_Readiness_Card.md`, `Race_Day_Heat_Sheet.md`, `Student_Tune_Log_Template.md`

### Week 10 - Race Day & Reflection

**Tuesday:** Final safety check, pilot order, heat structure, race event, and scorekeeping.

**Thursday:** Finals or showcase runs, teardown, maintenance review, and reflection on setup, flying, safety, and sportsmanship.

**Evidence:** Students participate safely as pilots, spotters, marshals, or race staff and complete a final reflection.

**Materials:** `Competency_Grid.md`, `Race_Day_Heat_Sheet.md`

## Assessment & Evidence of Learning

This workshop leans on **demonstration and reflection** more than traditional grades.

Suggested assessment tools:

- **Checklists:** students complete pre-flight and crash-recovery checks.
- **Config evidence:** students maintain named backups, screenshots, or tune notes.
- **Betaflight worksheet:** students record backup, orientation, receiver, modes, failsafe, motors, OSD, and rates.
- **Flight readiness card:** students are cleared for real flight or race roles through teacher signoff.
- **Exit tickets:** students write short reflections such as "one setting I changed and what it did."
- **Demonstrations:** students show safe arm/disarm, receiver mapping, and basic flight readiness.
- **Simulator drills and races:** students demonstrate controlled launch, turns, landing, clean laps, and race etiquette in VelociDrone.
- **Race roles:** students are assessed on pilot discipline, marshal behavior, and sportsmanship, not only lap time.
- **Competency grid:** students and instructors mark novice, developing, ready, or mentor status for safety, config, control, maintenance, troubleshooting, and race roles.

### Delivery conventions

- Use a team or classroom handle in filenames, not a legal name: `team-handle-whoop1-week05.json`.
- A submitted file proves that evidence was captured; it does not prove flight readiness. Instructor signoff still controls real flight.
- Scope the helper to the current week's Betaflight, simulator, safety, or troubleshooting topics. Use symptom → check → retest guidance rather than configuration dumps.
- Open real-flight modules manually after the room, aircraft, and student pathway are ready. ClassHub does not provide per-student competency unlocks.
- Use the final gallery only for a student-selected photo, track plan, or reflection excerpt cleared for class sharing.

## Adaptation Notes

- For shorter blocks, keep the same Tuesday/Thursday pairing but reduce flight volume.
- For more experienced pilots, compress Weeks 1-2 and spend more time on advanced tuning, track design, and race analysis.
- For beginners with no flight experience, add a VelociDrone warm-up before every real-flight block.
- If you expect outdoor flying, review `resources/us_indoor_outdoor_flight_rules.md` before the first outdoor battery is plugged in.
