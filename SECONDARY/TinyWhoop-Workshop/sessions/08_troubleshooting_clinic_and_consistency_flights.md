# Week 8 - Troubleshooting Clinic & Consistency Flights

**Theme:** Fix the pattern, not just the symptom.
**Tuesday focus:** Structured diagnosis and common Betaflight clues
**Thursday focus:** Pair diagnostics, checklist independence, and consistency flights
**Class length:** Two 90-minute meetings

---

## Goals

By the end of the week, students will:

- Work through a structured troubleshooting process.
- Recognize common arming flags and setup errors.
- Use Betaflight tabs to narrow a problem.
- Fly repeatable laps or repeated controlled patterns on a stable setup.
- Use a pre-flight checklist without prompting.

---

## Instructor Prep

- Prepare a demo rig with one or two intentional problems.
- Print or share `materials/Preflight_Checklist_student.md`.
- Print or share `materials/Troubleshooting_Decision_Tree.md`.
- Print or post `materials/Whoop_Down_Learning_Board.md`.
- Have a short list of common symptoms:
  - will not arm
  - wrong stick response
  - missing video
  - rough motor
  - profile feels different than expected
- Decide which problems students may fix and which require instructor handling.

## Tuesday Class - Troubleshooting Workflow

### 0:00-0:15 - Status Check

Ask students:

- What is your whoop's biggest weak point right now?
- What still feels unpredictable?
- What would keep you from racing today?

Students briefly check current profile and backup status.

### 0:15-0:45 - Demo Diagnosis

On the projector, use an intentionally broken or misconfigured demo rig.

Walk through:

1. What symptom do we see?
2. What changed recently?
3. What tab gives useful evidence?
4. What is safe to test?
5. What should we not touch yet?

Use:

- Setup tab for orientation
- Receiver tab for stick/switch behavior
- Modes tab for arming and mode ranges
- Motors tab for props-off motor checks
- OSD or warnings for flight clues
- arming flags as information

### 0:45-1:05 - Troubleshooting Map

As a class, build a quick map:

- **Will not connect:** cable, port, driver, FC power
- **Will not arm:** switch, mode range, arming flag, failsafe, angle too steep
- **Moves wrong in Receiver tab:** channel map or radio setup
- **Flies rough:** props, motors, frame, tune, battery
- **Video issue:** camera, VTX, channel, goggles/receiver

Compare the class map to `materials/Troubleshooting_Decision_Tree.md`. Students should mark the branch most relevant to their own whoop.

### 1:05-1:20 - Student Setup Snapshot

Students connect and record:

- active profile
- receiver response okay/not okay
- modes okay/not okay
- failsafe verified / pending / needs instructor check
- any warnings or arming flags
- one issue to check Thursday

### 1:20-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- The first thing I should check for my current issue is...
- One thing I should not randomly change is...

---

## Thursday Class - Pair Diagnostics & Consistency Flights

### 0:00-0:15 - Independent Pre-Flight

Students complete the pre-flight checklist without instructor prompting.

They must be able to state:

- current profile
- arming switch
- flight mode
- one known risk or issue

### 0:15-0:45 - Pair Diagnostics

Students pair up and inspect each other's setup.

Each pair should:

1. identify one symptom or confirm race-readiness
2. choose a Betaflight tab or physical inspection step
3. narrate the reasoning
4. record the conclusion
5. ask for instructor sign-off before any risky change

If a whoop cannot fly, the pair uses the whoop-down learning board to choose a useful role or station.

### 0:45-1:20 - Consistency Flights

Students fly repeatable laps or controlled patterns.

Focus:

- same takeoff ritual
- same line
- same landing discipline
- no random last-minute tuning
- post-crash checklist after any meaningful hit

If many quads are not ready, run a bench-clinic rotation instead of forcing flight time.

### 1:20-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- What issue did I diagnose or rule out today?
- Am I race-ready yet? Why or why not?

---

## Evidence of Learning

- Student uses a structured diagnosis rather than guessing.
- Student records setup status and at least one conclusion.
- Student uses the troubleshooting decision tree or whoop-down learning board when blocked.
- Student completes repeatable laps, controlled patterns, or a bench-clinic fix with documentation.

---

## Notes & Variations

- If many quads need bench work, cut flying time and stay in troubleshooting mode.
- If students are stable, add short time trials while preserving checklist discipline.
- Keep troubleshooting public and calm; the point is to model reasoning, not hide mistakes.
