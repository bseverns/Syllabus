# Week 7 - Track Design, Race Line & Marshal Roles

**Theme:** Racing is also choreography.
**Tuesday focus:** Track design, course vocabulary, and race line
**Thursday focus:** Marshal roles and structured track practice
**Class length:** Two 90-minute meetings

---

## Goals

By the end of the week, students will:

- Help build or refine a safe indoor race course.
- Understand pilot line, marshal positions, spectator area, and no-fly zones.
- Practice a smooth race line through gates and turns.
- Learn basic race etiquette and marshal responsibilities.
- Treat predictable movement as a safety skill.

---

## Instructor Prep

- Decide on a race layout before class, even if students help refine it.
- Mark clear safe zones for pilots, spectators, and marshals.
- Print or project `materials/Track_Roles_and_Marshal_Guide.md`.
- Decide whether students will fly full laps or track sections.

---

## Materials

- Gates, cones, chairs, tape, or other course markers
- Student whoops and radios
- Whiteboard or projector
- Track roles guide
- Pre-flight and crash-recovery checklists

---

## Tuesday Class - Course Design & Race Line

### 0:00-0:15 - Course Walk

- Walk the whole class through the proposed track.
- Name:
  - start/finish
  - gates
  - technical corners
  - danger points
  - pilot line
  - spectator area
  - marshal positions

### 0:15-0:35 - Safe Track Design Principles

Discuss what makes a school track teachable:

- enough space after takeoff
- turns that reward control
- no flight path aimed at spectators
- no-fly zones around doors, desks, shelves, and charging areas
- clear recovery paths for marshals

Students suggest one improvement and explain the safety reason.

### 0:35-0:55 - Race Line Basics

Explain:

- smooth beats fast when learning
- a wider setup can make turns easier
- cutting every corner is not always faster
- one clean lap is better than three chaotic ones

Use walking, tape, or a demo lap to show the intended line.

### 0:55-1:15 - Simulator or Walked Rehearsal

If VelociDrone is available, students rehearse similar gate/turn ideas in sim.

If not, students walk the course and call out:

- where to slow down
- where to turn
- where crashes are likely
- where a marshal can safely stand

### 1:15-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- The hardest part of this track will be...
- The safest marshal position is...

---

## Thursday Class - Roles & Track Practice

### 0:00-0:15 - Role Briefing

Use the track roles guide to define:

- pilot
- spotter/helper
- marshal
- race director/instructor

Practice the commands:

- pilots ready
- arm
- go
- stop
- land now
- course clear

### 0:15-0:30 - Pre-Flight and Role Assignment

Students:

- complete pre-flight checks
- confirm active profile
- confirm pilot order
- take a pilot, spotter, marshal, or observer role

### 0:30-1:15 - Structured Track Practice

Run short practice blocks:

- first section only
- one clean lap
- repeated same line
- clean launch and landing
- marshal reset only after motors stop

Rotate roles so students learn the event from more than the pilot seat.

### 1:15-1:25 - Debrief

Ask:

- What part of the track caused the most mistakes?
- Which role required the most attention?
- What should change before practice heats?

### 1:25-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- One race-line adjustment I will try next time is...
- One marshal rule I will remember is...

---

## Evidence of Learning

- Student explains the course and its safe zones.
- Student serves in a track role.
- Student flies, supports, or observes a structured practice run using the agreed commands.

---

## Notes & Variations

- If space is small, use a 3- to 5-feature technical course rather than a long track.
- If students are inconsistent, practice sections instead of full laps.
- If students are advanced, add timed consistency targets before adding head-to-head racing.
