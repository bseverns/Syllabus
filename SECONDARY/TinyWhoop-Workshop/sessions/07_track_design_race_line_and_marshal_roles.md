# Session 7 – Track Design, Race Line & Marshal Roles

**Theme:** Racing is also choreography.  
**Duration:** 90–120 minutes

---

## Goals

By the end of this session, students will:

- Help build or refine a safe indoor race course.
- Understand pilot line, marshal line, and spectator line.
- Practice a smooth race line through gates and turns.
- Learn basic race etiquette and marshal responsibilities.

---

## Instructor Prep

- Decide on your indoor race layout before class, even if students help refine it.
- Mark clear safe zones for pilots, spectators, and marshals.
- Print or project `materials/Track_Roles_and_Marshal_Guide.md`.

---

## Materials

- Gates, cones, chairs, tape, or other course markers
- Student whoops and radios
- Whiteboard or projector

---

## Schedule (example for 2 hours)

### 0:00–0:20 – Course Walk

- Walk the whole class through the track.
- Name:
  - start / finish
  - gates
  - technical corners
  - danger points
  - marshal positions

### 0:20–0:40 – Race Line Basics

Explain:

- smooth beats fast when learning
- wide setup can make turns easier
- cutting every corner is not always faster
- one clean lap is better than three chaotic ones

### 0:40–1:00 – Marshal Roles

Teach students how to:

- respond to a downed whoop only when safe
- wait for motors to stop
- avoid stepping into live flight paths
- reset a whoop without becoming part of the crash

### 1:00–1:45 – Track Practice

- Students fly short laps or segments.
- Focus on:
  - same route every time
  - clean takeoff
  - clean landing
  - staying calm after mistakes

### 1:45–2:00 – Wrap & Exit Ticket

- What part of the track is hardest?
- What marshal rule matters most?

---

## Notes & Variations

- If space is tiny, use a 3- to 5-feature technical course rather than a long track.
- If students are inconsistent, let them practice sections instead of full laps.
