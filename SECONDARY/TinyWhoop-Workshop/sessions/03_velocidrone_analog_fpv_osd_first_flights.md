# Week 3 - VelociDrone, Analog FPV, OSD & First Flights

**Theme:** Move from simulator control toward real flight readiness.
**Tuesday focus:** Simulator race-line drills, analog FPV chain, VTX norms, and OSD setup
**Thursday focus:** Flight-area routines and first controlled flights
**Class length:** Two 90-minute meetings

---

## Goals

By the end of the week, students will:

- Use VelociDrone for race-line, gate, clean-lap, and reset practice.
- Explain the difference between the radio-control link and the analog FPV video link.
- Configure a basic OSD with battery voltage and timer.
- Follow a safe indoor channel and pilot-area routine.
- Complete short first flights with control and safe landing habits.

---

## Instructor Prep

- Prepare a simple indoor flight area or track.
- Test at least one fully working whoop for demo flights.
- Set a conservative indoor VTX power and channel plan.
- Test VelociDrone and radio USB/controller input before class.
- Prepare one simple simulator course that can later become a Week 4 clean-lap challenge.
- Decide whether first flights will be line-of-sight, FPV, or a mix.
- Print or share `materials/Betaflight_Config_Worksheet_student.md`.
- Print or share `materials/Flight_Readiness_Card.md`.

---

## Materials

- Student quads and radios
- Laptops with Betaflight Configurator
- VelociDrone-capable machines if available
- Indoor flight area, cones, gates, tape, or chairs
- Optional FPV goggles or video receiver
- Pre-flight checklist

## Tuesday Class - Simulator Race-Line, FPV Chain & OSD

### 0:00-0:10 - Safety & Link Review

- Review:
  - radio on before battery
  - props off on the bench
  - pilot area and spectator area stay separate
- Ask students to explain what ELRS does and what the VTX does.

### 0:10-0:40 - VelociDrone Race-Line Block

- Connect a radio as a USB controller if supported.
- Calibrate sticks if needed.
- Run a control-to-line sequence:
  - clean launch
  - straight gate approach
  - one controlled turn
  - one clean gate exit
  - land or reset calmly
- Introduce clean-lap scoring: clean completion matters before time.
- Keep advanced students on repeatable clean laps, not chaotic free flight.

### 0:40-1:00 - Analog FPV Basics

On the projector or board:

- Explain the video chain: camera -> VTX -> goggles/receiver.
- Explain why this is separate from the ELRS control link.
- Set classroom norms:
  - low VTX power indoors
  - known channel before powering multiple whoops
  - no casual "more power" changes

If your fleet includes Air65-style hardware, note that 25 mW is a common conservative indoor reference point, but students should verify their actual gear.

### 1:00-1:20 - OSD Basics

On the projector:

- Open the **OSD** tab.
- Enable and place:
  - main voltage
  - flight time or timer
  - warning or arming indicator

Students:

1. Enable the same basic OSD elements.
2. Save their layout.
3. Write down which OSD element will help them decide when to land.
4. Record OSD battery, timer, and warning visibility in the Betaflight configuration worksheet.

### 1:20-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- What is one simulator habit I want to carry into real flight?
- What OSD element matters most for a beginner flight?
- What makes a clean lap different from a fast crash?

## Thursday Class - First Controlled Flights

### 0:00-0:15 - Flight Area Briefing

- Walk the class through:
  - takeoff area
  - landing area
  - pilot line
  - spectator area
  - no-fly directions
  - stop commands
- Make the stop command unambiguous and practice the response.

### 0:15-0:30 - Pre-Flight Routine

Students use the checklist to confirm:

- correct model on radio
- battery condition
- prop/duct/canopy check
- expected flight mode
- video channel if using FPV
- pilot area is clear

Instructor uses the flight readiness card to confirm:

- backup saved
- orientation verified
- receiver/modes verified
- failsafe verified or instructor-approved exception documented
- OSD battery/timer visible

### 0:30-1:15 - First Flights

Run one student or a carefully spaced pair at a time.

Each student attempts:

1. arm safely
2. lift into a stable hover
3. fly a short box, gate-to-gate move, or partial lap
4. land
5. disarm
6. unplug and inspect

Use line-of-sight first unless the room and student readiness support FPV.

### 1:15-1:25 - Quick Debrief

Ask:

- What felt easier in the simulator than in the real room?
- What felt harder?
- Which safety habit was hardest to remember?

### 1:25-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- I am currently more comfortable with line-of-sight / FPV because...
- My next flight goal is...

---

## Evidence of Learning

- Student completes a simulator drill or observes and explains the drill sequence.
- Student describes one race-line habit that should transfer to real flight.
- Student configures basic OSD information.
- Student has a flight readiness card started before real flight.
- Student completes or supports a controlled first flight routine.

---

## Notes & Variations

- If students are nervous, use hover boxes or short gate-to-gate moves instead of laps.
- If the room is small, emphasize takeoff, controlled motion, and landing rather than speed.
- If working quads are limited, rotate stations: simulator, bench setup, flight line, observer.
