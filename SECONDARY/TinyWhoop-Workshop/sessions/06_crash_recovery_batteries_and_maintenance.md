# Week 6 - Crash Recovery, Batteries & Maintenance

**Theme:** Keeping a whoop alive is part of piloting.
**Tuesday focus:** Crash inspection, wear points, and relaunch decisions
**Thursday focus:** Battery habits and maintenance stations
**Class length:** Two 90-minute meetings

---

## Goals

By the end of the week, students will:

- Perform a basic crash inspection.
- Decide when to relaunch, bench-check, or ground a quad.
- Identify common tiny-whoop wear points.
- Practice safe battery handling and cooldown habits.
- Treat maintenance as a normal part of flight, not an interruption.

---

## Instructor Prep

- Print or share `materials/Crash_Recovery_Checklist_student.md`.
- Read `resources/maintenance_and_crash_recovery.md`.
- Prepare a demo quad with a few common issues if possible:
  - bent prop
  - shifted canopy
  - hair in motor
  - cracked duct
  - loose battery lead example
- Gather spare props, tweezers, basic tools, and a bright inspection light.

---

## Materials

- Student quads and radios
- Crash-recovery checklists
- Spare props and basic tools if available
- Tweezers and bright inspection light
- Battery charging/storage setup approved by your program

---

## Tuesday Class - Crash Recovery & Relaunch Decisions

### 0:00-0:10 - Why Crashes Are Data

- Frame crashes as expected learning events.
- Explain the core rule: disarm first, inspect before relaunch.
- Ask students what they have already seen go wrong after crashes.

### 0:10-0:35 - Demonstration: Post-Crash Ritual

Demonstrate:

1. disarm
2. approach only after props stop
3. unplug if needed
4. inspect props, ducts, frame, canopy, battery lead, camera, and antenna
5. spin motors gently by hand
6. decide relaunch, bench-check, or ground

Narrate why each step exists.

### 0:35-1:05 - Student Inspection Practice

Students:

- Inspect their own whoops.
- Rotate through any prepared problem quads.
- Use the checklist to make a relaunch decision.
- Ask for instructor clearance when uncertain.

### 1:05-1:20 - Grounding Decisions

Discuss examples:

- bent prop
- cracked duct
- rough motor
- loose antenna
- puffy battery
- damaged battery lead

Students sort each example into:

- safe to relaunch
- bench-check first
- grounded until repaired

### 1:20-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- One thing I might relaunch after is...
- One thing that should ground the quad is...

---

## Thursday Class - Batteries & Maintenance Stations

### 0:00-0:15 - Battery Safety Habits

Review:

- battery damage signs
- puffy packs as a stop sign
- cooling between flights
- unplugging by the connector, not wires
- storage and charging rules used by your program

### 0:15-1:05 - Maintenance Stations

Rotate students through stations such as:

- prop inspection and replacement
- motor hair cleanup
- canopy and camera-angle reset
- battery-lead inspection
- antenna routing check
- tune/backup check against current quad

At each station, students record what they checked and whether anything changed.

### 1:05-1:20 - Short Relaunch Readiness Check

Students complete the crash-recovery checklist on their own quad after maintenance.

If safe and time allows, run a brief hover or short pattern to confirm the quad still behaves normally.

### 1:20-1:30 - Exit Ticket

Students answer:

- The most likely wear point on my quad is...
- My maintenance habit before next flight will be...

---

## Evidence of Learning

- Student completes a crash-recovery checklist.
- Student identifies at least one no-fly condition.
- Student practices at least one maintenance inspection or minor maintenance task.

---

## Notes & Variations

- If you do not want students changing props yet, keep stations inspection-only.
- If students crash often, repeat the checklist before every later flight session.
- If battery policy is strict at your site, replace handling practice with a teacher-led demo and student observation notes.
