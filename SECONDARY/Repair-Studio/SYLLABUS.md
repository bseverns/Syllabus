# Syllabus — Repair Studio

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners use observation, safe tests, and repair logs to make a grounded repair, reuse, or salvage decision.
- **Group / staffing:** 10–16 learners; one lead and one assistant recommended for device handling and tool flow.
- **Final share:** a repair dossier with before/after evidence or an honest postmortem.
- **Agreement:** *We document before we change a thing.*

## Materials for 12 learners

Twelve pre-screened, battery-powered or unpowered devices: headphones, keyboards, mice, LED lights, handheld fans, controllers, or battery toys; six precision screwdriver sets; six multimeters; spudgers; tweezers; screw trays; labels; painter's tape; magnifiers; repair logs; and sorted salvage bins.

**Preflight:** facilitator inspects every device, removes batteries where appropriate, and labels it `safe to open`, `observe only`, or `salvage only`. Exclude mains-powered appliances, swollen/damaged lithium batteries, CRTs, microwaves, and any device with unknown hazards. Instructor controls soldering and battery disposal.

## 90-minute session rhythm

10-minute launch; 12-minute demonstration; 35-minute paired investigation; 8-minute reset; 15-minute test/documentation round; 10-minute share and cleanup.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Intake: look before touching

**Target:** distinguish observation from a repair guess.

**Prompt:** *What can we know without opening this device?*

**Build/play:** learners complete an intake sheet, describe the symptom, check externally visible parts, and make one safe non-invasive test.

**Evidence:** device ID, symptom, initial observation, and a proposed next test.

### Session 2 — Open, identify, document

**Target:** open a pre-cleared device while preserving a path back.

**Prompt:** *How will another person know where every part belongs?*

**Build/play:** pairs photograph/sketch before opening, manage fasteners in a tray, label visible parts, and produce a simple exploded-view note.

**Evidence:** labeled interior sketch or photo set; all fasteners accounted for.

### Session 3 — Test, isolate, decide

**Target:** use one controlled test to narrow a cause.

**Prompt:** *What is the cheapest safe test that could change our mind?*

**Build/play:** teams inspect contacts/connections, use continuity only with facilitator approval, compare to a known-good component where available, and choose repair, donor-part, or salvage-only status.

**Evidence:** `symptom → test → result → decision` repair-log entry.

### Session 4 — Reassemble, hand off, reflect

**Target:** return a device safely or make its future status legible.

**Prompt:** *What should a future steward know before they touch this?*

**Build/play:** teams reassemble when safe, run a final test, label the device, and assemble a short repair dossier or salvage card.

**Evidence:** final function test or documented non-repair decision plus one stewardship recommendation.

## Safety, access, and recovery

Learners may inspect, sort, document, narrate, or manage parts; tool use can be role-based. If a device cannot be opened or is unexpectedly unsafe, switch to a pre-cleared teacher case, an intact exterior study, or a parts-taxonomy task. A responsible decision not to repair earns full credit.

## Assessment and partner description

Observe: names a symptom precisely; preserves parts and evidence; chooses a safe test; distinguishes repair from salvage; writes a handoff another person can use.

**Partner description:** A low-voltage repair studio where learners practice careful diagnosis, documentation, reuse, and honest decisions about what should not be repaired.

## Pilot review after delivery

Record device families that created unsafe ambiguity, the actual device-to-pair ratio, fastener loss, tool bottlenecks, common repair outcomes, and the sourcing route for future cohorts.
