# Listening Log — Sound / Systems / Society

Speakers move people and politics; let’s tune both.  
The listening log trains you to hear not just *sound* but the systems wrapped around it: concrete, wiring, labor, law, neighbors, bass, history.

## What you do

Across the term, you’ll keep a **weekly listening log** (aim for 10–12 entries total). Each entry captures one sound situation from your real life and traces the systems that shape it.

This is not about exotic or “special” sound. It’s about learning to hear:

- the school intercom, the bus PA, the church band, the neighbor’s subwoofer, the cafe playlist, the siren, the fan.

## For each entry, include

### 1. Header

- `Entry #`
- `Date & time`
- `Location` (be as specific as you feel safe being)
- Optional: a quick phone photo or simple sketch of the space/system.

### 2. What you heard (3–5 sentences)

Describe the sound in concrete terms:

- Volume, texture, rhythm, repetition.
- Where it seemed to come from.
- How it moved (static? panning? echoing in a hallway?).

Avoid “nice / pretty / ugly” as your main descriptors—be specific.

### 3. Systems at work (3–6 sentences)

Name at least **one technical system** involved.

- Example: “Cheap Bluetooth speaker on a plastic shelf; school-wide PA; car stereo with aftermarket sub; laptop → interface → powered monitors.”

Name at least **one social / political / economic system** wrapped around it.

- Example: school schedule, policing, zoning, gentrification, worship, nightlife economy, family routines, labor rhythms, platform algorithms.

Connect briefly to course materials when you can:

- “This reminds me of *Dub Echoes*’ focus on how bass travels through walls.”
- “This echoes the argument in *Sound System: The Political Power of Music* about who gets to control volume in public space.”

### 4. You in the mix (2–4 sentences)

- Where is your body in relation to the sound?
- What can you control? What can’t you control?
- How do you feel about this sound (physically, emotionally, politically)?

### 5. One question

End each entry with *one* question you’re left with about:

- the system,
- the politics of the sound,
- or how it might be redesigned.

Examples:

- “Who has the key to the PA closet, and how did they get that power?”
- “What would happen to this neighborhood’s sound if the bar closed?”
- “What does a ‘healthier’ version of this siren sound like?”

## Rhythm and format

- **Frequency:** Aim for **one entry per week**.
- **Length:** Roughly **250–400 words** per entry (not counting headers).
- **Format for submission:**
  - Either a single rolling document (shared online) with clear headings per entry,
  - or a small folder with one `.md` / `.doc` / `.pdf` file per entry.
- You may include links to short recordings if appropriate (30–60 seconds max).

## Assessment (sketch)

You’re graded on **consistency and depth**, not prose perfection.

- **Exceeds expectations**
  - On time; richly detailed sonic description.
  - Clear, thoughtful naming of technical *and* social systems.
  - Regular references to readings / screenings / class discussion.
  - Strong, genuine questions that open further inquiry.
- **Meets expectations**
  - On time; adequate description.
  - At least one system named; some reflection on your own position.
  - Occasional references to course ideas.
- **Below expectations**
  - Irregular or missing entries.
  - Vague descriptions; systems barely addressed.
  - Little evidence of engagement with course materials.

## Access notes

If a sound environment would be unsafe or uncomfortable (volume, crowd, trauma), **you are not required to enter it in person**. Use remote mixes, recordings, documentation, or sound you encounter at a safe distance.
