# Project 1 – Sound System Roots: Lineages, Places, Politics

We don’t start with gear. We start with roots.

For this project you’ll trace the roots of a sound system tradition or scene—how it emerged, who built it, what politics it carried, and how it moved people. Your case might be global or deeply local, but it should connect clearly to our shared materials:

- *Bass Culture* (excerpt),
- Documentary: *Dub Echoes*,
- Blog / zine materials on DIY sound systems,
- *Sound System: The Political Power of Music*.

## Your task

Pick **one** of the following (or propose your own with approval):

- Jamaican reggae / dub sound systems,
- a specific city’s house/techno scene (Chicago, Detroit, Berlin, etc.),
- local DIY punk / noise basement culture,
- a church or gospel sound system tradition,
- a campus or community radio station,
- a mobile DJ / block party culture from your region.

Then produce:

### 1. Written Case Study (1200–1500 words)

Address:

- **Origins and context**
  - Where and when did this sound system tradition emerge?
  - What social/economic conditions shaped it (migration, austerity, racism, youth culture, etc.)?
- **System and space**
  - How did the physical system evolve? (boxes, amps, decks, mics, formats)
  - What kind of spaces did it occupy (street, club, church, yard, basement)?
- **Politics and power**
  - How did it distribute or contest power? (voice, volume, visibility, policing)
  - Who was included or excluded?
- **Connections to our materials**
  - Cite and engage at least **two** of our readings/films/blogs directly.
  - Make at least **one** specific comparison to the arguments in *Sound System: The Political Power of Music*.

### 2. Timeline and Diagram (1 page)

Create a single page that combines:

- A **short timeline** with 5–8 key moments in the tradition’s development.
- A **simple diagram** of a “typical” system from your case:
  - boxes, amps, decks, mics, people, crowd, street/room outline.

This can be hand-drawn and scanned/photographed if neat and legible.

### 3. Three Listening Notes

Choose 3 tracks / recordings tied to this tradition.

For each (150–200 words):

- Describe the sound.
- Explain how it reflects the system and politics you’ve described.

## Access and scope

You are not required to interview anyone or travel. This project can be completed using:

- assigned texts,
- additional research,
- and your own listening.

If you already have lived experience inside a specific tradition, you’re welcome (but not required) to draw on it—be mindful of what you share and protect what needs to stay off the page.

## Assessment (sketch)

- **Research and accuracy** – do you show a grounded understanding of the tradition?
- **Systems thinking** – do you connect gear + space + people + politics?
- **Engagement with readings/media** – are references specific and thoughtful?
- **Clarity and form** – is the case study coherent, properly cited, and readable?
- **Timeline and diagram** – do they make the system legible at a glance?
