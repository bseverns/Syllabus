# Final Project – Community Mix and Gathering Documentation

Speakers move people and politics; let’s tune both, together.

For your final project you will:

- **mix in community** – curate and/or perform a short mix or sonic set,
- **design or inhabit a small PA rig** – building on Project 2,
- **document the gathering** – capturing how the system, space, and people interact,
- and **reflect** on what you learned about sound, systems, and society.

This can be a live event, a staged “studio event,” or a carefully designed remote session, depending on access and resources.

## Core components

### 1. The Mix (5–10 minutes)

A short set that could include:

- tracks from different traditions,
- your own recordings,
- speech, poetry, ambient, field recordings.

You will:

- design the arc (how it starts, rises, falls),
- consider how it lands on your audience (even if small).

### 2. The System and Setting

Either:

- use the tiny system designed in Project 2,
- or adapt an available system (classroom PA, studio monitors, etc.) with intention.

You must:

- diagram your actual final setup (even if it changed),
- note any compromises from your ideal design.

### 3. The Gathering

Minimum: a small audience of humans who are not just you.

- This could be classmates, friends, roommates, etc.

During or after the mix, attend to:

- how people move or don’t move,
- who feels comfortable or not,
- who has control (of volume, playlist, space).

### 4. Documentation

Collect **3–5 forms of documentation**, such as:

- short video clips (10–30 seconds),
- photos of the room and system,
- a recording of the mix (if feasible),
- audience comments or quick written feedback,
- your own field notes taken immediately after.

### 5. Written Reflection and Analysis (1500–2000 words)

Organize your writing into:

#### Part 1 – Narrative

- Describe the event from planning to teardown.
- What did you intend? What actually happened?

#### Part 2 – System analysis

- How did the physical system behave? (strengths, weak spots, surprises)
- How did space, neighbors, rules, and resources shape the event?

#### Part 3 – Politics and community

- Who was centered? Who was missing?
- How did power move through the system (mic, mixer, playlist)?
- How does this connect to our readings (*Bass Culture*, *Sound System*, zines, films, etc.)?

#### Part 4 – Futures

- If you ran this again, what would you change in:
  - the mix,
  - the system design,
  - the invitation and audience?

### 6. Process Log Excerpt

Attach or highlight **5–7 key entries** from your process log:

- early idea,
- system decision shift,
- technical problem + fix,
- access / safety decision,
- post-event debrief.

## Access and safety

- You are not required to throw a large public event.
- Acceptable “gatherings” include:
  - a small in-class listening session,
  - a living-room mix with roommates,
  - a carefully structured online listening with live chat.
- Where volume, crowds, or content would be unsafe or inaccessible:
  - scale down the event,
  - or choose a remote / recorded format.
- Ear protection (for in-person work) and volume ceilings are non-negotiable.

## Ties back to the brief

- **Trace sound system roots** – you are building on what you learned in Project 1.
- **Mix in community** – this is the center of the project.
- **Design small PA rigs** – you inhabit or adapt the tiny system from Project 2.
- **Document gatherings** – your documentation + reflection are graded as heavily as the mix.

## Assessment (sketch)

- **Mix and sonic arc** – is there intention and coherence, even if technically simple?
- **System design and adaptation** – did you make thoughtful choices with what you had?
- **Documentation quality** – do we get a vivid sense of the gathering?
- **Reflection depth** – do you connect experience to systems and politics, not just “it was fun” or “it was stressful”?
- **Use of process log** – is there evidence of development, not just last-minute assembly?
