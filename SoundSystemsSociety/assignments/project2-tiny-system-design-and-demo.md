# Project 2 – Tiny System, Big Stakes: Designing a Small PA

## Connection to the two-week starter arc

- **Week 1:** history and listening party – we look at systems that already exist.
- **Week 2:** build tiny system – we build one.

This project crystallizes that starter arc into a concrete design and a small-scale demo.

## Scenario

You’ve been asked to design a **small, mobile PA system** for a specific community purpose. Examples:

- youth open mic in a community center,
- poetry night in a campus lounge,
- DJ set at a block party on a residential street,
- spoken-word performance at a protest,
- neighborhood movie night needing simple sound.

You may use **real spaces** available to you, or design for a **plausible** one.

## Your task (group or solo, as assigned)

### 1. Site and Use Case Brief (1 page)

Describe:

- the space (dimensions, surfaces, neighbors),
- the audience (age range, size, access needs),
- the primary use (music? speech? mixed?).

Name at least one **constraint**:

- budget, power, neighbors, time, local noise rules, etc.

### 2. System Design (diagram + equipment list)

Produce:

- A **diagram**:
  - Show signal flow: source(s) → mixer/interface → amplifier(s) → speakers.
  - Include power sources, cables, stands, and where people will be.
- An **equipment list** with rough costs:
  - Type of speakers (powered? passive?),
  - Mixer or controller,
  - Microphones and stands,
  - Cables, power strips, safety items.

Indicate what is:

- **Available gear** (from school/department/you),
- **Hypothetical gear** (what you’d acquire if you could).

### 3. Access, Safety, and Politics Notes (1–2 pages)

Explain how you will:

- manage volume and protect hearing (ear protection, volume limits),
- minimize risk to neighbors (orientation, time of day, bass management),
- ensure access (seating, paths, distance to speakers, alternative listening modes).

Then name the **political dynamics** at play:

- Who controls the mic?
- Who can shut the event down?
- How does your design respond?

### 4. Tiny System Demo (in-class)

Using available gear, your group will configure:

- a simplified version of your system (or something analogous with what we have).

In a **5–7 minute demo**:

- introduce the use case and site,
- show how your signal flows,
- play a short snippet (music or speech),
- point out one success and one limitation of the setup.

## Access notes

- You will not be evaluated on owning gear.
- If physical setup is limited, you may:
  - prototype your system in a DAW or diagram only,
  - or use the classroom PA to simulate your design.
- Ear protection will be provided; we will not run unsafe levels in class.

## Assessment (sketch)

- **Fit to site and use case** – does the system make sense for that space and purpose?
- **Clarity of diagram and list** – could someone else reproduce it?
- **Attention to access and safety** – do you take the brief seriously?
- **In-class demo** – is the signal path clear and explained in plain language?
