# Lunch Lab: Sound Logo Sprint
## Make a Tiny Brand Sound from Everyday Objects

## Plain-language definition

**Foley** means making useful sounds with everyday objects. A crumpled paper bag can become fire. Keys can become a cash register, a bike chain, or a tiny robot. A cardboard box can become a door, a package, a drum, or a footstep.

This Lunch Lab does not require participants to know audio production. It asks them to build a short, intentional sound using simple materials, then explain the design choice.

## Defined objective

Participants will create a 5–10 second sound logo using 3–5 recorded object sounds, simple rhythm, silence, and one clear design intention.

## Participant output

Each pair or small team leaves with:

1. a 5–10 second sound logo, or a completed cue sheet if export is not possible;
2. a title;
3. a list of source objects used;
4. one sentence explaining the feeling or message the sound is meant to communicate.

## Recommended Target lunch-break prompt

> Make a 5–10 second sound logo for a moment when a guest finds exactly what they came for.

Other strong prompts:

- Make the sound of a product arriving on time.
- Make the sound of a calm checkout lane.
- Make the sound of a helpful app notification.
- Make the sound of a store opening for the day.
- Make the sound of a team solving a problem quickly.

## Technologies and materials

- phones or Chromebooks
- BandLab, Soundtrap, GarageBand, or a simple browser/phone recorder
- a clearly labeled Foley object kit
- table cards showing what each object can sound like
- optional littleBits/Korg synth kit for one synthetic layer
- headphones or small speakers for review

See `FOLEY_OBJECT_GUIDE.md`, `FOLEY_BIN_SETUP.md`, and `FOLEY_SHOPPING_LIST.csv` for the exact object list.

## Why this works for corporate lunch breaks

This lab connects sound to brand experience, emotional design, accessibility, timing, attention, and communication. Participants are not simply "making noise." They are choosing materials to create a compact signal that has a job.

## Best 45-minute version

Record three sounds and arrange them into a 5-second cue:

1. one **impact** sound;
2. one **texture** sound;
3. one **motion** sound.

## Best 60-minute version

Add:

1. one **synthetic** or tonal sound;
2. one intentional silence;
3. a title card or QR-linked gallery entry.

## Good audience size

- Ideal: 8–16 participants.
- Manageable: 20–30 participants if run in pairs or stations.
- Large-room option: run as a table with timed rotations and one facilitator-operated editing station.

## Facilitation stance

Start with the artifact. Show one finished sound logo. Then show the objects that made it. Avoid assuming that "Foley" is a familiar word. Use the sentence:

> Foley is sound design made with ordinary objects.
