# Facilitator Guide: Sound Logo Sprint

## Lab purpose

Participants create a 5–10 second sound logo using everyday object sounds, rhythm, layering, and editing.

The facilitator's job is to make the activity concrete fast:

> Foley is sound design made with ordinary objects. Today we will turn office and household materials into a tiny brand sound.

## What participants make

A 5–10 second sound logo with:

- a title;
- 3–5 source object sounds;
- a clear emotional or functional intent;
- a completed cue sheet.

## 45-minute run

| Time | Segment | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0:00–0:04 | Show the artifact | Play one finished example. Then show the objects that made it. |
| 0:04–0:08 | Define Foley | Say: "Foley is sound design made with ordinary objects." Point to the labeled bins. |
| 0:08–0:12 | Choose prompt | Teams choose one prompt card and circle a desired feeling. |
| 0:12–0:22 | Record sounds | Each team records one impact, one texture, and one motion sound. |
| 0:22–0:32 | Arrange | Teams place sounds in a simple 5-second order. Silence counts as a design choice. |
| 0:32–0:38 | Name and cue | Teams complete the cue sheet: title, sources, intent. |
| 0:38–0:43 | Lightning share | Play 2–3 examples or read cue sheets if export is slow. |
| 0:43–0:45 | Bridge | Connect the compressed lab to youth sound design, media literacy, and creative confidence. |

## 60-minute run

| Time | Segment | Action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Show the artifact | Play an example and reveal the object recipe. |
| 0:05–0:10 | Orient to bins | Explain impact, texture, motion, voice/gesture, and optional synthetic layer. |
| 0:10–0:15 | Choose prompt | Teams pick a Target/workplace prompt and desired feeling. |
| 0:15–0:28 | Record | Teams record 3–5 sounds. Encourage close recording and quiet pauses. |
| 0:28–0:42 | Arrange and revise | Teams make a first version, listen, then change one thing. |
| 0:42–0:50 | Title and document | Complete cue sheet; export if possible; otherwise save recording names. |
| 0:50–0:56 | Share | Ask: "What object did you use, and what did it become?" |
| 0:56–1:00 | Bridge | Connect to youth learning, volunteer pathways, and follow-up. |

## Required table explanation

Use this exact sentence when introducing the material table:

> The Foley table has five bins. Use one object from the Impact bin, one from the Texture bin, and one from the Motion bin. The other bins are optional.

## Bin categories

1. **Impact** — short hits, clicks, drops, starts, stops.
2. **Texture** — continuous sounds, friction, atmosphere, shimmer, roughness.
3. **Motion** — whooshes, rolling, sliding, zipping, movement through space.
4. **Human / gesture** — clap, snap, breath, small voice, footstep.
5. **Tone / electronic** — optional synth, chime, app-like tone, littleBits/Korg layer.

## Recommended starter recipe

For a first-time team, give them this recipe:

1. Tap a cardboard box once for the beginning.
2. Crinkle tissue paper softly for texture.
3. Slide a zipper bag or fabric pouch for motion.
4. Add a short key jingle at the end.
5. Leave a half-second of silence before the final sound.

## Key vocabulary

- Foley: sound design made with ordinary objects
- Source: the object or action used to make a sound
- Cue: a sound placed at a specific time
- Layer: two or more sounds heard together
- Texture: the surface feeling of a sound
- Impact: a short sound that marks a moment
- Silence: empty time used intentionally
- Iteration: listening once, changing once

## Facilitator warnings

- Do not say "just grab some objects" unless the bins are already clearly labeled.
- Do not over-explain audio editing. The object sounds are the hook.
- Do not let the room become a free-for-all noise cloud. Use short recording windows.
- Ask participants to record close to the object, not across the table.
- Use headphones when possible.

## Closing prompt

> What object surprised you? What did it become after you recorded it?
