# Readings and Media — Imaging Otherwise

This list is a starting point. Tune it to your institution, communities, and technologies.

## From the brief

- **“Against Visual Norms”**  
  - A short text or essay that interrogates mainstream rules of seeing and representation.

- **Legacy Russell – _Glitch Feminism_ (Introduction)**  
  - Frames glitch as a feminist and queer strategy of refusal, error, and opacity.

- **John Berger – _Ways of Seeing_ (Episode 1)**  
  - Classic critique of Western art history, the gaze, and image reproduction.

- **Hydra video toolkit docs**  
  - Practical orientation to a live-coding video environment and its possibilities for imaging otherwise.

## Possible additions by theme

### Visual regimes and critique

- Excerpts from Tina Campt, Ariella Aïsha Azoulay, Hito Steyerl, or similar writers.
- Short texts on photography, race, and power.

### Glitch, error, and refusal

- Additional chapters from _Glitch Feminism_.
- Artist talks or interviews with glitch and net artists.

### Surveillance and machine vision

- Essays on face recognition, dataset construction, and “smart” cameras.
- Documentation of activist interventions into CCTV and biometric systems.

### Archives and missing images

- Writing on community archiving, counter-archives, and absented histories.
- Case studies where communities build their own visual records.

### Access and disability justice

- Writing on alt text, tactile graphics, and accessible imaging.
- Resources from disability justice collectives around visual culture.

## Access notes

- Provide texts in accessible formats (structured headings, alt text, high contrast).
- Caption all video materials; offer transcripts where possible.
- Where visual density or content may be overwhelming, give content notes and alternatives (audio-only, summary, or opt-out).

Students should feel empowered to bring in their own references—artists, memes, games, feeds, family archives—as long as they can situate them in relation to the course’s justice questions.
