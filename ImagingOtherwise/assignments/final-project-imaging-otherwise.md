# Final Project — Imaging Otherwise Apparatus

Images shape truth; this project asks you to warp that truth with care, in public.

Your task is to design and build an **imaging otherwise apparatus** or practice and share it beyond the class. This can be:

- a tool (physical, digital, or hybrid),
- a protocol or performance,
- a set of images and interactions that depend on a specific workflow,
- or a combination of the above.

The key is that it **changes how images are made, seen, or circulated** in ways that respond to questions of justice, access, and power.

## Components

### 1. Apparatus or practice

Design and build an imaging setup that:

- intervenes in at least one visual default (e.g., perspective, beauty, legibility, legibility to machines),
- foregrounds a justice question (representation, surveillance, archive gaps, access, etc.),
- is repeatable or documentable so that others could try it.

Examples (you are not limited to these):

- A DIY scanner ritual for archiving overlooked objects in your living space.
- A Hydra-based live video performance that makes facial recognition fail while foregrounding bodies’ presence.
- A protocol for making and sharing portraits that protects subjects from platform extraction.
- A low-tech “camera” assembled from found lenses, reflective surfaces, and paper, with instructions for others.

### 2. Public share-out

You must share your work beyond just the instructor, in a way that feels safe and appropriate. Options include:

- mini-exhibition or installation in a campus hallway or classroom,
- small screening or live demo,
- online release (class website, social story, small group chat),
- zine or printed booklet distributed to a small audience.

The scale can be modest; the emphasis is on **legibility** and **care** rather than spectacle.

### 3. Documentation package

Prepare a documentation bundle that could live in a public repository or portfolio. Include:

- 6–12 images or stills, and/or short video loops,
- a one-page overview of the apparatus (what it is, how it works, what problem it addresses),
- instructions or a protocol (step-by-step or conceptual) for others to attempt their own version,
- credits and acknowledgements for collaborators, sources, and communities.

### 4. Written reflection (1200–1800 words)

Organize your writing roughly into:

- **Context**  
  - What visual default(s) or problems were you addressing?
  - What readings, media, or artists informed your approach?

- **Process**  
  - How did your idea evolve through studies and experiments?
  - What failed, and what did you change along the way?

- **Apparatus**  
  - Describe your final imaging apparatus or practice in detail.
  - How does it intervene in existing imaging systems?

- **Justice and access**  
  - Who is this work for? Who might be harmed or excluded by it?
  - What choices did you make around consent, visibility, and accessibility?

- **Futures**  
  - How might this project continue beyond the course?
  - What would you change with more time, resources, or collaborators?

### 5. Process log excerpt

Select and submit **8–10 key entries** from your process log that show:

- origin of the idea,
- a major pivot or crisis,
- first working prototype,
- test with others,
- prep for the public share-out,
- post-event reflection.

You can annotate these entries briefly to highlight why they matter.

## Access and safety

- You are not required to expose yourself or others to unsafe spaces, surveillance, or harassment to complete this project.
- If working with self-imaging, you may anonymize or obscure yourself.
- If working with others, obtain clear consent and be open to them withdrawing their images at any time.
- Where possible, build in low-vision and low-hearing access (descriptions, tactile elements, captions, sound options).

## Assessment (sketch)

We look for:

- **Conceptual clarity** — can you state clearly what default you’re intervening in and why?
- **Experimentation and rigor** — did you test, iterate, and reflect, rather than settling on the first idea?
- **Imaging otherwise** — does the apparatus genuinely shift how images are made/seen/circulated, not just apply an aesthetic filter?
- **Justice and access** — are questions of representation, surveillance, disability, and/or consent handled with care?
- **Documentation and openness** — could another person learn and build from what you’ve shared?
