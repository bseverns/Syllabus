# Process Log — Imaging Otherwise

The process log is your running record of how you are unlearning, breaking, rebuilding, and reimagining imaging systems.

It holds:

- tiny experiments,
- technical tests,
- readings that rewired your thinking,
- project pivots,
- conversations and critique notes,
- glitches and failures you decide to keep.

It becomes a core artifact for assessing your work and for sharing it openly.

## What you do

From Week 1 through the final share-out, keep a **process log** documenting any work you do for this course, especially the final project.

Include:

- short notes after each studio session (in or out of class),
- sketches and diagrams,
- screenshots, exports, and print photos,
- reflections on readings and crits that changed your direction.

You may keep this as:

- a digital notebook (Markdown, Docs, Notion, etc.), or
- a hybrid sketchbook, as long as it can be scanned or photographed for submission.

## Structure for each entry

You don’t have to be rigid, but as a guide, each entry might include:

### 1. Header

- Date  
- Related module or assignment (e.g., “Module I – glitch study”, “Final project – apparatus test 02”)  
- Solo or group work?

### 2. Actions (short list)

Concrete things you did, for example:

- “Read the *Glitch Feminism* introduction; highlighted passages on refusal.”
- “Ran 3 scanner passes dragging fabric; changed DPI between passes.”
- “Tried Hydra patch from docs and added my own source chain.”

### 3. Artifacts (attachments or links)

- stills, GIFs, short videos, or prints,
- code snippets (Hydra, JS, etc.),
- photos of physical setups or installations.

Label them clearly (e.g., `glitch-test-03.png`, `hydra-loop-A.mp4`).

### 4. Decisions and questions (3–6 sentences)

- What did you decide today and why?
- What’s not working, and what did you learn from that?
- What questions are you carrying into the next session?

## Expectations

- Aim for **at least 10–12 entries** over the term, with clear clusters around your final project.
- Entries can be short, but they should be specific.
- It is fine—and encouraged—to include failures, false starts, and abandoned directions.
- The log should help someone else understand **how** you built your final work, not just what it looks like at the end.

## Assessment (sketch)

We look for:

- **Consistency** — did you keep showing up in your own process?
- **Specificity** — can we see what you actually tried and changed?
- **Reflection** — do you connect experiments to readings, tools, and justice questions?
- **Openness** — would another artist/researcher be able to learn from this if you shared it?

Polish matters far less than honesty and depth.
