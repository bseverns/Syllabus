# Studies and Experiments — Imaging Otherwise

These short studies give you a scaffold for weekly making. Most are designed to be completed in 1–2 sessions. They are low-stakes and graded primarily for completion and honest risk-taking.

You do **not** need to complete every option here; think of this as a menu that instructors can curate and students can choose from.

Connect each study back to your **process log** with notes and artifacts.

---

## Study 1 — Camera Unlearning

- Work with any camera you have (phone, DSLR, webcam, etc.).
- Intentionally “misuse” at least three defaults:
  - over/under-expose aggressively,
  - break composition rules,
  - shoot while moving the camera wildly,
  - block or partially obscure the lens,
  - use “beauty” or portrait filters in obviously “wrong” contexts.

**Deliverables:**

- 6–12 images.
- A short paragraph (150–250 words) on:
  - which defaults you tried to disrupt,
  - what new problems or possibilities appeared.

---

## Study 2 — Scanner as Time Machine

- Use a flatbed scanner, document camera, or phone-as-scanner.
- Scan the same object or body part multiple times while changing:
  - motion during the scan (drag, twist, tilt),
  - resolution,
  - color / grayscale settings,
  - what touches the glass (fabric, plastic, skin, paper).

**Deliverables:**

- 4–8 scans showing clearly different temporal/spatial effects.
- A short note on how the scanner sees time and touch differently than a camera.

---

## Study 3 — Glitch as Question

- Take one “ordinary” image (any subject).
- Run it through at least two different glitch processes, for example:
  - re-saving at extreme JPEG compression,
  - opening the file in a text editor and deleting/rearranging chunks,
  - feeding it into a glitch tool or script.

**Deliverables:**

- Original + at least 3 glitch variants.
- A 150–250 word reflection on what the image seems to gain/lose, and how this relates to ideas from *Glitch Feminism* or other readings.

---

## Study 4 — Seeing With and Without Faces

- Capture or appropriate a small set of images that normally center faces.
- Produce a transformed set where faces are:
  - obscured, blurred, removed, or replaced,
  - or where context replaces facial identity as the point of the image.

**Deliverables:**

- Before/after set (at least 4 pairs).
- A short reflection on how identity, recognition, and care shift between the two.

---

## Study 5 — Hydra / Live Video Sketch

- Using Hydra (or a similar live video tool), start from a basic example patch.
- Modify it to incorporate:
  - a live camera source, or
  - your own recorded material,
  - plus at least two transformations (feedback, color shifts, layering).

**Deliverables:**

- One short captured loop (10–30 seconds) or a few stills.
- A note about:
  - what parameters you controlled,
  - how it felt to work with a live, code-based apparatus.

---

## Study 6 — Imaging Access

- Pick a visual artifact you care about (your own work or someone else’s).
- Create at least two **alternative ways of experiencing it**:
  - tactile diagram or relief drawing,
  - text-only description,
  - sonification / audio description,
  - low-contrast or simplified version.

**Deliverables:**

- Original + 2 alternatives.
- A reflection on what each version emphasizes or erases, and who each might serve.

---

You or your instructor can add additional studies to this file over time, tuned to specific tools, readings, or local contexts. Treat this as a living menu, not a fixed checklist.
