# Tuning Tiny Whoops: A Betaflight Configurator Workshop

## Overview

**Length:** 4 sessions (recommended 90–120 minutes each)  
**Audience:** High school students with access to tiny whoop–class quadcopters (AIO FC/ESC)  
**Focus:** Using **Betaflight Configurator** as the primary tool for understanding and shaping how the quad flies.

The workshop walks students from “I can plug it in” to “I can read and shape my whoop’s behavior.”  
We move in this order:

1. Safety, hardware anatomy, and first connection  
2. Radio mapping, arming, and flight modes  
3. Rates, presets, OSD, and test flights  
4. Profiles, troubleshooting, and flight–readiness rituals

## Assumptions & Prerequisites

Students:

- Can operate a laptop comfortably (installing software, basic USB troubleshooting)
- Have a tiny whoop–class quad that can run Betaflight firmware
- Have a bound (or bindable) radio transmitter
- Have at least a casual interest in flying FPV or line–of–sight

Each student should bring:

- Tiny whoop with AIO FC/ESC  
- Radio transmitter  
- 2–4 charged 1S LiPo batteries  
- A **data–capable** USB cable for their flight controller  

The classroom provides:

- Laptops or desktops with a Chromium–based browser
- Access to the Betaflight Configurator app or web version
- A safe indoor flying area (even a small gym or classroom with a simple track)
- Extra USB cables and a few spare whoops if possible

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the workshop, students will be able to:

1. **Connect & Back Up**
   - Connect a tiny whoop to Betaflight and export a configuration backup.

2. **Map Controls Safely**
   - Verify radio channels, set up arming and flight modes, and test motors safely with props off.

3. **Shape Flight Behavior**
   - Adjust basic rate, expo, and mode settings and explain how they affect the “feel” of the quad.

4. **Read the Aircraft**
   - Use on–screen information and simple tab checks (Setup, Receiver, Modes, Motors, OSD) to diagnose basic issues.

5. **Operate with Intention**
   - Follow a written pre–flight checklist and articulate simple safety practices for small drones.

## Session Sketch

### Session 1 — Ground School & First Connection

- Safety culture and expectations  
- Anatomy of a tiny whoop (frame, AIO FC/ESC, motors, receiver, VTX, battery)  
- Installing / opening Betaflight Configurator  
- Connecting and exporting a first backup  
- Verifying FC orientation in the Setup tab

**Milestone:** Each student has connected their whoop and saved at least one config backup.

---

### Session 2 — Radio, Modes & Arming

- Binding / checking the radio link  
- Receiver tab: channel mapping, endpoints, AUX channels  
- Modes tab: arming switch, beginner–friendly flight modes  
- Safe motor testing in the Motors tab (props off)

**Milestone:** Each student can safely arm/disarm (props off) and show correct stick motion in Betaflight.

---

### Session 3 — Feel & Feedback: Rates, Presets & OSD

- Using a sensible tiny–whoop preset (if available for your target)  
- Rates and expo as “character controls”  
- Creating at least two rate profiles (mild vs spicy)  
- OSD basics: voltage, timer, and basic warning elements  
- Short test flights on a simple indoor track

**Milestone:** Each student can feel the difference between two rate profiles and see battery voltage on screen while flying.

---

### Session 4 — Profiles, Troubleshooting & Flight Readiness

- Saving a “final” backup and recording key settings  
- Reading arming flags and basic error messages  
- Troubleshooting clinic with common whoop issues  
- Writing and practicing a personal pre–flight checklist  
- Optional: short demo flights or time trials

**Milestone:** Each student leaves with a whoop that arms safely, responds predictably, shows useful OSD info, and has at least one documented backup.

## Assessment & Evidence of Learning

This workshop leans on **demonstration and reflection** more than grades.

Possible assessment tools:

- **Checklists** – students complete and sign off on their own pre–flight checklist.
- **Exit tickets** – short written reflections at the end of each session (e.g., “One setting I changed and what it did”).
- **Demonstrations** – each student shows a safe arm/disarm sequence and a simple flight.

## Adaptation Notes

- For shorter blocks (e.g., 60 minutes), split each session into A/B halves and run the workshop over 6–8 meetings.
- For more experienced pilots, compress Sessions 1–2 and spend more time on advanced tuning and blackbox logging.
- For beginners with no flight experience, add a simulator warm–up each session before touching real quads.
