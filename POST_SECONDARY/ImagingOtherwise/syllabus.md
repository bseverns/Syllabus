# Imaging Otherwise

**Tagline:** Images shape truth; let's warp them with care.

- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Credits:** 3
- **Format:** Studio–seminar (discussion + lab)
- **Keywords:** image, justice, experiment

## Course description

Images do not simply represent the world; they help build it. Every camera setting, compression algorithm, and interface choice smuggles in assumptions about whose bodies are visible, what counts as evidence, and how “good” pictures should look.

This course invites students to **image otherwise**: to question visual defaults, build alternative imaging tools and workflows, and craft speculative visuals that point toward more just possible worlds. We will move between critical reading, close looking, hands-on experiments (from scanners to live-coded video), and iterative project work. Along the way, we will keep asking: **who is this image for, what system is it part of, and what might it become if we warp it with care?**

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the semester, students will be able to:

1. **Question visual defaults** in mainstream imaging pipelines (cameras, scanners, phones, platforms, datasets), articulating their historical and political lineages.
2. **Build alternative imaging tools and workflows** using accessible hardware and software (e.g., DIY scanners, camera misuses, Hydra or similar live video tools).
3. **Craft speculative visuals** that imagine different ways of seeing and being seen, grounded in critical frameworks about race, gender, class, and disability.
4. **Share research openly**, producing process logs, experiments, and a final project that are documented for others to learn from and build on.
5. **Reflect on access and justice**, designing imaging practices that attend to who can perceive, participate, and consent, including tactile and low-vision options.

These outcomes rest directly on the original brief’s goals: question visual defaults, build alt imaging tools, craft speculative visuals, and share research openly.

## Format and rhythm

- One 3-hour meeting per week for 14 weeks (or two shorter blocks per week).
- Each week typically includes:
  - a short seminar on the readings / media,
  - a technical or conceptual demo,
  - studio time for experiments or project work,
  - brief crits or share-outs.

## Assessment mix (suggested)

- Crits and participation (in critique, discussion, and lab) — **30%**
- Process Log — **30%**
- Final project + public share-out (including documentation) — **40%**

Weekly studies and experiments are graded primarily on completion and honest risk-taking rather than polish.

## Required work (headline)

- **Process Log** — ongoing documentation of experiments, tests, and project decisions.
- **Studies and experiments** — short weekly imaging exercises, often done in-class.
- **Final project** — an “imaging otherwise apparatus” or practice, plus public share-out.
- **Final documentation** — process log excerpts, visual artifacts, and a written reflection.

See `assignments/` for detailed briefs.

## Two-week starter arc

The course opens with a compact arc that sets the tone for questioning vision and building alternative tools.

### Week 1 — Deconstructing camera norms

- Watch or discuss: *Ways of Seeing* (episode 1) and a short text such as “Against Visual Norms”.
- Group exercise: collectively map the defaults embedded in a smartphone camera app (aspect ratio, face detection, “beauty” filters, HDR, auto white balance, etc.).
- Studio: “Unlearning the camera” experiment (shoot with “wrong” settings, block the lens, misuse exposure).
- Introduce the **Process Log** and studies menu.

### Week 2 — DIY scanner experiments

- Look at examples of scanner art, glitch practices, and flatbed-misuse projects.
- Technical demo: low-tech capture (flatbed scanners, document cameras, photocopiers, phone-as-scanner).
- Studio: “scanner as vehicle” exercises — dragging objects, bodies, and surfaces across time.
- Introduce the **Final Project** at a high level and talk about what “imaging otherwise” might mean in their own practices.

## Week-by-week outline (sketch)

### Module I — Unlearning the Camera (Weeks 1–4)

- **Week 1:** Deconstructing camera norms  
  - Visual regimes, perspective, exposure, framing; *Ways of Seeing*; “Against Visual Norms”.  
  - Camera-unlearning study; first process-log entry.

- **Week 2:** DIY scanners and flat imaging  
  - Scanner experiments; Hydra / live video intro as time permits.  
  - Experiments with motion, time, and the slice of the scanner bed.

- **Week 3:** Glitch, error, and refusal  
  - Read from *Glitch Feminism* (intro) and look at artists who use error as method.  
  - Exercises in intentional corruption (compression, datamosh, mis-decoding).

- **Week 4:** Surfaces, skins, and screens  
  - How images cling to walls, feeds, faces; display technologies and their biases.  
  - Small critique of Module I studies; identify patterns and interests.

### Module II — Imaging and Justice (Weeks 5–9)

- **Week 5:** Archives and missing images  
  - Whose histories are preserved, misrepresented, or absent?  
  - Exercise: annotate an institutional image archive or stock library.

- **Week 6:** Surveillance and “smart” vision  
  - Face recognition, datasets, predictive policing, content moderation.  
  - Map a single camera or dataset as a system (inputs, outputs, harms, resistances).

- **Week 7:** Self-imaging, identity, and refusal  
  - Social media self-portraiture, filters, anonymity, opacity.  
  - Exercises in self-imaging that resist extraction and platform metrics.

- **Weeks 8–9:** Midterm mini-crits and project seeding  
  - Students share a cluster of experiments from Modules I–II.  
  - Begin forming directions for the **Final Project**; sketch early proposals.

### Module III — Imaging Otherwise (Weeks 10–14)

- **Week 10:** Apparatus design  
  - What is an “apparatus” in imaging (hardware, software, protocol, social script)?  
  - Students design initial scaffolds for their imaging otherwise apparatus/practice.

- **Week 11:** Building and iterating  
  - Studio-heavy week; 1–2 short pin-up crits focused on function and ethics.

- **Week 12:** Testing with others  
  - Run small tests with peers or invited guests.  
  - Collect feedback and refine apparatus, interaction, consent practices.

- **Week 13:** Public share-out  
  - Mini-exhibition, screening, performance, or web-based release.  
  - Can be low-key but should be legible to people beyond the class.

- **Week 14:** Reflection and futures  
  - Final crit and discussion; written reflection due.  
  - What practices will students carry forward to “image otherwise” in their own fields?

## Access and justice notes

From the brief: darkroom alternatives, screen readers supported, quiet viewing rooms, tactile diagrams. In practice, this means:

- Offering **quiet viewing rooms** or low-stimulation options when screening or critiquing dense visual material.
- Designing materials to be **screen-reader compatible** (alt text, structured headings, high-contrast documents).
- Providing **tactile diagrams** or physical models when possible for key concepts (camera layouts, pipelines, interfaces).
- Allowing students to choose lower-brightness, lower-saturation, or text/audio-based alternatives for some assignments when visual overload is a concern.

Access is not separate from imaging; it is part of how we image otherwise.
