# Instructor Notes — Imaging Otherwise

Notes for running and adapting this course.

## From brief to stack

Original brief (v0.1, 2024-05-29) emphasized: question visual defaults, build alt imaging tools, craft speculative visuals, share research openly, with a two-week starter arc (camera norms + DIY scanners), and an assessment mix centered on crits, process log, and public share-out plus access notes (darkroom alternatives, screen readers, quiet viewing rooms, tactile diagrams).

This stack keeps those as the spine, and adds:

- weekly structure,
- a menu of studies,
- a more detailed final project brief.

## Assessment mix (aligned to the brief)

Minimal-viable structure:

- Crits / participation — 30%
- Process Log — 30%
- Final Project + public share-out (incl. documentation) — 40%

You can add small numeric weights for studies if your institution requires it, but they work well as pass/fail or check/plus assignments.

## Pacing ideas

- Weeks 1–4: prioritize experiments over polished outcomes.
- Weeks 5–7: anchor justice questions (archives, surveillance, identity).
- Weeks 8–9: midterm sharing and project seeding; don’t overload reading.
- Weeks 10–13: treat as a rolling lab with short check-ins rather than long lectures.
- Week 14: leave more room than you think you need for reflection and admin.

## Tooling ranges

You can run this course in multiple tool configurations:

1. **Low-tech / analog-heavy**
   - Cameras, scanners, photocopiers, projectors, printouts.
   - Hydra and other software are optional extras.

2. **Laptop-heavy**
   - Hydra, p5.js, simple Python or JS scripts, basic photo/video editors.
   - Emphasize code-based and interface-based imaging.

3. **Hybrid lab**
   - Combine both; let students choose their main medium and cross-pollinate in crits.

The key is not the specific software but that students see the **pipeline** and its defaults.

## Access practices

- Always have a quieter space or alternative viewing option (smaller screens, lower brightness, printed stills).
- Normalize breaks during screen-heavy sessions.
- Encourage and support students who wish to work in lower-contrast, text-first, or tactile modes.
- Make accessibility a design constraint of the final project, not an afterthought.

## Crit culture

Because images are often tied to identity and lived experience, set up critique norms early:

- Ask what kind of feedback each student wants before starting.
- Allow “no group crit” or “small group only” options for sensitive work.
- Model critique that speaks to systems and choices, not to bodies.

## Sharing openly

The brief calls out sharing research openly. That can mean:

- class-only shared drive or repo,
- a small, invite-only website or PDF,
- contributions to an institutional collection or zine rack.

Clarify IP and consent:

- Students retain rights to their work.
- Sharing beyond the class is opt-in and revocable.
- If hosting code or images in public repos, discuss licensing.

Treat this stack as versionable; update `reading-media.md`, tweak assignments, and adjust pacing as you learn what “imaging otherwise” looks like with each cohort.
