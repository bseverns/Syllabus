# Sound / Systems / Society

**Tagline:** Speakers move people and politics; let's tune both.

## Course description

Sound is never “just” sound: it is carried by cables, concrete, servers, zoning laws, labor, and power. This course listens through loudspeakers and headphones to the systems that make them possible—technical systems (signal chains, codecs, networks), social systems (institutions, infrastructures, labor), and ecological systems (materials, land, noise, extraction).

Students move between theory and practice: reading in sound studies and media theory, diagramming actual sound systems, building small sonic interventions, and conducting fieldwork in the soundscapes they inhabit. By the end of the course, students will be able to analyze how sound infrastructures shape social life—and design thoughtful, situated responses.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the semester, students will be able to:

1. **Trace sound system roots** across at least one tradition, scene, or city, linking sonic practice to history and politics.
2. **Use listening as method**, collecting, annotating, and reflecting on field recordings and everyday sound situations.
3. **Diagram and model signal chains**, explaining technical and social flows in PAs, radios, streams, and other systems.
4. **Design small PA rigs** tailored to specific spaces, communities, and constraints, with attention to safety and access.
5. **Mix in community**, designing short sets or listening sessions and reflecting on how they land with real audiences.
6. **Document gatherings** as entangled technical and social events, producing written, visual, and sonic records.
7. **Reflect critically** on their own positionality as listeners, producers, and system designers.

## Format and credits

- 3-credit upper-division course.
- Recommended format: one weekly 3-hour meeting (seminar + lab), for 14 weeks.
- Alternative: two 80–90 minute meetings per week; sessions below can be split accordingly.

## Assessment mix (suggested)

- Participation & presence — 15%
- Listening Log — 15%
- Project 1: Sound System Roots Case Study — 15%
- Project 2: Tiny System Design & Demo — 20%
- Project 3: Community Mix & Gathering Documentation (Final Project) — 25%
- Final portfolio & reflection — 10%

## Required work (headline)

- **Listening Log** — weekly entries that document and analyze everyday sound situations.
- **Process Log** — ongoing documentation for Projects 1–3.
- **Project 1** — Sound system roots case study (history, politics, listening notes).
- **Project 2** — Tiny system design & demo (small PA for a specific space and community).
- **Project 3** — Community mix & gathering documentation (final project).
- **Final portfolio & reflection** — curated revisions and a written synthesis.

## Two-week starter arc

The course opens with a compact arc that sets the tone:

- **Week 1: History and listening party**
  - Read selections from sound system history (e.g., Jamaican dub, diasporic scenes).
  - Watch and discuss excerpts from the documentary *Dub Echoes*.
  - Guided listening party: focus on bass, space, power, and the physicality of sound.
  - Introduce the Listening Log and Project 1.

- **Week 2: Build tiny system**
  - In-lab dissection of a small PA (or classroom system).
  - Students cooperatively wire up a tiny rig: source → mixer → amp → speakers.
  - Discussion of ear protection, neighbors, building policies, and volume ceilings.
  - Introduce Project 2 (Tiny System Design & Demo).

Subsequent weeks pull outward into platform infrastructures, labor and venue politics, and designed interventions.

## Week-by-week outline (sketch)

### Module I – Listening to Systems (Weeks 1–4)

- **Week 1 – Sound, systems, society**
  - Concepts: sound as infrastructure; basics of systems thinking.
  - Activities: soundwalk; in-class discussion; course overview.
- **Week 2 – Listening as method / build tiny system**
  - Concepts: deep listening, ethics of recording, safe levels.
  - Activities: watch *Dub Echoes* excerpt; wire a tiny PA; introduce Project 2.
- **Week 3 – Signal chains and technical systems**
  - Concepts: signal flow; gain staging; analog vs digital paths.
  - Activities: diagram a classroom PA or studio; short hands-on routing exercises.
- **Week 4 – Mapping personal soundscapes**
  - Concepts: sonic autoethnography; home/commute/work as systems.
  - Activities: workshop and critique for Project 1; focus on narrative and diagram.

### Module II – Sound, Power, and Infrastructure (Weeks 5–9)

- **Week 5 – Speakers, sirens, and control**
  - Concepts: sound and policing; emergency alerts; school bells; protest sound.
  - Activities: case studies; local noise ordinance exploration.
- **Week 6 – Labor, venues, and invisible work**
  - Concepts: who runs the board; technical riders; venues as infrastructures.
  - Activities: guest sound engineer or AV tech (if available); Q&A.
- **Week 7 – Platforms, streams, and servers**
  - Concepts: compression, streaming, recommendation systems.
  - Activities: trace a track from artist to listener across platforms.
- **Weeks 8–9 – System diagram teach-in**
  - Activities: student mini-lectures built around their Project 2 systems.
  - Synthesis of patterns across sites and systems.

### Module III – Designing Interventions (Weeks 10–14)

- **Week 10 – From critique to proposal**
  - Concepts: interventions vs extraction; ethics and consent.
  - Activities: form groups; propose final projects; feedback on scope.
- **Week 11 – Building prototypes**
  - Activities: lab build sessions (mix design, rig configuration, signage, zines).
- **Week 12 – Testing in context**
  - Activities: test events or simulations; documentation gathering.
- **Week 13 – Community mix and share-out**
  - Activities: final mix sessions; in-class festival or showcase; invited guests.
- **Week 14 – Reflection and futures**
  - Activities: portfolio presentations; written synthesis; course debrief.

## Access and safety notes

- Ear protection is required to be available any time we use amplified sound.
- Students may choose lower-volume or headphone-based listening where needed.
- No one is required to enter spaces that are unsafe or inaccessible to them.
  - Remote mixes, pre-recorded materials, and controlled listening environments are acceptable alternatives.
- We will establish community agreements around:
  - volume and fatigue,
  - consent for recording,
  - and respectful engagement with different sonic backgrounds and sensitivities.
