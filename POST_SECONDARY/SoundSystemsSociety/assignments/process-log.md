# Process Log — Building and Moving Systems

The listening log tracks what the world sounds like to you.  
The **process log** tracks what you do to *change* that world: research, sketches, wiring diagrams, test mixes, conversations, failures.

Your final project and tiny-system work should leave a trail. This is that trail.

## What you do

From Week 1 through your final project, keep a **process log** documenting any work you do *for projects in this class*:

- research into sound system histories,
- viewing or rewatching *Dub Echoes* and taking notes,
- drafting diagrams for a small PA,
- troubleshooting hum in a powered speaker,
- planning a community listening session,
- debriefing after a mix demo.

Each entry should include:

### 1. Header

- Date  
- What project it relates to (`P1`, `P2`, `P3`)  
- Solo or group work?

### 2. Actions (bullet list)

Concrete things you did, for example:

- “Watched 35 minutes of *Dub Echoes* and took notes.”
- “Sketched two tiny-PA layouts for a community room.”
- “Tested how far bass carries outside the space; neighbors heard it at X.”

### 3. Artifacts

Attach or link to:

- photos of whiteboard diagrams,
- screenshots of DAW sessions,
- sketches of room layout,
- quick phone recordings of before/after changes.

### 4. Decisions and questions (3–5 sentences)

- What did you decide today and why?
- What’s not working?
- What questions are you carrying into the next work session?

## Expectations

- Aim for **at least 8–10 entries** over the term, spread across the major projects.
- Entries can be short, but must be specific.
- This log is both:
  - a tool for you (to remember what you actually did), and
  - evidence during final evaluation that your projects developed over time.

The log matters for the final portfolio and reflection.
