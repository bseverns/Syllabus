# Swarm Aesthetics: Schedule

This schedule assumes a 12-session studio, roughly 2 to 3 hours per meeting.

## Arc

### Week 1: Swarms & Systems

- focus: systems language, distributed behavior, course framing
- artifact: first-pass system map

### Week 2: Reading Perceptual Drift

- focus: close reading, redraws, and annotation as design method
- artifact: annotated reading response + revised system diagram

### Week 3: Recipes & Roles

- focus: how instructions, constraints, and roles produce collective behavior
- artifact: first drift recipe + role-card draft

### Week 4: Telemetry as Material

- focus: signals, state, observability, and what data does aesthetically
- artifact: telemetry semantics sheet + source inventory

### Week 5: Mappings, Assumptions, Ethics

- focus: what gets measured, how it gets transformed, and who gets framed
- artifact: mapping sketch + ethics memo

### Week 6: Prototype 0 - Screen / Tabletop Swarm

- focus: low-risk prototype proving behavior, timing, and legibility
- artifact: prototype video or photo set + test notes

### Week 7: Designing a Drift

- focus: turning a prototype into a structured public-facing event
- artifact: final project proposal + production plan

### Week 8: Rehearsal Lab

- focus: timing, transitions, failure modes, and cueing
- artifact: rehearsal run sheet + revision log

### Week 9: Interfaces, Signage, Scripts

- focus: audience framing, operator instructions, and event legibility
- artifact: signage draft + facilitator or operator script

### Week 10: Event / Internal Showing

- focus: install, run, observe, and document the event
- artifact: internal showing + raw documentation capture

### Week 11: Debrief & Documentation

- focus: what actually happened, what failed, and what the evidence shows
- artifact: documentation bundle draft + debrief memo

### Week 12: Final Crit & Futures

- focus: critique, reflection, transfer, and next-step framing
- artifact: final portfolio submission

## Minimum teaching logic

The course should move through four phases:

1. understand the system
2. encode behavior in recipes, roles, and mappings
3. prototype and rehearse under controlled conditions
4. show, document, and critique with evidence
