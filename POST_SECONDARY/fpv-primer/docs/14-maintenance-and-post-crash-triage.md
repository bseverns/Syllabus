# Maintenance and post-crash triage

Treat maintenance as part of systems literacy, not janitorial work after the “real” lesson.

## After any meaningful crash

1. Disarm.
2. Approach safely.
3. Disconnect power if needed.
4. Inspect before any relaunch decision.

## Minimum triage checks

### Mechanical

- props chipped or bent?
- frame cracked?
- ducts damaged on whoop-class builds?
- camera angle shifted?
- antenna loose?

### Electrical

- battery lead strained?
- connector hot or damaged?
- exposed conductor?

### Propulsion

- motors spin freely by hand?
- hair or debris in bells or shafts?
- any roughness or grit?

### Configuration confidence

- if the crash included unexplained behavior, was the root cause actually identified?

If not, do not relaunch just because the craft still arms.

## Air65-style note

On a small whoop baseline:

- props are consumables
- ducts often show the first structural damage
- canopy shift can make FPV feel “wrong” before the pilot sees obvious breakage
- hair in motors is common enough to make it part of the standard inspection

## Teaching rule

Students should be able to say:

- what they inspected
- what looked normal
- what looked suspicious
- why they chose to relaunch or ground the rig

That verbal discipline matters as much as the physical inspection itself.
