# U.S. operations and radio-compliance note

**Official-source check:** 2026-07-19. Recheck the linked FAA and FCC pages before each delivery; requirements and operating interpretations can change.

This primer is not legal advice. It is a teaching note for post-secondary programs that may move from bench to field work.

## Why this belongs in the primer

FPV literacy is not just:

- motors
- PIDs
- rates
- telemetry

It is also:

- where you are flying
- under what operating framework
- what radio equipment you are using
- who is responsible for the decision to launch

## Indoor vs outdoor

For a whoop-class teaching rig such as an Air65:

- **indoor work** is the simplest teaching environment
- **outdoor work** adds FAA and site-planning concerns immediately

Do not treat “just outside the building” as a trivial extension of the bench or classroom.

## FAA teaching note

At the time of this update:

- outdoor recreational flyers need **TRUST**
- drones under **250 g** flown recreationally generally do not require registration
- drones that are required to be registered, or are registered, must comply with **Remote ID**

For educational institutions, the operational category is not always automatically “recreational.” If your program is structured, supervised, or institutionally organized, settle that framework before field sessions happen.

## FCC teaching note

Analog FPV video gear and some control equipment carry FCC obligations that are separate from FAA flight rules.

In practice, that means:

- use known compliant gear
- do not casually raise VTX power because “the signal is weak”
- understand whether any operation assumptions depend on licensed amateur bands
- keep classroom channel plans explicit

## Program rule

Bench-first and indoor-first are not signs of timidity. They are how you keep a post-secondary program rigorous enough to survive contact with real hardware.
