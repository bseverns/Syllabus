# Syllabus — Maintenance, Repair & Infrastructures Studio

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners make hidden upkeep legible and test a small intervention that supports continuity rather than only novelty.
- **Group / staffing:** 8–16 learners; one lead. A site steward joins only with consent and clear time boundaries.
- **Final share:** a maintenance map, low-risk prototype, handoff document, and postmortem.
- **Agreement:** *No intervention is helpful until the people who maintain it can use it.*

## Materials and preflight

Observation/consent sheets; clipboards; cameras or drawing materials; large paper; sticky notes; mapping templates; prototype materials (paper, labels, folders, low-fi signage); and one permissioned site/process or prepared case.

**Preflight:** name a site steward or use a non-live case. Confirm what learners may observe, photograph, ask about, and alter. Do not expose personal work records, access codes, security details, blame individuals, or touch active equipment/processes without authorization. Prepare a classroom case study fallback.

## 110-minute session rhythm

15-minute check-in; 20-minute case/field-method demonstration; 45-minute observation or studio work; 10-minute reset; 15-minute critique; 5-minute documentation parking.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — See the maintenance system

**Target:** recognize people, routines, dependencies, and choke points behind an ordinary service or space.

**Prompt:** *What only becomes visible when it fails?*

**Build/play:** learners observe a bounded process or case, then make a first steward/dependency map.

**Evidence:** field notes distinguishing direct observation from inference.

### Session 2 — Make labor and breakdown legible

**Target:** identify one recurring friction without assigning individual blame.

**Prompt:** *Where does continuity depend on hidden work?*

**Build/play:** teams create a maintenance timeline, breakdown log, or handoff map and test the account with a peer/site steward where possible.

**Evidence:** revised map and one stated uncertainty.

### Session 3 — Prototype for continuity

**Target:** make a small, authorized support tool: checklist, label, orientation card, parts map, or handoff prompt.

**Prompt:** *What could make the next person's work clearer or lighter?*

**Build/play:** teams make a low-fi intervention and define a test with a real or simulated user.

**Evidence:** prototype, intended user, test question, and authority boundary.

### Session 4 — Test, hand off, postmortem

**Target:** revise a support tool from evidence and state what it cannot solve.

**Prompt:** *Did this reduce confusion, or only describe it beautifully?*

**Build/play:** learners conduct a bounded user test, revise, and present the tool with a maintenance/handoff note.

**Evidence:** user-test record, final prototype, limitation, and next steward action.

## Access, safety, and recovery

Offer roles for observing, mapping, interviewing, writing, drawing, and testing. A learner can use the prepared case rather than a live site. If site contact fails, test the handoff tool with another team acting as future stewards. Do not equate aesthetic documentation with operational change.

## Assessment and partner description

Observe: notices system dependencies; distinguishes evidence from assumption; identifies a bounded maintenance friction; tests a usable support tool; names handoff and limitation clearly.

**Partner description:** A systems studio where learners study the care that keeps rooms and tools alive, then prototype practical ways to make that work more visible and survivable.
