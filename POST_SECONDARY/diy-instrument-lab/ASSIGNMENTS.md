# DIY Instrument Lab (College) – Assignments & Evaluation

Suggested weighting (tune to your program):

- Participation & lab practice – ~20%  
- Weekly-ish studies (small builds, listening, and logs) – ~20%  
- Prototype 0 instrument + midterm documentation – ~20%  
- Final instrument/system + performance/installation – ~25%  
- Final documentation bundle + individual reflection – ~15%  

---

## 1. Participation & Lab Practice (~20%)

Ongoing across Weeks 1–14.

Includes:

- Attendance and active participation in listening, discussion, and crits  
- Safe and respectful use of shared tools and bench space  
- Willingness to help troubleshoot peers’ instruments and share knowledge  
- Bringing in-progress work to class (not waiting until the night before crit)

You can define a simple rubric (e.g., **Prepared / In Progress / Absent**) and share it in your syllabus or LMS.

---

## 2. Weekly-ish Studies (~20%)

Short assignments that build skills and a vocabulary of techniques. Examples (tune to your semester):

- **Study A – Listening Log (Weeks 1–2)**  
  - Keep a short log of 5–10 instrument recordings or performances.  
  - For each: what makes this *this* instrument? Interface, sound, context?

- **Study B – Contact Mic + Sound Walk (Week 2)**  
  - Build a contact mic (or use a shared lab one).  
  - Record or document at least 5 surfaces/objects you’ve “plugged into”.  
  - Short write-up: what surprised you? What felt instrument-like?

- **Study C – Oscillator / Noise Box Sketch (Weeks 3–4)**  
  - Breadboard a simple oscillator or noise circuit.  
  - Document settings or component swaps that create distinct “voices”.

- **Study D – Interface Gesture Map (Week 5)**  
  - Draw your ideal instrument faceplate for a hypothetical box.  
  - Label each control with gesture type (twist, push, tilt, squeeze, etc.) and intended sonic effect.

- **Study E – Sensor Experiment (Week 6)**  
  - Prototype a one-sensor control patch (e.g., LDR → pitch, FSR → volume).  
  - Bring a short demo and 1-page mapping notes.

- **Study F – Microcontroller MIDI Test (Week 7)**  
  - Use an Arduino-like board to send at least two MIDI CCs or notes to a DAW or softsynth.  
  - Screenshot / diagram your routing.

Not all of these have to be graded with fine granularity; pass/fail with feedback often works well.

---

## 3. Prototype 0 Instrument + Midterm Documentation (~20%)

**Due Week 8 – Prototype 0 Crit**

A first full-pass instrument that is:

- Physically playable by at least one person in the room  
- Capable of at least two distinct sound states or voices  
- Safe and reasonably robust for a short demo

This can be:

- A contact-mic-based object with feedback routing  
- A simple noise box with a few controls  
- A controller-only device that drives a softsynth or sampler

**Deliverables:**

1. **Prototype 0 Demo**  
   - Live demo in class (3–5 minutes).  
   - Show how the player interacts with it and what sounds/states are possible.

2. **Prototype 0 Brief (2–3 pages or equivalent)**  
   - Concept: what is this instrument for? Who is it for?  
   - Signal path: block diagram of sound flow (input → processing → output).  
   - Interface: annotated photo or sketch of control surface and gestures.  
   - Build notes: main parts, biggest build problems, what broke.  
   - Next steps: what you would refine or re-think for the final.

## 4. Final Instrument / System + Performance or Installation (~25%)

**Spans Weeks 10–13**

A more developed instrument or instrument system, solo or collaborative. Possibilities include:

- A self-contained electronic instrument with speakers and controls  
- A controller for external synths or software, with a clearly framed performance practice  
- A small ensemble of interlinked devices designed to be played together  
- An instrument + environment pairing (e.g., site-specific or installation format)

Requirements:

- **Playability:** at least one coherent way to play the instrument that you can demonstrate.  
- **Range:** multiple states/voices or dynamic range in its response.  
- **Design care:** thought given to interface, ergonomics, accessibility, and robustness.  
- **Context:** conceptually grounded by at least one reading or reference artist/practice.  
- **Public moment:** presented in performance, demo session, or small public/department event (depending on your site).

You can decide whether these are solo or group projects and set size constraints to match your resources.

---

## 5. Final Documentation Bundle + Individual Reflection (~15%)

### Group / Project bundle (~10%)

One shared submission per project including:

- Title, credits, and short abstract  
- Concept narrative (1–2 pages)  
- Signal-path diagram(s) and interface diagrams (annotated photos or drawings)  
- Parts list / BOM (does not need exact vendor SKUs, but enough to be reconstructable)  
- Performance notes (how to play it, any special set-up steps)  
- 3–8 high-quality photos and, if possible, a short audio or video excerpt  
- Short bibliography of texts and artists that informed the project

### Individual reflection (~5%)

Each student writes a 1–2 page reflection addressing:

- Their role(s) across the build, testing, and performance/installation  
- What they learned technically (e.g., soldering, debugging, mapping)  
- What they learned conceptually (e.g., about instruments, access, noise, labor)  
- One decision they would make differently if they iterated this instrument

These can also serve as portfolio statements for students who want to showcase the work.
