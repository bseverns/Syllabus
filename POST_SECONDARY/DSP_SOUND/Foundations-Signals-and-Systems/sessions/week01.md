# Week 01 — Signals as objects (time domain first)

## Goals
- Recognize amplitude, frequency, and phase as *parameters you can touch*.
- Generate and visualize basic waveforms (sine, square, triangle, saw).
- Connect what you see (plot) to what you hear (sound).

## Session arc (suggested timing)
1. **Warm-up (10 min)** — play 3 short sounds; students describe them without using genre words
2. **Mini-lecture (25 min)** — signals as arrays; time axis; what a sample means; why plots matter
3. **Guided lab (60–80 min)** — build waveforms in Python; plot and listen; compare shapes
4. **Share-out (15 min)** — students show one waveform and describe what changed when they tweaked params
5. **Close (10 min)** — introduce weekly workflow: commit, reflect, push

## Prep (instructor)
- Install deps + verify Jupyter kernel.
- Pre-generate audio samples (optional) using `scripts/generate_samples.py`.
- Have a folder of 5–8 short sound clips ready for the warm-up (voice, noise, percussion, sustained tone).

## Materials
- JupyterLab + Python env
- Headphones
- Audacity (optional)
- Projector for live plotting

## Extensions (if time / advanced track)
- Show phase offsets and interference (two sines).
- Have students build a custom waveform by summing harmonics.
- Talk about clipping as a first taste of distortion.

## Exit ticket (5 min)
One sentence: *What does a “sample” mean today?* One question: *What felt confusing?*
