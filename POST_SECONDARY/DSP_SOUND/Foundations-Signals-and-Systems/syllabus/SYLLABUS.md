# Syllabus — Foundations of Signals and Systems (12 weeks)

**Audience:** advanced high school / undergraduate makers  
**Format:** weekly 1× 2.5–3h meeting (or 2× 75m), plus self-paced lab time  
**Primary mode:** listen → plot → modify → explain → document

## Course Description
Signals are the raw material of modern life: sound, images, sensor streams, radio, data traces. This course teaches students how to represent, analyze, and transform signals using practical tools and repeatable experiments.

We’ll build intuition in the time domain, then grow into frequency thinking, sampling, and filtering. The math is present—but always anchored to something you can test.

## Learning Outcomes
By the end, students can:
- identify and classify common signals and system behaviors
- explain sampling, aliasing, and quantization via experiments
- compute and interpret FFT spectra and spectrograms
- use convolution to predict LTI system outputs
- design and evaluate simple FIR and IIR filters
- document workflows and results in a clean repo

## Prerequisites
- Basic algebra; comfort with graphs
- Some programming experience is helpful; students without it complete the Week 0 bridge in `../FACILITATOR_LAUNCH_GUIDE.md`
- Curiosity and patience for iterative debugging

## Materials
- Laptop capable of running Python + Jupyter
- Headphones (recommended)
- Audacity installed (recommended)
