# Ethics, Accessibility, and Long-Term Thinking — Student Start Here

You do not need prior accessibility, ethics, engineering, or legal vocabulary. You will choose one bounded artifact, make specific claims, test a small improvement, and document what remains unknown.

You never need to disclose a diagnosis, disability, trauma, or personal access need to participate.

## The weekly loop

1. **Inspect:** What does the artifact do, and what does it hide?
2. **Name:** What specific barrier, risk, or maintenance problem appears?
3. **Promise:** What measurable commitment should guide the redesign?
4. **Change:** What is the smallest useful improvement?
5. **Test:** What evidence could support or challenge the claim?
6. **Document:** What worked, who was considered, and what remains unknown?

## Your artifact can be low-tech

You may work with a physical tool, software interface, instrument, website flow, classroom process, instruction sheet, service mockup, or instructor case packet. You do not need to buy equipment or publish your work.

If you cannot modify the artifact, you can improve its prototype, labels, setup instructions, troubleshooting, maintenance plan, access route, or procurement recommendation.

## Consent and testing

- Ask separately before observing, recording, quoting, or publishing.
- A participant can stop or withdraw material.
- Do not ask anyone to reveal private medical or access information.
- One person's feedback does not represent a whole community.
- Use redacted, fabricated, or instructor-supplied data when personal data is unnecessary.
- A protocol or simulation may be more responsible than direct user testing.

## Useful sentence starters

- “For the task ___ in the context ___, we observed ___.”
- “Our current evidence supports ___ but does not show ___.”
- “The barrier appears when ___.”
- “We changed ___ because ___.”
- “A person can opt out by ___.”
- “The system fails safely / unsafely when ___.”
- “Our known unknown is ___.”

## What you will submit

Your Responsibility Pack includes promises, harm/barrier and consent/data documents, accessibility and repairability work, a tested improvement, maintenance/lifecycle and licensing notes, usable documentation, contribution evidence, test records, and a postmortem. Keep private records private; publication is optional and requires permission.
