# Student handbook — DSP / Digital Making Series

You’re here to learn how to build digital things that make sound, sense, and meaning.
You’ll work with waveforms and microcontrollers, code and craft, measurement and play.

## What you’ll do (in plain language)
- record and visualize sound
- build small electronics that react to the world
- learn the core tricks of DSP (filters, envelopes, modulation)
- design an interface (knobs, buttons, LEDs) that feels good to use
- build systems that don’t collapse when stressed
- make a creative work with your system
- ship a final project with documentation

## How to succeed here
1) Show up, build something small, and keep it working.
2) Measure and listen. Trust your ears, then back them with evidence.
3) Write down what you learned while it’s still warm.
4) Keep your systems kind: safe defaults, clear modes, recovery paths.
5) Make it usable by someone who is not you.

## What you’ll be graded on
- iteration + evidence
- stability + correctness
- documentation quality
- user experience + accessibility
See `docs/shared/ASSESSMENT_RUBRIC.md`.

## What to bring
- laptop (recommended), headphones
- notebook (paper is allowed to be magical)
- curiosity and patience

## Office hours / help
Ask early. Struggle is normal; silence is optional.
