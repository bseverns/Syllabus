# Readings and Media Clusters

Keep the load teachable. Most weeks should combine:

- one anchor text
- one shorter example, video, or case
- one discussion prompt tied to current studio work

## Week 1 - What counts as civic media?

- Sasha Costanza-Chock, selections from **Design Justice**
- Eric Gordon and Paul Mihailidis, introduction to **Civic Media: Technology, Design, Practice**
- Short local scan of public notices, campus signage, city service interfaces, or neighborhood communication artifacts

Why this cluster belongs:
Students need a shared definition that connects media forms to power, institutions, and publics from the start.

## Week 2 - Tactical media and small interventions

- Rita Raley, selections from **Tactical Media**
- Excerpts or clips from **The Yes Men**
- Short set of activist posters, intervention zines, or civic graphics from local archives

Why this cluster belongs:
Week 2 needs permission to act small, fast, and pointed without mistaking spectacle for strategy.

## Week 3 - Public narratives, framing, and misdirection

- George Lakoff, short selections on framing
- bell hooks, selections on representation or speaking back
- Michael Warner, publics/counterpublics
- A current institutional website, campaign mailer, or service form students can dissect

Why this cluster belongs:
Teams need to see how public narratives are already designed before they try to redesign one.

## Week 4 - Systems, stakeholders, and leverage

- Donella Meadows, **Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System**
- Dan Hill, short writing on civic design / dark matter
- One case study of a public service redesign or community information project

Why this cluster belongs:
This is the week where the class moves from issue talk to system structure.

## Week 5 - Participation, publics, and co-design limits

- Ezio Manzini, short selections on social innovation and small-scale change
- Lucy Suchman, short selections on situated action or plans versus practice
- Example of a community toolkit, mutual aid guide, or public onboarding packet

Why this cluster belongs:
Students need to understand participation as situated coordination, not magic inclusion language.

## Week 6 - Working with partners without overpromising

- Design Justice Network principles
- Jo Freeman, The Tyranny of Structurelessness
- Short practitioner writing on community-engaged art or public humanities collaboration
- Sample memorandum, onboarding doc, or public-facing project brief

Why this cluster belongs:
Partner week should give students language for boundaries, reciprocity, and clear scope.

## Week 7 - Consent, harm, and unintended consequences

- Ruha Benjamin, short selections from **Race After Technology**
- Data for Black Lives or Electronic Frontier Foundation materials on data minimization and privacy
- One example of a well-meant public project that backfired

Why this cluster belongs:
Students need an explicit checkpoint where ethics change the build, not just the reflection.

## Week 8 - Public legibility and usability

- Steve Krug, short selections from **Don't Make Me Think** or comparable plain-language usability reading
- Public information design examples from transit, libraries, clinics, or mutual aid networks
- One short accessibility checklist or plain-language guide

Why this cluster belongs:
Prototype testing needs straightforward language about whether strangers can read the work.

## Week 9 - Documentation as civic infrastructure

- Mierle Laderman Ukeles, “Manifesto for Maintenance Art”
- Excerpts from project documentation guides, repair manuals, or open-source onboarding docs
- Anne Balsamo or similar short writing on design culture and prototyping
- One strong community toolkit or implementation guide

Why this cluster belongs:
Students should see documentation as part of public utility, not academic admin.

## Week 10 - Narrative proof, media capture, and accountability

- Selections from documentary ethics, visual consent, or journalism verification guidelines
- Example postmortem from a campaign, civic tech project, or public art intervention
- Short reading on captioning, alt text, or accessible documentation

Why this cluster belongs:
As final production begins, students need to know how to document without turning people into props.

## Week 11 - Public action and observation

- No heavy reading; keep it light
- Quick observation rubric or field note guide
- Optional short check-in on public feedback methods

Why this cluster belongs:
This is deployment week. Protect time and keep the reading load minimal.

## Week 12 - Postmortem, replication, and what happens next

- adrienne maree brown, short selections on emergent strategy or small-scale change
- One concise project postmortem and one maintenance / handoff document
- Student-selected references that became useful during the semester

Why this cluster belongs:
The course should end with reflection tied to maintenance, transfer, and accountability.

## Local notes

- Replace at least one reading cluster with a local case study from your city, campus, or partner ecosystem.
- If the cohort is reading-heavy already, cut one secondary text and keep the case study.
