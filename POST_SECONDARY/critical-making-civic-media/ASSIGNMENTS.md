# Assignments

All assignments are judged by what students can **show**, **test**, and **explain**. Discussion matters, but the course grade lives in artifacts.

## Submission rhythm

- Each team maintains a shared folder with dated subfolders and a living process log.
- Each major assignment includes:
  - the artifact itself
  - a short README or cover note
  - evidence of feedback, testing, or revision
- Students submit work in class when possible so critique happens against the actual artifact, not a promise.

## Assignment set

### 1. Process Log + Studio Accountability
- **Weight:** 10%
- **What it is:** Ongoing record of decisions, tests, dead ends, partner contact, role changes, and documentation captures.
- **Deliverables:**
  - weekly log entries
  - dated photos / screenshots / scans
  - role notes showing who owned what
- **Why it matters:** Makes invisible labor, revision, and accountability visible.

### 2. Civic Signal Scan
- **Weight:** 10%
- **What it is:** A focused dossier on one local civic issue, public friction point, or narrative distortion.
- **Deliverables:**
  - issue statement
  - 5-8 field observations or captures
  - audience definition
  - initial claim about where media could intervene
- **Submission logic:** Individual or pair work; becomes the seed for team formation.

### 3. Tactical Media Micro-Intervention
- **Weight:** 15%
- **What it is:** A fast, small public-facing prototype made in Week 2 or 3.
- **Deliverables:**
  - one working artifact
  - one-sentence public claim
  - 250-400 word reflection on what it clarified or failed to clarify
- **Accepted forms:**
  - poster or flyer
  - short video or slide loop
  - printed card set or zine spread
  - no-code landing page or form
  - simple kiosk or sensor demo

### 4. Systems + Stakeholder Packet
- **Weight:** 15%
- **What it is:** The team research packet that turns an issue into a teachable system.
- **Deliverables:**
  - system map
  - stakeholder map
  - leverage point notes
  - constraints and dependency list
- **Submission logic:** Team submission with named contributors.

### 5. Partner Alignment + Intervention Brief
- **Weight:** 15%
- **What it is:** The handoff-quality brief that confirms the project is actually in bounds.
- **Deliverables:**
  - intervention brief
  - partner meeting notes or public-facing constituency check
  - revised scope statement
  - success criteria and failure criteria
- **Checkpoint requirement:** Must happen before major production.

### 6. Ethics / Consent / Risk Review + Prototype Test
- **Weight:** 10%
- **What it is:** A practical review of harm, permissions, consent, framing, and unintended consequences attached to a tested prototype.
- **Deliverables:**
  - ethics review memo
  - prototype v1
  - 3-5 public legibility test responses
  - revision notes

### 7. Final Civic Media Action + Documentation Bundle
- **Weight:** 25%
- **What it is:** The final public-facing intervention and the archive that proves what happened.
- **Deliverables:**
  - final artifact or action
  - setup documentation
  - run-of-show
  - media captures
  - partner-facing summary or public framing statement
  - postmortem

## Grading proportions at a glance

- Process Log + Studio Accountability - 10%
- Civic Signal Scan - 10%
- Tactical Media Micro-Intervention - 15%
- Systems + Stakeholder Packet - 15%
- Partner Alignment + Intervention Brief - 15%
- Ethics / Consent / Risk Review + Prototype Test - 10%
- Final Civic Media Action + Documentation Bundle - 25%

## General submission rules

- A late artifact is better than a polished promise.
- When plans change, students must log the change and why.
- Teams can work across technical levels, but every final project must be legible to a public outside the team.
- If a team scales down scope responsibly, that is usually a strength, not a penalty.
