# Assessment Rubric

Use this rubric in critique, checkpoints, and final evaluation. Score each category on a **1-4 scale**.

- **4 - Strong:** clear, specific, tested, and documented
- **3 - Solid:** mostly clear and functional, with minor gaps
- **2 - Partial:** promising but underdeveloped, vague, or weakly evidenced
- **1 - Thin:** unclear, unsupported, or missing in action

## 1. Systems thinking

- **4:** names key actors, bottlenecks, dependencies, and leverage points; design decisions clearly respond to the mapped system
- **3:** identifies the main system structure but leaves some relationships or constraints underexplained
- **2:** recognizes the issue but treats it mostly as a theme rather than a structured system
- **1:** little evidence of systems analysis

## 2. Civic relevance

- **4:** addresses a real constituency, public friction, or institutional condition with a clearly bounded intervention
- **3:** project is relevant but constituency or site is still somewhat broad
- **2:** project gestures at a civic issue without a convincing local fit
- **1:** public relevance is generic, decorative, or assumed

## 3. Documentation / reproducibility

- **4:** another instructor, student, or partner could realistically rerun the work from the documentation bundle
- **3:** documentation is complete enough to understand the work, with a few operational gaps
- **2:** documentation captures outcomes but not enough process or setup detail to repeat it
- **1:** documentation is sparse, late, or mostly promotional

## 4. Ethical clarity

- **4:** consent, permissions, risk, representation, and unintended consequences are named and handled with concrete decisions
- **3:** major ethics questions are acknowledged and addressed, though some edge cases remain thin
- **2:** ethics appear as a paragraph rather than a design constraint
- **1:** project avoids or ignores foreseeable harms

## 5. Public legibility

- **4:** intended audience can grasp the claim, invitation, or function without insider explanation
- **3:** message is understandable with minor clarification
- **2:** project requires heavy narration from the makers to land
- **1:** artifact is confusing, overpacked, or inaccessible to the stated public

## 6. Collaboration / role accountability

- **4:** team roles are explicit, labor is traceable, and handoffs are documented; partners know who to contact
- **3:** collaboration is functional with some role clarity, though accountability is uneven
- **2:** labor distribution or ownership is hazy; the work depends on informal memory
- **1:** team process is opaque or unstable

## 7. Final prototype or action

- **4:** final intervention is scoped appropriately, executed competently, and supported by evidence from testing or public use
- **3:** final action works and is grounded, though some execution or logistics remain rough
- **2:** prototype exists but feels under-tested, over-claimed, or not ready for the stated context
- **1:** final artifact is mostly aspirational or incomplete

## Critique prompts tied to the rubric

- Where does the artifact show that the team understands the system, not just the topic?
- What proof do we have that the intended public can read or use this?
- What did the team do to reduce harm, confusion, or false promise?
- Could another group rerun this from the documentation?
- Is the project smaller and sharper than the original fantasy?
