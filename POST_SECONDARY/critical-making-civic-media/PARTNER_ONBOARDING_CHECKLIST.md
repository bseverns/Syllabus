# Partner Onboarding Checklist

Use this before the class treats any person or organization as a course partner.

## Basic alignment

- What issue, service gap, or public communication problem are we actually talking about?
- Why does this matter now?
- Who is most affected?
- What would be useful by the end of one semester?
- What would be unrealistic for a class to promise?

## Communication expectations

- Primary contact:
- Preferred communication method:
- Reasonable response time:
- Best times for meetings or site visits:
- Who can approve messaging, documentation, and public display decisions?

## Community care boundaries

- Are there groups, stories, or locations that require extra care?
- Are there topics the class should avoid naming publicly?
- Are there language, translation, or accessibility needs we should account for?
- Are there privacy, consent, or reputational risks if documentation is shared?

## Scope / timeline fit

- Can the project be prototyped in 12 weeks?
- What decision or event date matters most?
- What version would count as a useful pilot?
- If the full idea is too large, what is the smallest valuable version?

## Materials / permissions / documentation approvals

- Can students photograph or record in the relevant space?
- Are logos, names, or wayfinding systems allowed to appear in prototypes?
- What site permissions are needed for a pop-up, installation, or tabling action?
- Who approves public posting, screenshots, or video clips?
- What needs to stay internal?

## What the course can responsibly promise

The course can usually promise:

- a scoped prototype
- a pilot intervention
- documentation of process and outcomes
- a clear handoff of files and notes

The course should not promise by default:

- permanent maintenance
- ongoing staffing or communications labor
- legal, medical, housing, or policy expertise
- institutional adoption after the semester
- public release of sensitive information

## Good partner fit signs

- the partner can name a specific audience and friction point
- the partner is open to a pilot rather than a full rollout
- the partner can give timely feedback
- the partner understands that student learning is part of the project structure

## Warning signs

- the ask is too broad or too urgent for a teaching timeline
- the partner wants unpaid production labor with no room for iteration
- the work depends on permissions that may never arrive
- the project risks extractive storytelling or reputational harm
