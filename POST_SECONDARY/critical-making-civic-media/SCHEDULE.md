# Schedule

Default pacing assumes **12 weeks** with **two 110-115 minute sessions per week**. If you teach in a single weekly block, merge the paired session plans and preserve the phase logic.

## Phase logic

- **Weeks 1-3: Diagnosis** - learn the civic terrain, read the public story, and produce an early intervention fast.
- **Weeks 4-6: Framing and alignment** - map systems, define audience, and confirm scope with partners or publics.
- **Weeks 7-9: Prototype and accountability** - pressure-test ethics, consent, logistics, and public legibility.
- **Weeks 10-12: Action and replication** - stage the work, document it rigorously, and write the postmortem.

## Week-by-week arc

| Week | Focus | Core artifact | Why this week exists |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Civic media as a systems practice; local issue scan | Process log launch + first field notes | Starts from observable conditions and makes the course concrete immediately. |
| 2 | Tactical media fundamentals; narrative pressure points | Micro-intervention sprint | Creates an early win before students disappear into research fog. |
| 3 | Issue selection, claim testing, and audience definition | Civic signal scan dossier | Forces teams to name a precise public problem and constituency. |
| 4 | Systems thinking and leverage points | System map + stakeholder map | Moves the course from opinion to structure. |
| 5 | Medium choice, replication logic, and project planning | Intervention brief draft | Links concept to a medium, site, timeline, and setup reality. |
| 6 | Partner-facing checkpoint and scope revision | Partner packet + revised brief | Prevents fantasy projects and adds accountability before production. |
| 7 | Ethics, consent, permissions, and unintended consequences | Ethics / risk review | Makes harm reduction and reputational awareness explicit before public deployment. |
| 8 | Prototype sprint and first public legibility test | Prototype v1 + test notes | Checks whether strangers can understand the work without explanation. |
| 9 | Production planning, roles, and run-of-show | Run-of-show + materials checklist | Converts promising prototypes into something teachable and runnable. |
| 10 | Production and messaging refinement | Prototype v2 + documentation capture plan | Keeps framing, setup, and documentation aligned before final action. |
| 11 | Public action window | Final civic media action | Gives students a bounded but real moment of public contact. |
| 12 | Postmortem, replication, archive, and critique | Documentation bundle + postmortem | Ends with evidence, reflection, and reuse rather than only presentation. |

## Session structure shorthand

Most sessions follow a repeatable rhythm:

1. Quick check-in and artifact review
2. Short framing lecture or case study
3. Studio work or fieldwork planning
4. Structured critique tied to evidence
5. Documentation and next-step parking

## Notes for compression or expansion

- To run this in **10 weeks**, merge Weeks 4-5 and Weeks 8-9, but keep the ethics checkpoint and partner checkpoint.
- To run this in **14 weeks**, add:
  - one deeper reading discussion week after Week 3
  - one extra field-testing week between Weeks 9 and 10
- If partners are hard to secure, substitute a campus-facing public and require stronger observation and interview evidence.
