## At-a-glance outcomes

- Students analyze tactical media examples in terms of claim, audience, and consequence.
- Students identify what makes an intervention sharp rather than merely expressive.

## Before-class prep

- Bring 4-6 examples across formats: posters, stunts, websites, kiosks, short videos.
- Prepare one example that failed or backfired.

## In-class sequence

1. Case study carousel.
2. Small-group analysis using claim / audience / risk / evidence prompts.
3. Whole-class comparison of strong versus muddy interventions.
4. Teams draft one-sentence public claims for their own issue area.
5. Quick critique.

## Discussion prompts

- What exactly is this artifact trying to make happen?
- What part of the public did it understand well, and what part did it misread?

## Required artifacts

- Case study notes
- One-sentence public claim draft

## Instructor notes

- Use critique language tied to consequence, not taste.
- Keep reminding students that a small working intervention beats a giant platform fantasy.

## Contingency / low-tech fallback

- Print screenshots and run the case study analysis as a table-based gallery walk.
