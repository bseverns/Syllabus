## At-a-glance outcomes

- Students can define civic media as a systems practice, not just a content genre.
- Students begin a process log and make first observations of local public communication.

## Before-class prep

- Bring 3-5 examples of local civic media: signage, wayfinding, forms, websites, alerts, flyers.
- Print or share the process log template.
- Identify a short observation route in or around the building.

## In-class sequence

1. Course framing: what this class is and is not.
2. Quick artifact read of local notices and interfaces.
3. Mini-lecture on narratives, infrastructures, and public friction.
4. Observation walk in pairs.
5. Debrief and launch process logs.

## Discussion prompts

- Where do institutions speak clearly, and where do they hide behind procedure?
- What public message in this building assumes too much prior knowledge?

## Required artifacts

- First process log entry
- 3 documented observations per student

## Instructor notes

- Keep the intro short; the walk is the real beginning of the course.
- Push students to capture exact language, not vague impressions.

## Contingency / low-tech fallback

- If you cannot leave the room, use campus websites, forms, maps, and printed notices as the observation field.
