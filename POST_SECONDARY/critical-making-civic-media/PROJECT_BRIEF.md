# Final Project Brief

## Final assignment

Design and stage a **modest public-facing civic media action** that responds to a specific local issue, constituency, or institutional friction point.

The final project is not a brand campaign, not an awareness poster floating in space, and not a giant speculative platform pitch. It should be a bounded intervention that:

- makes a clear public claim or invitation
- meets an identifiable audience where they already are
- can be staged responsibly within the semester
- leaves behind documentation that another person could learn from or reuse

## Acceptable forms

- poster, flyer, or zine system
- public information kit or onboarding bundle
- no-code or light-code site, form flow, or kiosk
- portable installation, tabletop intervention, or listening station
- SMS, email, or screen-based information intervention
- sensor or microcontroller-based public interface, if the team can maintain it

## Required components

### 1. The action itself

The team must stage a real or realistically simulated public encounter. Minimum viable versions include:

- a pop-up table
- a hallway, library, lobby, or commons intervention
- a partner-facing presentation and pilot
- a classroom-to-public beta with external users

### 2. Public framing

Include:

- project title
- one-sentence public claim
- intended audience
- what you want people to understand, do, or question

### 3. Setup logic

Document:

- site or delivery context
- materials and technical setup
- permissions or approvals needed
- staffing / team roles
- accessibility considerations

### 4. Documentation bundle

Required:

- final artifact files
- installation or setup photos
- run-of-show
- brief setup diagram or flow
- process log excerpt
- audience or user feedback notes
- permissions / consent notes where relevant
- postmortem

### 5. Postmortem

Address:

- what worked
- what confused people
- what changed from prototype to final
- what you would scale, cut, or hand off
- what another instructor, partner, or student should know before repeating it

## Expectations for scope

- Smaller and sharper beats larger and vague.
- It is acceptable to build a temporary pilot if the documentation is strong.
- If a project touches sensitive populations, regulated information, or institutional conflict, the ethical review matters as much as the formal outcome.

## Evidence expectations

Teams should leave the final critique with proof, not only intention:

- photos
- screenshots
- user comments
- observation notes
- before / after comparisons
- evidence of a revision made because of testing

## Evaluation emphasis

The strongest final projects are not necessarily the most technical. They are the ones that are:

- civically specific
- publicly legible
- ethically clear
- appropriately scoped
- documented well enough to teach from
