# Assessment Framework — Scratch Game Studio (Grades 3–5, 12 weeks)

Assessment in this studio is a **flashlight**, not a hammer.  
We use it to find what’s working, to name what’s next, and to help students finish things they can be proud to share.

## What we assess
**A. Game Artifact (the thing they built)**
- Does the game run?
- Does it have *levels* (at least 2 gameplay rooms)?
- Does it include at least 1 *cutscene* (story moment between levels)?
- Does it include at least 1 core mechanic (score/collectibles, hazards/lives, win condition, etc.)?

**B. Design Thinking (the choices they made)**
- Can they explain what the player is supposed to do?
- Can they name one thing they changed after playtesting?
- Can they identify one bug and describe how they fixed it?

**C. Studio Habits (how they worked)**
- Uses the save ritual
- Follows studio agreements (safe stories, kind playtesting)
- Collaborates: shares space/tools, helps peers appropriately, accepts feedback

## How we assess (simple, repeatable)
### Formative (weekly, 2–4 minutes per student)
- **Checkpoint sweep**: teacher walks the room and checks 2–3 “look-fors”
- **Bug card**: student writes (or dictates) one bug they found
- **Two Stars + a Wish**: peer playtest feedback once per month (or more)

### Summative (end of course)
- **Final project rubric** aligned to: Levels + Cutscene + Stability + Player Clarity + Craft
- **Mini reflection**: “What are you proud of? What would you add with one more week?”

## Evidence to collect (fast)
Choose the lightest version that fits your setting:
- **Option 1 (best):** a shared link to each student’s Scratch project
- **Option 2:** a weekly screenshot + file name (stored in one folder)
- **Option 3:** a simple roster checklist with yes/no checkmarks

## Student-friendly scale (4 levels)
We avoid “points” language with young makers.  
We use a **4-level ladder**:
1) **Seed** — I started it  
2) **Sprout** — it mostly works  
3) **Bloom** — it works and is clear  
4) **Shine** — it works, is clear, and has polish or originality

These labels appear in the rubrics.

## Accommodations for high-energy groups (built into assessment)
Assessment doubles as a focus tool:
- **Short sprints**: “Show me only one thing right now.”
- **Choice**: students can demonstrate instead of explaining verbally.
- **Visible targets**: “By the end, your game should have *one* of these.”
- **Micro-celebrations**: stamp/sticker/checkmark when the checkpoint is hit.

## Culture and content notes
We welcome big imagination and help students translate it into shareable games:
- No hate symbols, targeted bullying, or graphic violence.
- If an intense idea appears, we guide students toward safer mechanics:
  “defeat” becomes reset/teleport/capture/lose-a-life; “weapon” becomes tool/magic/power-up.

## Feedback-only approach (recommended)
We do not grade this studio. We give **feedback that helps students finish**.

### What feedback sounds like
- **Glow (what’s working):** “Your controls feel predictable.”
- **Grow (one next step):** “Add a win moment so players know they finished.”
- **Goal (student choice):** “Do you want your next step to be *story* or *gameplay*?”

### Teacher routine (fast)
1) Ask: “Show me one thing that works.”
2) Name it: “That’s your *core loop*.”
3) Offer one next step: “Next, make your win trigger a cutscene.”
4) End with a micro-celebration: checkmark/sticker/high-five.
