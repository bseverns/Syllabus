## Why
A huge amount of technical and civic life depends on labor that is rarely named, rarely celebrated, and often only noticed when it fails. This course would turn maintenance into a formal studio subject: not the background condition of “real work,” but the work itself.

## Core question
What changes when we design **for care, handoff, and continuity** rather than only for novelty, launch, and spectacle?

## Possible project directions
Students might produce:
- maintenance maps of a campus lab, public room, or community site
- repair ethnographies of a device or workflow
- onboarding packets for neglected equipment or spaces
- signage or public-facing media that clarifies hidden infrastructure
- handoff kits for student workers or future maintainers
- modest service redesigns that reduce recurring friction
- salvage / reuse systems for a school or small institution

## Suggested arc
### Phase 1 - Seeing infrastructure
- breakdown logs
- observing maintenance routines
- identifying visible and invisible stewards
- mapping dependencies and choke points

### Phase 2 - Reading repair and care
- case studies in maintenance art, infrastructure, and repair culture
- documentation as operational memory
- who gets credit, who gets blamed, who gets called only after failure

### Phase 3 - Designing for continuity
- prototypes for signage, checklists, training kits, handoff docs, or public interventions
- test what actually reduces confusion or labor
- distinguish symbolic acknowledgement from real support

### Phase 4 - Public legibility and handoff
- show the work to actual users / stewards
- document setup, boundaries, failure points, and next steps
- produce archive-ready final bundles

## Assessment mix
- observation and maintenance log
- systems / dependency map
- repair or infrastructure case study
- prototype / toolkit / intervention
- final documentation bundle and postmortem

## Materials / access notes
Low-material version:
- notebooks
- cameras / phones
- shared docs
- interview and observation protocols
- printers for signage or packets

Higher-material version:
- lab equipment, storage systems, loaner devices, repair benches, and site-specific materials
- optional fabrication for holders, labels, or durable interfaces

## Artifacts
- maintenance log or ethnography
- system / dependency / steward map
- prototype documentation pack
- public-facing infrastructure clarification piece
- final handoff bundle

## Teacher notes
This course should not become a vague “care” seminar. Keep it anchored in actual rooms, tools, schedules, failure modes, and operational burdens. The strongest version will live where poetry touches inventory.
