## Snapshot

**Intended level:** PRIMARY

**Learning outcomes:**
- Understand that motion can be designed through parts, pivots, tension, and timing.
- Build and test simple mechanisms such as cams, levers, wheels, sliders, and linkages.
- Use movement as part of a toy, sculpture, or story-driven object.
- Improve a design by changing one variable at a time and observing what happens.

## Why
This course would give the repo a younger mechanical literacy track. It sits nicely beneath later work in engineering challenges, printability, robotics, and physical interaction, while staying highly tactile, playful, and low-tech.

## Core idea
Students learn that movement is not magic and not just “something the motor does.” Motion can be shaped through structure.

The course should revolve around a small set of physical ideas:
- push / pull
- spin / swing
- lift / drop
- repeat / cycle
- connect one movement to another

## Suggested arc
### Phase 1 - Motion is a system
- observe toys, hinges, wheels, doors, fans, flaps
- identify what moves and what stays still
- quick cardboard experiments with pivots and sliders

### Phase 2 - Mechanisms as vocabulary
- cams
- levers
- wheels and axles
- pulleys
- simple linkages
- compare different ways of making the same thing move

### Phase 3 - Motion with purpose
- build a moving creature, toy, or storytelling scene
- introduce timing, rhythm, and repeated action
- optional integration with simple motors or hand cranks

### Phase 4 - Playable machine studio
- students design a final object that moves for a reason
- demo, test, revise
- explain the mechanism, not just the theme

## Assessment mix
- observation drawings and motion notes
- mechanism mini-builds
- in-class demos
- final playable or viewable moving object
- brief explanation of how motion travels through the build

## Materials / access notes
Strong low-cost kit:
- cardboard
- scissors / craft knives (teacher-managed as needed)
- brass fasteners / brads
- straws, skewers, dowels
- tape, glue
- rubber bands, string
- bottle caps, recycled parts
- LEGO if available

Optional enrichment:
- small motors
- battery packs
- 3D printed cams / gears
- simple hand tools

## Artifacts
- motion sketchbook
- 3-5 mechanism tests
- one mid-course moving prototype
- one final movement-based object
- labeled explanation sheet or rebuild guide

## Teacher notes
This should feel closer to a motion playground than a dry engineering primer. Keep examples visible, movable, and strange. Let students handle mechanisms repeatedly before naming them too heavily.
