## Snapshot
**Working title:** Energy, Electronics & Circuits  
**Intended level:** PRIMARY  
**Estimated hours:** 8-12 weeks, adaptable for 60-90 minute sessions  
**Suggested future path:** `PRIMARY/Energy-Electronics-and-Circuits`

**Learning outcomes:**
- Understand simple circuits as energy pathways rather than magic.
- Use switches, polarity, and component placement to control light, sound, and motion.
- Build playful inventions that combine structure, circuitry, and iterative debugging.
- Explain what changed when a build stopped working and how it was repaired.

## Why
This course would give the repo a strong early physical-systems on-ramp. It sits beneath later work in robotics, control, sound tools, and instrument-making, helping younger students learn that electricity is something they can route, test, and shape rather than merely consume.

## Core idea
Students build systems that do one or more of three things:

1. light up  
2. make sound  
3. move  

Those three outcomes keep the course concrete and highly demo-able while quietly teaching the logic behind circuits, power, switching, and simple control.

## Suggested arc
### Phase 1 - Energy is visible
- batteries, polarity, LED basics
- paper circuits and taped pathways
- what counts as a complete path

### Phase 2 - Control and choice
- switches, buttons, tilt or contact triggers
- simple if/then thinking without needing heavy code
- compare “always on” to “controlled on”

### Phase 3 - Sound and motion
- buzzers, motors, vibration, fan blades, spinners
- cardboard structures that give outputs a body
- debugging noisy / weak / intermittent builds

### Phase 4 - Tiny invention studio
- students combine light, sound, and motion into one small interactive object
- build, test, revise, and present
- end with a demonstration and explanation, not just a finished object

## Assessment mix
- process sketches and observation notes
- build checks and debug logs
- mini demos / explain-backs
- final invention presentation

## Materials / access notes
Best case:
- coin cells or AA packs
- LEDs, buzzers, small motors, copper tape, tape, paper, cardboard
- littleBits or LEGO Spike for selected sessions
- scissors, markers, glue, fasteners

Low-tech fallback:
- focus on paper circuits, hand-powered mechanisms, and teacher-demo stations
- rotate powered components through small groups
- keep code optional rather than required

## Artifacts
- first successful closed-circuit build
- switch-controlled light or sound build
- motorized or vibrating mini-object
- final hybrid invention
- visual build journal / notebook

## Teacher notes
This course should stay playful and highly physical. Resist overloading it with terminology too early. Let the vocabulary arrive in response to actual builds and actual failures.
