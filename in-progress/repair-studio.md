## Snapshot
**Working title:** Repair Studio: Devices, Diagnostics & Reuse  
**Intended level:** SECONDARY  
**Estimated hours:** 8-12 weeks, ideally 90-120 minute sessions  
**Suggested future path:** `SECONDARY/repair-studio`

**Learning outcomes:**
- Diagnose simple faults through structured observation rather than guessing.
- Open, sort, label, and reassemble devices with evidence discipline.
- Build habits around screw management, parts tracking, and documentation.
- Understand repair as stewardship, cost reduction, and resistance to disposability.

## Why
This course would support the actual ecology of labs, makerspaces, classrooms, and community programs where equipment breaks, wears down, or becomes partially usable. It would also teach a powerful systems habit: before making something new, learn how an existing thing fails, survives, and gets brought back into circulation.

## Core idea
Students learn to work like careful stewards rather than reckless tinkerers. The course is less about heroic repair and more about:

- diagnosis
- documentation
- reuse
- responsible salvage
- knowing when **not** to proceed

## Suggested device families
Good starting candidates:
- headphones
- small speakers
- keyboards and mice
- battery-powered toys
- LED lights
- handheld fans
- game controllers
- classroom tech peripherals
- printers or fabrication tools as instructor-led case studies

Avoid or tightly control:
- mains-powered appliances
- swollen lithium batteries
- CRTs, microwaves, and other high-risk electronics
- anything students cannot open safely in a classroom setting

## Suggested arc
### Phase 1 - Looking before touching
- what counts as a symptom
- visual inspection and intake notes
- screw discipline, labeling, tool basics

### Phase 2 - Open / identify / document
- opening devices
- board, shell, wire, connector, fastener vocabulary
- parts photography and exploded-view notes

### Phase 3 - Test and isolate
- continuity, contact issues, worn wires, dirty switches
- swap tests and simple substitution
- “repairable now” vs “needs part” vs “salvage only”

### Phase 4 - Reuse and handoff
- parts library logic
- donor devices
- writing repair notes another student could follow
- building a class maintenance handbook or wall chart

## Assessment mix
- intake and triage sheets
- repair / salvage logs
- tool discipline and bench habits
- short demos of successful repair or honest postmortem
- shared parts inventory / handbook contribution

## Materials / access notes
Best case:
- precision screwdriver sets
- spudgers, tweezers, trays, labels
- multimeters
- donated broken devices
- bins for sorted salvage
- phones or cameras for documentation

Safer school version:
- battery-powered and low-voltage devices only
- instructor controls any soldering or hazardous battery handling
- students work in pairs with explicit checklists

## Artifacts
- individual or pair repair log
- 2-3 documented tear-downs
- one successful repair or well-documented failed attempt
- shared salvage taxonomy
- final “repair and maintenance mini-handbook”

## Teacher notes
The point is not to make every device live again. The point is to cultivate disciplined technical attention. Honest failure with strong documentation should count for real credit.
