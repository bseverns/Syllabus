## Why
The repo has strong material for media foundations, imaging, sound, sensing, and public systems, but less explicit course architecture for video as a live or public infrastructure. This course would fill that gap by treating screens, projections, and real-time image systems as spatial and social media.

## Core question
What changes when video is not simply watched, but encountered in a room, on a wall, in passing, in repetition, or under conditions that never stay still?

## Possible project directions
Students might build:
- projection loops for specific architectural surfaces
- multi-screen or single-screen public works
- responsive video systems tied to time, sound, or input
- gallery or hallway installations
- information screens with experimental image logic
- portable screen objects or micro-cinema interventions
- video systems designed for event conditions rather than black-box playback purity

## Suggested arc
### Phase 1 - Screen as site
- observe existing screens and projections
- compare gallery, hallway, classroom, public lobby, and outdoor conditions
- analyze audience attention, dwell time, and legibility

### Phase 2 - Loop, drift, and real-time behavior
- work with repetition, procedural change, reactive inputs, and timing systems
- compare authored sequence to system behavior
- test what remains readable over time

### Phase 3 - Installation logic
- mounts, playback, power, brightness, speakers, cable paths, signage
- how audience flow changes what a video is
- build setup diagrams and maintenance notes

### Phase 4 - Final public video system
- stage a bounded installation or event-ready piece
- document setup, operating assumptions, and rerun requirements
- write a postmortem focused on site, audience, and technical reality

## Teacher notes
This course should resist two traps:
1. cinema purity that ignores the room  
2. installation vagueness that ignores playback and maintenance  

Keep forcing the work back into actual conditions:
- who sees it?
- for how long?
- from where?
- under what technical constraints?
- what happens when it needs to run again next week?
