# Tiny Machines

> **PILOT syllabus.** Follow the shared [K–2 operating model](README.md). This is the short, tactile wrapper for [Machines That Move](../machines-that-move.md), not a second mechanism curriculum.

## Course snapshot

- **Promise:** learners make movement visible—spin, swing, slide, lift, or flap—then give it a purpose in a tiny creature or scene.
- **Group / staffing:** 8–12 learners; one lead, with an assistant recommended for brad/cutting support.
- **Final share:** each learner or pair demonstrates one movement and says, points to, or shows what makes it happen.
- **Community agreement:** *We test before we judge; we return parts so someone else can build too.*

## Materials for 12 learners

**Core tote:** 24 cardstock or cereal-box panels; cardboard scraps; 24 large paper fasteners/brads; 24 short straws; 24 wooden skewers/dowels used only with capped ends and adult prep; 24 bottle caps or cardboard wheels; masking tape; painter's tape; glue sticks; rubber bands; markers; 12 floor/table trays; and child-safe scissors.

**Helpful extras:** LEGO, pre-cut circles/cams, hole punches, fabric scraps, and one facilitator camera for optional S1 documentation.

**Prep:** pre-punch two holes per learner for Session 1; pre-cut stiff card and cap/shorten dowels; put loose parts in table trays rather than a shared pile; make one working and one half-working demo.

## Four-session sequence

### Session 1 — Hinges and flaps

**Target:** make one part move around a pivot.
**Prompt:** *What could open, wave, or peek?*
**Demo:** one brad hinge and one tape hinge; name only `pivot` after learners have handled both.
**Build:** learners make a flap creature, door, wing, or mouth. During sprint 2, they add one drawing or label that explains the action.
**Evidence / parking:** photo or facilitator note: `My ___ moves when I ___.` Park in a labeled tray.

### Session 2 — Sliders and wheels

**Target:** compare two ways to move something across a surface.
**Prompt:** *Should this character roll or slide? Why?*
**Demo:** straw slider and bottle-cap wheel.
**Build:** test both, then choose one for a small vehicle, animal, or moving sign.
**Evidence / parking:** mark or say which motion was smoother and change one attachment if it sticks.

### Session 3 — Repeat motion

**Target:** use a hand crank, wheel, or off-center cam to make a repeated action.
**Prompt:** *What action could happen again and again?*
**Demo:** a cardboard cam lifting a paper character; adult holds the build during the first test.
**Build:** pairs add a crank or turnable wheel to a known-good base; solo learners may use a facilitator-held base.
**Evidence / parking:** facilitator checks `turns / moves / returns`; learners choose a motion word for the action.

### Session 4 — Moving story share

**Target:** make a tiny machine move for a reason.
**Prompt:** *Who is your machine and what does it do?*
**Demo:** show how to introduce a build in two parts: name it, then make it move.
**Build:** reinforce the working action, add one story detail, and rehearse a 20-second show-and-tell.
**Evidence / share:** gallery circle; each learner demonstrates or asks an adult/partner to demonstrate, then names one change they made.

## Safety, access, and failure backup

Adults manage hole punching, dowel/skewer preparation, and any cutting beyond a learner's safe ability. Use tape hinges instead of brads if fine-motor load, parts safety, or time makes brads a poor choice. A movement that works by hand is a success; motors are not part of the pilot requirement.

If Session 3 mechanisms fail, return to a strong hinge, wheel, or slider and ask learners to make the motion clearer rather than adding more parts.

## Assessment and public description

Use the shared four look-fors, plus: **the learner can show where movement begins and where it appears.**

**Partner description:** A screen-light making workshop where young learners use cardboard and loose parts to discover that motion can be designed, tested, and turned into a playful story.

## Pilot review after delivery

Record: which pre-punched parts saved time, which attachments failed most often, whether 60 minutes supported a completed Session 3 mechanism, and what to remove from the tote next time.
