# Build a Neighborhood

> **ADAPT skeleton.** The source course is [Build Your Block](../../PRIMARY/Build_Your_Block/README.md), documented for grades 3–7. This version keeps its audience-and-place ethic while removing its technical and capstone weight.

## Promise

Children build places people use—home, park, library, bus stop, garden, playground—and explain who each place helps.

## Smallest delivery shape

1. **Notice a place** — draw or role-play a place people share.
2. **Build one place** — cardboard, blocks, paper, and loose parts.
3. **Connect the block** — add roads, paths, signs, trees, or a shared service.
4. **Welcome visitors** — give a guided tour using `who is this for?` and `what can they do here?`.

## Tote

Cardboard, blocks/LEGO, masking tape, paper, markers, fabric scraps, recycled containers, toy figures, and printed neighborhood-picture cards. A shared camera is optional documentation, not instruction.

## Keep it K–2

- Start with lived experience, not abstract civic vocabulary.
- Let learners build independently before asking the group to connect pieces.
- Use spoken tours, drawings, or role-play in place of writing-heavy planning sheets.

## Reuse, do not copy

Borrow the public-facing purpose, mixed-material construction, and showcase rhythm from [Build Your Block](../../PRIMARY/Build_Your_Block/README.md). Do not borrow its seven-week scope, coding platform, or capstone paperwork.
