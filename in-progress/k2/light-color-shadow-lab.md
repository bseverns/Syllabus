# Light, Color and Shadow Lab

> **PILOT skeleton.** [Digital Imaging Lab](../../SECONDARY/digital-imaging-lab/README.md) contributes observation, composition, and image-ethics lineage; it is not a K–2 optics packet.

## Promise

Children test what light, color filters, and objects do to a wall or table, then make a simple shadow scene or optical sculpture.

## Smallest delivery shape

1. **Find a shadow** — use a safe flashlight and objects to compare near/far and big/small shadows.
2. **Mix a window** — layer transparent color films and notice what changes.
3. **Build a scene** — make paper/loose-part silhouettes and choose a background.
4. **Show the light** — stage a one-minute shadow story or gallery walk.

## Tote

Low-heat LED flashlights, translucent color film, white paper/screens, cardboard, tape, scissors, simple silhouettes, and a dimmable corner. No projectors are required.

## Keep it K–2

- Adult controls lighting setup and checks heat/battery condition.
- Treat color as observation rather than a quiz with one required answer.
- Keep photography optional and facilitator-operated; ask permission before documenting learners.

## Reuse, do not copy

Borrow careful observation, framing, and consent habits from [Digital Imaging Lab](../../SECONDARY/digital-imaging-lab/README.md). Do not import camera, editor, or software requirements.
