# Sensing & Gesture (College) – Instructor Notes

## Positioning

This course can sit between:

- Performance / movement studies  
- Physical computing / interaction design  
- Media theory / critical data studies  

Decide how “tech-heavy” vs “movement-heavy” you want it up front and communicate that in the syllabus and first day framing.

## Tech tiers

- **Tier 0 – Low-tech gesture**  
  - Paper scores, analog props, no electronics required.  
  - Useful if students are new to movement or anxious about cameras.

- **Tier 1 – Lightweight sensing**  
  - Microcontrollers with IMU/accelerometers, basic flex / capacitive touch sensors.  
  - Simple webcam-based motion (no heavy ML).  

- **Tier 2 – Heavier sensing (optional)**  
  - Pose estimation libraries, depth cameras, more complex pipelines.  
  - Only if you have the hardware/time, and you’re okay with more debugging overhead.

You can run the course largely at Tier 0–1 and only gesture toward Tier 2 with demos and readings.

## Safety & embodiment

- Warm-ups should respect:
  - Students’ varied bodies, abilities, and comfort with movement.  
  - Need for accessible alternatives (micro-gestures, chair-based scores, non-visual sensing).  
- Be explicit: no touching others without consent, no unsafe stunts, no compulsory physical intensity.  
- Offer multiple ways to participate in embodied exercises (e.g., describing gestures, drawing, or manipulating objects).

## Ethics

Gesture tracking is never neutral.

- Talk early and often about surveillance, labor (e.g., motion capture, warehouse tracking), and ableism.  
- Ask who is likely to be misread by your systems and what you’ll do about that.  
- Encourage projects that **reveal**, **complicate**, or **critique** gesture sensing, not just replicate consumer UX patterns.

You do not need to have all the answers; model how to pause a technical plan to consider access and power.

## Archiving

Decide where student work lives after:

- GitHub org / shared drive / internal site  
- Course blog or internal wiki  
- Institutional repository (if relevant)

Gesture documentation is often video + annotated scores + diagrams; plan for that in final deliverables and make expectations explicit.
