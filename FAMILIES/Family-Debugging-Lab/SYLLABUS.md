# Fix It Together: Family Debugging Lab — Pilot Syllabus

## Workshop contract

- **Audience:** mixed-age family teams with participating caregiver; adaptable from age 6+
- **Duration:** 90 minutes or three 20-minute stations plus opening/closing
- **Result:** two investigated cases and one reusable family debugging routine

## Preflight and materials

Prepare easy, medium, and stretch cases with exactly one deliberate fault each. Verify each baseline, fault, clue path, fix, and reset checklist. Keep known-good parts/programs separate. Provide process cards, evidence sheets, sticky notes, markers, paper puzzles or mechanisms, and optional numbered SPIKE kits/devices.

The shared routine is: **What did we expect? What happened? What do we know? What will we change? What changed afterward?**

## Flow

1. **Failure encounter — 0–10:** Facilitator demonstrates a repeatable failure and models neutral description without saying “broken.”
2. **Roles — 10–15:** Assign driver, navigator, evidence-keeper, and tester. The navigator may ask questions but not seize the system.
3. **Case one — 15–35:** Reproduce the symptom twice, preserve one trusted fact, choose a one-variable test, and record the result.
4. **Compare — 35–43:** Families share useful tests rather than answers. Rotate roles.
5. **Case two — 43–65:** Choose an appropriate difficulty; follow the routine; request one clue only after recording evidence.
6. **Transfer — 65–75:** Write a clue for the next family that supports inquiry without revealing the fix.
7. **Family routine — 75–84:** Apply the five questions to an everyday non-sensitive example; no one must disclose a personal conflict or device problem.
8. **Close/reset — 84–90:** Name what a teammate noticed; facilitators restore every case from checklist.

## Case boundaries

Use only prepared, non-networked, non-personal systems. Never troubleshoot participant accounts, phones, passwords, medical devices, household wiring, or safety-critical equipment. Keep small parts age-appropriate and moving mechanisms contained.

## Access and fallback

Offer visual sequences, read-aloud cards, large pieces, seated roles, no-device cases, and challenge choice. Praise evidence and role-sharing rather than speed. If a planted case accumulates a second fault, retire it and replace it with a paper sequence; do not improvise an unknown technical mystery during delivery.

## Observable assessment

A family can make a failure repeat, distinguish observation from assumption, run one controlled test, record what changed, rotate roles, and leave a non-spoiling clue.

## Post-delivery review

Record case completion/reset failures, accidental extra faults, clue use, age/access fit, takeover behavior, timing, and which case should be revised or retired.
