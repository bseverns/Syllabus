# Family Flight Night — Pilot Syllabus

## Workshop contract

- **Audience:** one older child/teen paired with an adult; recommended ages 10+
- **Duration:** 90–120 minutes
- **Equipment:** one simulator-ready laptop and compatible transmitter per pair
- **Result:** a documented simulator progression and shared coaching vocabulary

There is no live flight, LiPo handling, charging, RF operation, or racing in this workshop.

## Preflight and materials

Verify every radio, USB connection, simulator profile, reset command, input range, and display. Use one consistent control mode and label stations. Prepare control diagrams, drill cards, observation sheets, accessible input options where available, and one unpowered demonstration aircraft.

## Flow

1. **Boundary and system encounter — 0–12:** State simulator-only rule; inspect battery, motors, flight controller, receiver, video transmitter, camera, and goggles without powering aircraft.
2. **Controls — 12–25:** Demonstrate throttle, yaw, pitch, roll, arm/reset, and neutral input. Each pair predicts a movement before testing it.
3. **Drill 1 — 25–42:** Pilot lifts, holds approximate altitude, lands, and resets. Coach describes observed motion but never grabs the radio.
4. **Drill 2 — 42–60:** Move deliberately through a large box or gate with wide turns. Switch roles.
5. **Recovery — 60–75:** Practice pausing, leveling, reducing input, landing/resetting, and naming disorientation without blame.
6. **Chosen challenge — 75–95:** Select hover consistency, wide circuit, or simple gate sequence; record one metric such as clean resets or controlled passes.
7. **System/strategy share — 95–110:** Pairs explain one control relationship, one coaching phrase, and one recovery choice. Shorten proportionally for a 90-minute run.
8. **Reset — 110–120:** Save no personal account data; restore profiles, controls, radios, and stations.

## Safety, access, and fallback

Treat transmitters as shared instruments: wrist strap if supplied, two hands, no throwing or cable pulling. Provide seated operation, reduced sensitivity, alternate observer/system roles, breaks for motion sickness, and non-goggle display. Stop for discomfort or frustration. If a station fails, pair learners at a working display and use the unpowered aircraft/system cards; do not substitute live flight.

## Observable assessment

Each participant can name the four controls, complete a bounded simulator drill or explain it as observer, use a coaching statement without taking control, perform a calm reset, and identify major aircraft subsystems.

## Post-delivery review

Record compatibility failures, calibration changes, accessibility issues, nausea/fatigue, drill completion, role balance, and needed station changes. Do not infer live-flight readiness.
