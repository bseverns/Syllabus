# Robot Orchestra — Pilot Syllabus

## Workshop contract

- **Audience:** family teams of 2–5 with a participating caregiver; recommended ages 7+
- **Duration:** 120 minutes
- **Capacity:** one tested SPIKE kit and compatible device per team
- **Staffing:** one lead plus one roaming technical/reset facilitator
- **Result:** one expressive sensor-to-behavior score and a short ensemble performance

## Preflight and materials

Charge and number hubs/devices; inventory trays; load one known-good starter build and program; disable account requirements where possible; tape safe test zones; prepare role and behavior cards. Each team needs a SPIKE kit, device, sensor, motor or light output, build tray, paper score, marker, and project label.

## Flow

1. **Encounter — 0–10:** Watch two starter behaviors. Families describe what they perceived before seeing code.
2. **Roles and baseline — 10–25:** Rotate builder, programmer, observer, tester, and conductor. Reproduce the starter response and verify reset.
3. **Choose a score — 25–35:** Select one bounded prompt: approach/retreat, distance-to-tempo, call-and-response, or a changed fourth repetition.
4. **Build sprint — 35–65:** Change one input mapping and one output quality. Observers keep notes; conductors do not touch the device.
5. **Test and debug — 65–80:** Run the score three times. Change one variable if the audience cannot perceive the intended response.
6. **Ensemble rehearsal — 80–100:** Pair teams, agree on start/stop signals and safe spacing, then rehearse a 30–60 second piece.
7. **Performance — 100–112:** Share in small groups; public participation or recording is optional.
8. **Explain and reset — 112–120:** Each family names the input, rule, output, revision, and next experiment; restore the kit checklist.

## Facilitator prompts

“What can an audience actually perceive?” “Is it responding or merely moving?” “What changed when you changed distance?” “How can the coach help without taking control?”

## Safety, access, and fallback

Keep robots on tables or marked floor zones; tie back hair and keep fingers clear of moving parts. Stop on heat, odor, damaged parts, or unexpected high-speed movement. Provide visual role cards, seated builds, headphones/quiet viewing, and non-programming roles. If a hub or device fails, use paper-code cards to conduct the known-good starter robot; no family loses the full performance cycle.

## Observable assessment

A family can identify the sensed input and programmed output, demonstrate a repeatable response, describe one test-driven change, rotate roles, and reset its station.

## Post-delivery review

Record actual setup time, connection failures, kit completeness, role balance, sensory needs, perceived behaviors, and the revision required before another run.
