# Build Together: Family Invention Lab — Pilot Syllabus

## Workshop contract

- **Audience:** mixed-age family teams of 2–5; a participating caregiver remains with each child
- **Duration:** 90–120 minutes
- **Group size:** one shared artifact per family team
- **Screen load:** S0 minimum; S1 only when a shared SPIKE kit serves the family's idea
- **Readiness:** PILOT — runnable as written, pending real delivery and revision

## Learning goals

Families will:

1. turn a broad invitation into one shared prototype;
2. rotate consequential roles rather than assigning technical work by age;
3. test what the invention actually communicates or does;
4. explain one evidence-based revision.

## Materials

### Minimum, screen-free tote

- cardboard and clean recycled building material;
- LEGO or DUPLO for quick structure, if available;
- tape, markers, paper, string, craft sticks, and reusable fasteners;
- role cards and one reflection card per family;
- scissors appropriate to the participants and site.

### Optional technology extension

- simple LEDs with protected low-voltage battery holders;
- low-voltage motors with facilitator-tested connections; or
- one inventoried LEGO Education SPIKE kit and compatible shared device per team.

Do not introduce technology unless it makes the family's idea clearer. Do not mix loose LEDs, motors, or batteries into the youngest-participant material area without direct supervision.

## Before families arrive

- Confirm attendance, age mix, caregiver participation, access needs, room layout, and whether work may remain on site.
- Set one clearly bounded work surface per family and a separate tool/material station.
- Pre-cut difficult cardboard shapes and remove staples, sharp edges, and unsuitable reclaimed material.
- Test every optional motor, battery holder, SPIKE kit, and device; provide a screen-free path for every prompt.
- Print role cards: **designer, builder, tester, navigator, recorder, conductor**. Teams use only the roles that fit their size.
- Prepare a quiet reset area and keep pathways and seated work positions accessible.

## Workshop sequence

### 1. Encounter and choose — 10 minutes

Show one rough prototype and name one thing it failed to do. Families select or adapt one invitation:

- make something that welcomes a person;
- make something useful for a shared space;
- communicate without written words;
- build something that changes when two people cooperate;
- invent a wonderfully unnecessary machine that makes another family smile.

Each family sketches one idea and chooses its first roles.

### 2. Build sprint one — 20–25 minutes

Teams make the smallest version that another person can encounter. Facilitators ask who is deciding, building, and observing; they do not become the team's fixer.

### 3. Test and switch roles — 10–15 minutes

A neighboring family tries the invention without an explanation first. The makers record:

- what they expected;
- what the visitors actually did or understood;
- one part to preserve and one part to change.

Every participant changes roles before the next build.

### 4. Build sprint two — 20–30 minutes

Families revise one important behavior or message. Optional light, motor, sensor, or SPIKE coding may be added only when it supports that revision.

### 5. Share — 15–20 minutes

Each family gives a two-minute demonstration: **We tried… / It did… / We changed… / Next we would…** Invite participants to describe decisions made by both children and adults.

### 6. Document and reset — 10–15 minutes

Photograph work only with site-approved consent. Otherwise use the reflection card or a quick drawing. Sort reusable materials, recycle scraps, return tools, and remove batteries from temporary circuits.

## Facilitation and access

- Adults participate; they do not automatically hold the technical or leadership role.
- Offer drawing, pointing, demonstrating, speaking, or writing as equivalent ways to contribute.
- Provide pre-cut parts, large-grip tools, seated work positions, visual role cards, and extra processing time as needed.
- Do not require reading, color discrimination, fine-motor precision, or a personal device to hold a meaningful role.
- Keep demonstrations short and tied to the next physical action.

## Evidence of learning

The artifact does not need to be polished. Evidence is present when a family can show:

- a shared decision visible in the prototype;
- at least one genuine role switch;
- an observed test result;
- a revision or a specific next change grounded in that result.

## Safety and permissions

- Caregivers remain responsible for children and follow site supervision rules.
- Facilitators control cutting tools, hot tools, exposed conductors, and battery access according to participant age and site policy.
- Use only low-voltage, current-limited components; stop using damaged batteries, wires, motors, or kits.
- Obtain explicit consent before photographing, recording, naming, or publicly sharing family work.
- Do not require families to take projects, batteries, devices, or accounts home.
