# Family Sound Lab — Pilot Syllabus

## Workshop contract

- **Audience:** mixed-age family teams with participating caregiver
- **Duration:** 90 minutes
- **Result:** a 30–60 second live cue-score or optional recording
- **Default:** objects and paper, no accounts or participant screens

## Preflight and materials

Confirm room/noise limits and a quiet retreat. Decide whether voices, recording, storage, and playback are allowed; default to no retained files. Prepare safe sound objects, paper, markers, picture cues, simple percussion, optional recorder, speaker, and headphones. Remove sharp, breakable, ingestible, or culturally inappropriate objects.

## Flow

1. **Listening arrival — 0–8:** Notice three sounds without making new ones.
2. **Sound safety — 8–15:** Agree on stop signal, comfortable volume, quiet-zone, and opt-out routes.
3. **Object exploration — 15–28:** Each person finds two contrasting safe sounds; no one must use their voice.
4. **Roles and prompt — 28–38:** Rotate conductor, sound-maker, listener, and optional recorder. Choose Foley scene, rhythm, or sound postcard.
5. **Compose — 38–55:** Draw a 4–8 event cue score using pictures, marks, or words. Include one silence.
6. **Rehearse and listen — 55–68:** Perform once; the listener reports what was perceivable, then the family changes timing, repetition, or volume.
7. **Share — 68–82:** Exchange with another family or use a quiet gallery/listening route. Recording and public performance remain optional.
8. **Reflect and reset — 82–90:** Name the strongest sound relationship, the revision, and one sound to collect next; return objects.

## Facilitator prompts

“What changed when silence got longer?” “Which sound carries the action?” “Can another person follow the score?” “How can the arrangement work at a gentler volume?”

## Safety, access, consent, and fallback

Use a visible stop signal and enforce the agreed volume ceiling. Provide hearing protection, distance, visual conducting, non-performing roles, and a silent picture-score option. Never record or retain voices/images without specific caregiver and participant consent. Do not upload files by default. If playback fails, perform the cue score live.

## Observable assessment

A family creates a readable sequence, rotates roles, responds to a listener with one revision, respects the stop/volume agreement, and explains how timing, silence, repetition, or dynamics changed meaning.

## Post-delivery review

Record noise conflicts, sensory accommodations, object hazards, role balance, recording choices, timing, and the smallest revision for the next workshop.
